# How to Use This File

These prompts are conversation starters, not rigid commands.

Replace bracketed values with the context of the current discovery.

---

# 1. Prepare for a New Discovery

> I am starting discovery with **[business area]**.
>
> The business objective is **[objective / unknown]**.
>
> Known stakeholders are **[roles]**.
>
> What we currently know is **[context]**.
>
> I have **[time/session length]** available.
>
> Using DISCOVER, tell me:
> 1. what I should try to understand first;
> 2. which DISCOVER stages are relevant;
> 3. which POPIT-V lenses should be prioritised;
> 4. which Golden Thread links we need to establish;
> 5. what evidence is missing;
> 6. which artefacts would be useful.
>
> Do not design a technical solution.

---

# 2. Build a Workshop

> Design a **[60/90/120]-minute** discovery workshop for **[business area]**.
>
> Participants: **[roles]**
>
> Current context: **[context]**
>
> Desired outcome: **[outcome]**
>
> Build the workshop according to the DISCOVER workshop standard.
>
> Keep it business-led and solution-neutral.
> Include primary questions, optional probes, evidence to capture and expected outputs.

---

# 3. Turn a Conversation Into a Workshop

> Based on our conversation so far, create the most appropriate workshop.
>
> First summarise what you believe the discovery objective is.
> Then identify the relevant DISCOVER stages, POPIT-V lenses and Golden Thread links.
>
> Flag anything you have inferred rather than learned from evidence.
>
> Then create the workshop agenda and facilitator questions.

---

# 4. Create a Facilitator Pack

> Create a facilitator pack for the workshop we have agreed.
>
> Include:
> - opening explanation;
> - workshop objective;
> - agenda;
> - questions;
> - deeper probes;
> - facilitation notes;
> - evidence to capture;
> - likely derailers;
> - parking-lot topics;
> - closing validation questions;
> - expected outputs.
>
> Keep questions neutral and avoid leading participants toward technology.

---

# 5. Analyse Workshop Notes

> Analyse the supplied workshop notes using DISCOVER.
>
> Produce:
> - executive summary;
> - POPIT-V findings;
> - Golden Thread;
> - decisions;
> - business concepts and terminology;
> - business rules;
> - pain points;
> - evidence;
> - assumptions;
> - contradictions;
> - open questions;
> - candidate opportunities;
> - validation questions.
>
> Do not fill gaps with general knowledge.

---

# 6. Find the Decisions

> From the supplied sources, identify the important business decisions.
>
> For each decision capture:
> - decision;
> - owner;
> - trigger/frequency;
> - information required;
> - business rules;
> - source systems/documents if explicitly known;
> - resulting action;
> - current pain points;
> - evidence;
> - open questions.
>
> If the decision owner or rule is not established, mark it unknown.

---

# 7. Build the Information Model

> Identify the important business concepts contained in the sources.
>
> Produce a concept catalogue containing:
> - concept;
> - business definition;
> - synonyms;
> - related concepts;
> - owner if known;
> - source/evidence;
> - ambiguity or conflicting definitions.
>
> Then suggest relationships that appear to form a business ontology.
>
> Clearly label inferred relationships.

---

# 8. Trace the Golden Thread

> Construct the Golden Thread using only the evidence available.
>
> Strategy
> → Capability
> → Process
> → Decision
> → Information
> → Business Rules
> → Technology/Data
> → Data Product/Analytics
> → Insight/Action
> → Value
>
> Show missing links as **UNKNOWN** rather than inventing them.
>
> Finish with the five most valuable questions to close the gaps.

---

# 9. Challenge a Dashboard Request

> The stakeholder has requested **[dashboard/report]**.
>
> Do not assume the requested output is the correct solution.
>
> Use DISCOVER to identify:
> - intended outcome;
> - users;
> - decisions;
> - actions;
> - information required;
> - business rules;
> - current failure/pain point;
> - success measure;
> - value.
>
> Then tell me what discovery questions I should ask before accepting the requirement.

---

# 10. Explore an AI Opportunity

> A stakeholder has suggested using AI for **[use case]**.
>
> Assess this through DISCOVER.
>
> Identify:
> - business outcome;
> - user task;
> - decision/process affected;
> - information required;
> - available evidence;
> - human oversight;
> - controls/risk;
> - measurable value;
> - simpler alternatives;
> - unknowns.
>
> Do not recommend AI merely because it is possible.

---

# 11. Assess Maturity

> Assess the supplied evidence using the DISCOVER maturity model.
>
> Score People, Organisation, Process, Information, Technology and Value separately:
> Fragmented / Developing / Defined / Managed / Adaptive.
>
> For each dimension provide:
> - assessment;
> - evidence;
> - gaps;
> - confidence;
> - what would indicate the next maturity level.
>
> Do not average the scores.

---

# 12. Build an Outcome-Led Roadmap

> Using only validated or clearly labelled findings, create a Now / Next / Later roadmap.
>
> Each roadmap item must link:
> outcome
> → capability
> → problem/opportunity
> → decision/process
> → information/data dependency
> → proposed intervention
> → value
> → success measure.
>
> Flag dependencies and unvalidated assumptions.
>
> Do not invent delivery dates or effort estimates.

---

# 13. Prepare the Next Interview

> Based on everything currently known, what should I investigate next?
>
> Rank the unanswered questions by discovery value.
>
> Prefer questions that close important gaps in the Golden Thread or test assumptions that could materially change our direction.

---

# 14. Produce an Executive Readout

> Produce a concise executive discovery readout.
>
> Structure it as:
> - why we investigated;
> - what we learned;
> - evidence;
> - biggest business problems/opportunities;
> - why they matter;
> - what remains unknown;
> - recommended next discovery actions.
>
> Keep technology recommendations out unless the evidence supports them.
