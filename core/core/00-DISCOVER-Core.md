# DISCOVER Core

## Purpose

DISCOVER is a structured methodology for understanding a business problem before converging on technology or solution design.

It is intended to help practitioners move from business strategy and capabilities through decisions, information and rules to appropriate data products, analytics, AI and technology outcomes.

The methodology must remain business-led, evidence-based, traceable and value-focused.

---

# DISCOVER Lifecycle

DISCOVER =

1. **Define**
2. **Investigate**
3. **Study**
4. **Capture**
5. **Organise**
6. **Validate**
7. **Evaluate**
8. **Roadmap**

## Progression Rule

DISCOVER is iterative, not a rigid waterfall.

Do not progress on the basis of critical untested assumptions. Establish **sufficient confidence** to move forward and validate continuously.

Learning may return the practitioner to an earlier stage at any point.

Perfect certainty is not required.

## Define

Establish why discovery is taking place.

Understand:
- business context;
- strategic objectives;
- scope;
- desired outcomes;
- stakeholders;
- known constraints;
- current concerns;
- success measures.

Do not begin with a proposed technical solution.

## Investigate

Explore the business domain broadly.

Understand:
- stakeholders;
- capabilities;
- terminology;
- current activities;
- known issues;
- existing artefacts;
- available evidence;
- major systems and information sources.

The objective is breadth before premature convergence.

## Study

Investigate priority areas in depth.

Study:
- business processes;
- decisions;
- information requirements;
- business rules;
- hand-offs;
- pain points;
- controls;
- systems;
- data;
- measures;
- dependencies.

## Capture

Record discovery evidence in reusable structured artefacts.

Capture:
- facts;
- stakeholder statements;
- assumptions;
- unresolved questions;
- decisions;
- business concepts;
- relationships;
- processes;
- rules;
- measures;
- opportunities.

## Organise

Connect the captured knowledge.

Organise findings using:
- POPIT-V;
- the Golden Thread;
- capability models;
- process models;
- decision catalogues;
- information / concept models;
- data product candidates;
- opportunity registers;
- traceability links.

## Validate

Take findings back to the people who understand the business.

Validate:
- terminology;
- process understanding;
- ownership;
- assumptions;
- business rules;
- data interpretation;
- pain points;
- opportunities;
- measures of success.

Do not treat an AI-generated synthesis as validated business truth.

## Evaluate

Assess what the evidence means.

Evaluate:
- root causes;
- business impact;
- risk;
- value;
- feasibility;
- dependencies;
- maturity;
- governance implications;
- candidate interventions.

Remain solution-neutral until the problem and desired outcome are sufficiently understood.

## Roadmap

Translate validated findings into an outcome-led sequence of work.

DISCOVER roadmaps provide **direction for delivery**, not detailed release plans, sprint plans or delivery estimates.

Roadmap items should show:
- business outcome;
- capability affected;
- problem/opportunity;
- decision/process affected;
- proposed intervention;
- information/data dependency where relevant;
- expected value;
- evidence;
- confidence;
- assumptions;
- dependencies;
- validation still required;
- success measure;
- owner;
- horizon where useful.

# POPIT-V

Use POPIT-V as the primary analytical lens.

## People

Understand:
- roles;
- responsibilities;
- skills;
- behaviours;
- incentives;
- capacity;
- knowledge;
- decision rights;
- adoption needs.

Ask:
- Who performs the work?
- Who makes the decision?
- Who owns the outcome?
- Where is specialist knowledge concentrated?
- Where are users working around existing processes or systems?

## Organisation

Understand:
- business capabilities;
- organisational boundaries;
- ownership;
- governance;
- operating model;
- teams;
- external partners;
- strategic alignment.

Ask:
- Which capability produces the outcome?
- Who owns it?
- Where do responsibilities cross organisational boundaries?
- Which governance forums influence the work?

## Process

Understand:
- activities;
- triggers;
- inputs;
- outputs;
- hand-offs;
- controls;
- exceptions;
- delays;
- manual interventions.

Ask:
- What actually happens?
- What triggers the process?
- Where does work wait?
- Where are reconciliations or rework required?
- What exceptions matter?

## Information

Understand:
- business concepts;
- terminology;
- measures;
- data;
- metadata;
- documents;
- information flows;
- quality;
- lineage;
- business rules.

Ask:
- What information is required to make the decision?
- What does each term mean to the business?
- Which definitions differ by team?
- Which data is authoritative?
- What information is missing, duplicated or distrusted?

## Technology

Understand:
- systems;
- platforms;
- applications;
- integrations;
- spreadsheets;
- reporting tools;
- data platforms;
- automation;
- AI;
- technical constraints.

Technology should explain how the business is currently enabled or constrained. It should not dictate discovery.

## Value

Understand:
- desired outcomes;
- KPIs;
- benefits;
- costs;
- risk;
- opportunity cost;
- customer/business impact;
- strategic contribution.

Every recommendation should answer:

> **So what?**

Discovery has no purpose unless it can ultimately be linked to meaningful business value.

# Golden Thread

Use the Golden Thread to connect discovery from business intent through to measurable value.

**Business Strategy**
→ **Business Capability**
→ **Business Process**
→ **Business Decision**
→ **Business Information**
→ **Business Rules**
→ **Technology / Data**
→ **Data Products / Analytics**
→ **Insight / Action**
→ **Business Value**

The Golden Thread is not necessarily a linear delivery process. It is a traceability model.

When analysing evidence, identify which links are known, which are assumed and which are missing.

# Evidence Model

When producing outputs, classify important statements as one of:

- **Evidence** — explicitly supported by a supplied source.
- **Stakeholder Statement** — reported by a stakeholder but not yet independently validated.
- **Assumption** — plausible but unverified.
- **Interpretation** — analytical conclusion drawn from evidence.
- **Recommendation** — proposed action or intervention.
- **Open Question** — information still required.

Never present an assumption or inference as established fact.

When evidence conflicts:
1. show the conflict;
2. identify the sources or stakeholder positions;
3. do not silently reconcile them;
4. propose a validation question.

Use only the artefacts that help answer the discovery objective.

Potential artefacts include:

- Discovery Brief
- Stakeholder Map
- Business Capability Map
- Context Diagram
- Process Map
- Journey / Value Stream Map
- Decision Catalogue
- Business Rules Catalogue
- Information / Concept Catalogue
- KPI Catalogue
- Data Source Catalogue

- Systems Landscape
- Pain Point Register
- Assumption Log
- Open Questions Log
- Risk Register
- Opportunity Register
- Benefits Register
- Data Product Catalogue
- AI Opportunity Register
- Maturity Profile
- Prioritisation Matrix
- Benefits Realisation Plan
- Outcome-led Roadmap

Do not create artefacts merely because they are available.

# DISCOVER Maturity Model

Assess maturity separately across each POPIT-V dimension.

Levels:

1. **Fragmented**
2. **Developing**
3. **Defined**
4. **Managed**
5. **Adaptive**

Prefer a maturity profile showing variation across dimensions rather than collapsing maturity into one overall score.

Any maturity judgement must include:
- supporting evidence;
- gaps;
- confidence;
- what would be required to move to the next level.

# Workshop Design Rules

When asked to design a workshop:

1. Restate the business outcome.
2. Identify the DISCOVER lifecycle stages relevant to the session.
3. Select only the POPIT-V perspectives needed.
4. Identify the part of the Golden Thread being explored.
5. Define tangible workshop outputs.
6. Create an agenda with timings.
7. Provide primary questions and optional probes.
8. Identify evidence to capture.
9. Include validation at the end.
10. Avoid solutioning unless explicitly appropriate to the stage.

Questions should encourage stakeholders to describe the business in their own language.

Prefer:
- "What decision are you trying to make?"
- "What happens next?"
- "What information do you use?"
- "How do you know this has worked?"

Avoid prematurely asking:
- "Which dashboard do you need?"
- "Which AI tool should we build?"
- "Which database should this go into?"

# Post-Workshop Analysis Rules

When analysing workshop notes or transcripts:

## Step 1 — Extract
Extract only what the evidence supports.

## Step 2 — Classify
Classify findings into POPIT-V.

## Step 3 — Connect
Build Golden Thread relationships where evidence permits.

## Step 4 — Separate
Clearly separate:
- facts;
- stakeholder statements;
- assumptions;
- interpretations;
- recommendations;
- open questions.

## Step 5 — Find Gaps
Identify:
- missing owners;
- undefined terms;
- unresolved rules;
- unclear decisions;
- missing measures;
- contradictory evidence;
- unknown sources;
- weak value links.

## Step 6 — Validate
Create focused validation questions.

## Step 7 — Evaluate
Only after validation, identify candidate opportunities.

# Opportunity Evaluation

A candidate opportunity should describe:

- problem or opportunity;
- affected users/stakeholders;
- business capability;
- process/decision affected;
- information needed;
- current constraint;
- expected outcome;
- measurable value;
- dependencies;
- risks;
- evidence;
- confidence.

Do not rank opportunities using fabricated precision.

Where quantitative evidence is absent, use qualitative categories and state that they require validation.

---

# Data Product Thinking

A data product is not simply a dashboard, table or pipeline.

When a candidate data product appears, ask:

- Who is the consumer?
- Which business decision or action does it support?
- Which information does it provide?
- Which business concepts must be consistently defined?
- What quality expectations exist?
- Who owns the source data?
- Who owns the product?
- What freshness is required?
- What governance applies?
- How will adoption be measured?
- How will business value be measured?

---

# AI Discovery

AI should be treated as a possible capability, not a predetermined answer.

Before recommending AI, understand:

- the business outcome;
- the user task;
- the decision or process;
- the information available;
- risk and control requirements;
- human oversight;
- explainability needs;
- governance;
- measurable value;
- whether a simpler intervention would solve the problem.

---

# Required Behaviour for the Notebook

When helping with DISCOVER work:

- treat `docs/methodology/` as authoritative if it is supplied;
- use canonical artefact templates when they are supplied;
- preserve stable traceability IDs across updates;
- ground analysis in supplied sources;
- preserve business terminology used in those sources;
- cite or name the evidence used where possible;
- distinguish evidence from inference;
- identify gaps rather than invent answers;
- challenge technology-first framing;
- trace recommendations back to business outcomes;
- ask whether decisions and information are understood before proposing data products;
- include Value explicitly;
- remain solution-neutral during early discovery;
- propose validation questions when confidence is low;
- do not silently overwrite conflicting stakeholder definitions, rules or relationships;
- do not turn a roadmap into a fabricated delivery schedule.

The objective is not to produce the most polished answer.

The objective is to produce the most useful, traceable and defensible understanding of the business.
