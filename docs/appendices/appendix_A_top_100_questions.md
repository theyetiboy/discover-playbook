# Appendix A — Top 100 DISCOVER Questions

> Use these questions as a **question bank**, not a script. Select those that help close the most important gaps in the current discovery.

Good discovery questions are neutral, evidence-seeking and connected to an outcome. Follow the stakeholder's language, ask for concrete examples and use probes such as:

- Can you show me?
- Can you give me a recent example?
- Is that always true?
- What happens when it goes wrong?
- Who decides that?
- What information tells them?
- Where is that rule written?
- How do you know this worked?

The 100 questions below are organised around DISCOVER, POPIT-V and the Golden Thread.

---

## 1. Outcome, Strategy and Scope — Questions 1–10

1. What business outcome are we trying to improve?
2. Why does this matter now?
3. Which strategic objective does this support?
4. Who receives the value if this improves?
5. What happens if nothing changes?
6. How is success measured today?
7. What would leadership notice if the outcome improved?
8. What is explicitly in scope?
9. What is explicitly out of scope?
10. Which assumptions are we making before discovery has begun?

## 2. Business Capability and Organisation — Questions 11–20

11. What must the organisation be able to do to create this outcome?
12. Which business capability owns or enables the outcome?
13. Who is accountable for that capability?
14. Which other capabilities does it depend on?
15. Which teams currently perform the work?
16. Where does responsibility cross organisational boundaries?
17. What is intentionally centralised and what is intentionally local?
18. Which governance forum can make the key decisions?
19. Where is ownership unclear or duplicated?
20. Which external partners, suppliers or regulators matter?

## 3. People and Knowledge — Questions 21–30

21. Who performs the work day to day?
22. Who makes the important decisions?
23. Who can approve an exception or override?
24. Who creates, owns and validates the information?
25. Which people hold specialist knowledge that is not documented?
26. What happens if a key person is unavailable?
27. Where are capacity or workload pressures highest?
28. Which activities require the most judgement?
29. What skills or confidence are needed to use the information effectively?
30. What would make users trust—or distrust—a future change?

## 4. Process and Flow — Questions 31–40

31. What triggers the process?
32. What does “complete” look like?
33. What happens first?
34. What happens next?
35. Where does the work wait?
36. Where are hand-offs between teams?
37. Where is information re-keyed, exported or reconciled?
38. Which step creates the most rework?
39. What are the most common exceptions?
40. Which controls or approvals exist and what risk do they manage?

## 5. Decisions — Questions 41–50

41. What decision is the user actually trying to make?
42. Who owns that decision?
43. What event or situation triggers it?
44. How frequently is the decision made?
45. What alternatives are available?
46. What information is required?
47. Which business rules influence the result?
48. What happens when the information is incomplete or contradictory?
49. What action follows the decision?
50. How do you know whether the decision was good?

## 6. Information, Meaning and Semantics — Questions 51–60

51. What are the most important business concepts in this domain?
52. How does the business define each concept?
53. Which terms mean different things to different teams?
54. Who owns or validates the definition?
55. Which concepts are related and how?
56. Which classifications or hierarchies matter?
57. Which information is authoritative for this particular purpose?
58. Which information is frequently challenged or distrusted?
59. How fresh, complete or accurate must the information be for the decision?
60. Can the reported information be traced back to its source and rule?

## 7. Rules, KPIs and Controls — Questions 61–70

61. Which business rules determine eligibility, calculation, classification or approval?
62. Where are those rules documented?
63. Which rules exist only in spreadsheets, code or people's knowledge?
64. Who can approve a change to a rule?
65. Which rules vary by customer, territory, product, contract type or time?
66. Which KPIs are genuinely used to make decisions?
67. How is each KPI calculated?
68. What is included and excluded?
69. Which targets or thresholds matter?
70. Which rules or measures are currently disputed?

## 8. Technology and Data — Questions 71–80

71. Which systems enable the capability?
72. What business purpose does each system serve?
73. Where is information first created?
74. How does information move between systems and teams?
75. Which integrations are automated and which are manual?
76. Which spreadsheets are business-critical?
77. Which business rules are embedded in technology?
78. What happens when a system, feed or workflow fails?
79. Which technology constraints materially limit the business?
80. Which planned technology changes could affect the discovery?

## 9. Pain, Opportunity and Value — Questions 81–90

81. What is the most painful part of the current experience?
82. What evidence shows that this is a real problem?
83. Is the stated problem a symptom or has the root cause been established?
84. Who experiences the impact?
85. What is the cost in time, money, risk or missed opportunity?
86. Which improvement would create the greatest business value?
87. Which opportunity unlocks others?
88. What measurable benefit would a successful change produce?
89. What baseline do we have today?
90. Who owns benefits realisation after delivery?

## 10. Data Products, AI, Validation and Roadmap — Questions 91–100

91. Who would consume the proposed data product or analytical capability?
92. Which decision or action would it improve?
93. What service, quality, freshness and ownership expectations would make it a real product?
94. If AI is proposed, what user task or decision specifically benefits?
95. What must remain under human control?
96. What non-AI alternative could achieve the same outcome?
97. What evidence would prove or disprove the current hypothesis?
98. Which Golden Thread links remain `UNKNOWN`?
99. What must be validated before we invest?
100. What is the smallest next step that creates useful learning or measurable value?

# How to Use the Question Bank

## Before a Workshop

Select roughly 5–12 **primary questions** tied to the session objective. Keep deeper probes in reserve.

## During a Workshop

Do not chase all 100 questions. Follow evidence and terminology that reveal:

- decisions;
- business rules;
- semantic conflicts;
- exceptions;
- ownership;
- value;
- Broken Threads.

## After a Workshop

Review the notes and ask:

1. Which questions were actually answered?
2. Which answers are evidence versus stakeholder statements?
3. Which answers conflict?
4. Which important questions remain open?
5. What is the next highest-value question?

## Question Selection by Workshop Type

| Workshop | Useful Question Ranges |
|---|---|
| Executive / Outcome | 1–20, 81–90 |
| Capability Discovery | 1–20, 81–90 |
| Process Discovery | 21–50, 61–70 |
| Information / Ontology | 41–70 |
| Data Product | 41–80, 81–93 |
| AI Opportunity | 41–60, 71–100 |
| Validation | 47–70, 81–100 |

The ranges are guidance only. The business problem should determine the questions.
