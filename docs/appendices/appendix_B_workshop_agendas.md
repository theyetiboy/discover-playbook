# Appendix B — Example Workshop Agendas

> These agendas are reusable patterns, not mandatory workshop designs. Select the DISCOVER stages and POPIT-V lenses required by the objective.

## Workshop Design Principles

Every workshop should have:

1. a clear business outcome;
2. a specific discovery objective;
3. the right participants;
4. a defined evidence capture method;
5. explicit validation;
6. tangible outputs.

Avoid trying to complete the whole DISCOVER methodology in one workshop.

---

# Agenda 1 — 60-Minute Executive Discovery

## Purpose

Establish context, outcomes, strategic priorities and the highest-value areas for deeper discovery.

## Primary DISCOVER Stages

Define → Investigate

## Primary POPIT-V Lenses

Organisation + Value, with People where ownership matters.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity | Objective | Evidence / Output |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Opening | Confirm purpose and scope | Validated workshop objective |
| 5–15 | Business Context | Understand why now | Strategy / outcome notes |
| 15–30 | Capabilities & Priorities | Identify important business abilities | Candidate `CAP-*` |
| 30–40 | Decisions & Friction | Find high-value decision problems | Candidate `DEC-*`, `PAIN-*` |
| 40–50 | Value | Establish success and cost of inaction | Candidate `VAL-*` |
| 50–57 | Unknowns & Risks | Surface assumptions and missing voices | `ASM-*`, `OQ-*`, `RSK-*` |
| 57–60 | Validate / Close | Confirm understanding | Next discovery actions |

### Core Questions

- What outcome are we trying to improve?
- Which capability matters most?
- Which decisions are currently difficult?
- What would improve if we solved this?
- Who else must we involve?

# Agenda 2 — 90-Minute Process & Decision Discovery

## Purpose

Understand a priority business process, its decisions, rules, information, hand-offs and friction.

## DISCOVER Stages

Investigate → Study → Capture

## POPIT-V

Process + People + Information, with Organisation and Value as needed.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity | Objective |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–10 | Context & boundaries | Trigger, outcome and scope |
| 10–30 | Current flow | Major activities and actors |
| 30–50 | Decisions | Who decides what and why |
| 50–65 | Information & rules | Required facts, definitions and rules |
| 65–75 | Exceptions / controls | Understand non-happy path and risk |
| 75–83 | Pain / value | Identify evidenced friction and impact |
| 83–90 | Validate | Confirm flow and open questions |

### Outputs

- process map;
- decision catalogue entries;
- business rules;
- information requirements;
- pain points;
- validation questions.

# Agenda 3 — 90-Minute Finance Revenue Recognition Discovery

## Purpose

Understand how revenue-recognition decisions are made and evidenced without assuming the problem or solution.

## Suggested Participants

- Financial Controller;
- Commercial Finance;
- accounting SME;
- Sales / contract representative;
- reporting/data analyst;
- relevant system/data owner where useful.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity | Example Questions |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–10 | Outcome / scope | Which reporting or control outcome matters? |
| 10–25 | Trigger / process | What event causes recognition assessment? |
| 25–45 | Decisions | Who decides treatment? What judgement exists? |
| 45–60 | Information | Which contract/licence facts are required? |
| 60–70 | Rules / exceptions | Which rules vary? Who owns them? |
| 70–80 | Reconciliation / evidence | How is the value traced and validated? |
| 80–87 | Pain / value | Where is effort/risk concentrated? |
| 87–90 | Validation | What remains unknown? |

### Avoid

- jumping to a Finance dashboard;
- assuming poor data quality is the root cause;
- treating a spreadsheet as inherently the problem;
- prescribing AI before decision/rule understanding.

# Agenda 4 — 120-Minute Information / Ontology Workshop

## Purpose

Establish business concepts, relationships, definitions and competency questions for a domain.

## DISCOVER Stages

Study → Capture → Organise → Validate

## Primary POPIT-V

Information, supported by Organisation, Process and Value.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity | Output |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–10 | Business questions | Competency questions |
| 10–30 | Concept storm | Candidate concepts |
| 30–50 | Definitions | Glossary candidates / conflicts |
| 50–75 | Relationships | Ontology relationship hypotheses |
| 75–90 | Rules / lifecycle | Constraints, states, events |
| 90–105 | Context / ownership | Bounded contexts and owners |
| 105–113 | Source mapping | Candidate physical mappings |
| 113–120 | Validate | Gaps / next evidence |

### Facilitation Prompts

- What is this thing?
- What is it not?
- Can it exist without the other concept?
- Who owns the meaning?
- Does this term mean the same thing in another team?
- Which business question requires this relationship?

# Agenda 5 — 90-Minute Data Product Discovery

## Purpose

Test whether a proposed data product represents a coherent, owned and valuable product rather than simply a report or dataset.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity | Output |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–10 | Consumer / outcome | Product beneficiary and value |
| 10–25 | Decisions & actions | `DEC-*`, `ACT-*` |
| 25–45 | Information / semantics | `INF-*`, `BR-*` |
| 45–60 | Quality / service | Freshness, quality, access |
| 60–70 | Ownership / governance | Product and data ownership |
| 70–80 | Current constraints | `PAIN-*`, dependencies |
| 80–87 | Value hypothesis | `VAL-*`, success measures |
| 87–90 | Validate | Product hypothesis status |

### Decision

At the end, the correct conclusion may be:

- viable candidate data product;
- more discovery required;
- problem is primarily process/ownership;
- existing product can be extended;
- no product needed.

## Purpose

Assess whether an AI capability is a credible response to an evidenced business need.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity | Output |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–10 | Outcome / problem | Evidenced problem |
| 10–25 | User task / workflow | Human workflow |
| 25–40 | Decision / action | `DEC-*`, `ACT-*` |
| 40–55 | Information / grounding | `INF-*`, `DAT-*`, provenance |
| 55–65 | Human oversight | Accountability / approval |
| 65–75 | Risk / controls | `RSK-*` |
| 75–83 | Alternatives | AI vs simpler interventions |
| 83–88 | Evaluation / value | acceptance measures |
| 88–90 | Recommendation | experiment / more discovery / reject |

# Agenda 7 — 60-Minute Validation Workshop

## Purpose

Validate existing findings rather than generate an entirely new model.

## Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|---:|---|
| 0–5 | Validation scope |
| 5–15 | What we believe we know |
| 15–30 | Conflicts / assumptions |
| 30–42 | Golden Thread / Broken Threads |
| 42–52 | Opportunities / value |
| 52–57 | Decisions required |
| 57–60 | Sign-off / next evidence |

### Validation Language

Use:

> "Our current evidence suggests..."

> "We have heard two different definitions..."

> "This relationship remains unvalidated..."

Avoid:

> "The AI says..."

# Agenda 8 — Half-Day Domain Discovery

## Purpose

Create a broad shared picture of an unfamiliar domain before deeper targeted sessions.

| Time | Activity |
|---:|---|
| 0–15 | Context, outcomes, scope |
| 15–45 | Capability landscape |
| 45–75 | Stakeholders / ownership |
| 75–90 | Break |
| 90–125 | End-to-end value/process flow |
| 125–155 | Decisions / information |
| 155–175 | Pain, risk and opportunities |
| 175–200 | Terminology / semantic hotspots |
| 200–220 | Value / prioritisation |
| 220–235 | Assumptions / missing evidence |
| 235–240 | Close |

Do not attempt detailed process mapping, ontology and solution design in the same half-day. Use the session to identify the next focused discovery.

# Remote Workshop Guidance

For virtual sessions:

- make the objective visible;
- provide a shared board/document;
- use silent individual contribution before group debate;
- keep instructions short;
- allow regular breaks for sessions over 90 minutes;
- assign a note/evidence capture role;
- export the board immediately after the session;
- distinguish raw notes from validated artefacts.

# Workshop Output Rule

A workshop is not “complete” because the meeting ended. It is complete when:

- evidence has been captured;
- findings have been classified;
- contradictions and assumptions are visible;
- artefacts have been updated;
- validation owners are known;
- the next discovery action is clear.
