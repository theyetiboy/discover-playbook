# Appendix C — DISCOVER Facilitation Guide

> The facilitator's job is to improve shared understanding, expose evidence and uncertainty, and help the group learn—not to demonstrate expertise or steer stakeholders toward a predetermined solution.

## 1. Before the Session

### Clarify the Outcome

Be able to state:

- why the session exists;
- what business question it helps answer;
- what is in/out of scope;
- what tangible outputs are expected;
- which DISCOVER stages and POPIT-V lenses are relevant.

### Invite the Right People

Ask:

- Who owns the outcome?
- Who performs the work?
- Who understands exceptions?
- Who owns the information/rules?
- Who can validate the result?
- Which missing perspective could invalidate our assumptions?

### Review Evidence

Read relevant:

- strategy/policy;
- process documentation;
- glossary;
- reports/KPIs;
- contracts/rules;
- system/data catalogues;
- prior workshop notes.

Create hypotheses, but label them as hypotheses.

### Design Questions

Prepare:

- 5–12 primary questions;
- deeper probes;
- parking-lot topics;
- evidence to capture;
- validation questions.

## 2. Opening a Workshop

A strong opening covers:

1. **Purpose** — why we are here.
2. **Outcome** — what we want to understand.
3. **Scope** — what we will and will not cover.
4. **Working agreement** — curiosity, challenge, no blame.
5. **Evidence rule** — disagreement is useful; unknown is acceptable.
6. **Output** — what will happen to the information after the session.

### Example Opening

> "The purpose today is to understand how the current forecasting decision works—who makes it, what information and rules it depends on, and where the main constraints are. We are not selecting a technology today. Where we hear different versions of the process, we'll capture the difference rather than force agreement without evidence."

## 3. Core Facilitation Behaviours

### Stay Neutral

Avoid leading questions:

Bad:
> Would a dashboard solve that?

Better:
> What makes the decision difficult today?

### Ask for Examples

When a participant says “always”, “never”, “the system”, “bad data” or “everyone”, ask for a concrete example.

### Separate Symptom From Cause

Stakeholder:
> The report is useless.

Probe:
- Which decision is it meant to support?
- What makes it unusable?
- Is the problem timing, definition, quality, granularity or something else?
- Can you show a recent example?

### Make Language Visible

If two people use “customer” differently, stop and capture the definitions.

### Protect the Unknown

Do not reward false certainty.

Use:

- `UNKNOWN`;
- `ASM-*`;
- `OQ-*`;
- conflicting definitions/status.

## 4. Handling Dominant Voices

Techniques:

- silent brainstorm first;
- round-robin;
- ask operational roles directly;
- private voting where suitable;
- park debates;
- summarise then invite challenge.

Useful prompt:

> "We've heard the leadership view. I'd like to understand how this actually works for the person performing the activity."

## 5. Handling Disagreement

Disagreement is evidence.

### Do

- capture both versions;
- ask what source supports each;
- identify context;
- identify who can validate;
- create an open question.

### Do Not

- vote on business truth;
- choose the senior person's answer automatically;
- ask AI to resolve it;
- silently rewrite the artefact.

## 6. Handling Solutioning

Stakeholders naturally suggest solutions.

Capture them as hypotheses:

`OPP-001 Candidate opportunity: ...`

Then ask:

- What outcome would it create?
- Which problem does it address?
- Which decision/action improves?
- What evidence suggests it would help?
- What simpler alternative exists?

Do not suppress ideas; prevent them becoming untested requirements.

## 7. Handling AI Enthusiasm

When somebody says:

> "Can we use AI?"

ask:

1. Which user task?
2. Which decision/action?
3. What information would it need?
4. What must remain human-controlled?
5. How would we evaluate quality?
6. What business value would it create?
7. What non-AI alternative exists?

## 8. Timeboxing

When a debate is valuable but exceeds the session objective:

1. summarise the disagreement;
2. capture it as hotspot/open question;
3. assign validation;
4. return to agenda.

Suggested language:

> "This matters, but resolving it requires evidence we don't have in the room. I'm capturing it as an open question so we don't manufacture an answer."

## 9. Visual Facilitation

Useful visual structures:

- capability map;
- process timeline;
- EventStorm;
- concept map;
- decision-information-rule matrix;
- value stream;
- Golden Thread.

Keep legends visible.

Use one visual grammar per exercise.

Do not create an intricate diagram while participants wait.

## 10. Evidence Capture

Assign a capture role where possible.

For important statements record:

- what was said;
- role/source;
- classification;
- related artefact IDs;
- validation need.

After the session, separate raw notes from validated canonical artefacts.

## 11. Closing the Workshop

Allow time for validation.

Ask:

- What have we misunderstood?
- What is missing?
- Which assumption concerns you?
- Which definition still conflicts?
- Who else should validate this?
- What evidence should we obtain next?
- What is the most important thing we learned?

Summarise:

- validated understanding;
- open questions;
- decisions;
- next step.

## 12. After the Workshop

Within a practical timeframe:

1. preserve raw notes;
2. update Evidence Register;
3. classify statements;
4. update canonical artefacts;
5. preserve stable IDs;
6. identify Broken Threads;
7. send focused validation;
8. update opportunity/value hypotheses only where evidence supports them.

## 13. Common Facilitation Failure Modes

### The Interview Disguised as a Workshop
Only the facilitator asks questions and participants do not interact.

### The Solution Pitch
The session exists to get approval for a solution already chosen.

### The Sticky-Note Factory
Lots of content is generated but never organised or validated.

### The Executive Echo Chamber
Only leadership perspectives are captured.

### The False Consensus
Disagreement is removed from the final notes.

### The Methodology Performance
The facilitator insists on completing every POPIT-V lens or artefact.

### The AI Shortcut
An assistant is used to fill missing business facts.

## 14. Accessibility and Inclusion

Consider:

- remote participation;
- time zones;
- language/jargon;
- accessible colours and text sizes;
- neurodiversity;
- different communication styles;
- advance pre-read;
- breaks;
- asynchronous contribution.
