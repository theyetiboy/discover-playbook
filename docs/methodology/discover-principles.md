# DISCOVER Principles

The DISCOVER principles describe **how practitioners should behave while doing discovery**.
They are deliberately more durable than any individual workshop technique, template or technology.

The principles apply across the full DISCOVER lifecycle and should be used to challenge weak discovery practice.

---

## 1. Business Before Technology

Begin with the business, not the proposed technical solution.

Understand the outcomes, capabilities, processes, decisions, information and rules before deciding how technology should respond.

A stakeholder may arrive asking for a dashboard, data platform, automation or AI agent. Treat that request as useful evidence of a perceived need, not automatically as the final requirement.

**Challenge question:** What business problem or opportunity would still exist if the proposed technology did not?

---

## 2. Start With Outcomes

Discovery should begin by understanding what the organisation is trying to achieve and how success will be recognised.

Outputs such as reports, workshops, models and backlogs are not outcomes.

**Challenge question:** What will be measurably better if this work succeeds?

---

## 3. Decisions Drive Data

Data becomes valuable when it helps a person, team or automated process make a better decision or take a better action.

When somebody asks for more data or better reporting, ask:

1. What decision are you trying to make?
2. What action follows the decision?
3. What information is required?
4. What rules affect interpretation?
5. What happens when the information is late, wrong or missing?

---

## 4. Model the Business, Not the Systems

Business concepts should be understood independently from the applications that currently store or process them.

Systems change. The underlying business meaning is often more durable.

A CRM table is not the definition of a Customer. A rights-management application is not the definition of a Right. A finance ledger is not the definition of Revenue.

---

## 5. Understand People in Context

People do not operate independently from organisational structures, incentives, controls, tools and constraints.

Understand what people are trying to accomplish, the environment in which they work, the decisions they own and the consequences they face.

Avoid describing user behaviour as a problem without understanding why that behaviour exists.

---

## 6. Evidence Over Assumption

Discovery should progressively replace assumptions with evidence.

Classify important statements as:

- **Evidence** — explicitly supported by a trusted source;
- **Stakeholder Statement** — stated by a stakeholder but not independently validated;
- **Assumption** — plausible but unverified;
- **Interpretation** — an analytical conclusion drawn from evidence;
- **Recommendation** — a proposed intervention;
- **Open Question** — information still required.

Never silently convert an assumption into a fact.

---

## 7. Create Shared Understanding

A major purpose of discovery is to establish a common understanding between stakeholders who may use different language, measures, rules or mental models.

Expose differences rather than smoothing them over.

A disagreement about a definition is a discovery finding.

---

## 8. Capture Knowledge Once, Reuse It

Discovery knowledge should become a reusable organisational asset.

Where practical, capture capabilities, concepts, rules, decisions, measures and evidence in structured artefacts that can be referenced by future discovery work.

Avoid creating disconnected workshop outputs that cannot be maintained.

---

## 9. Connect Everything

Individual artefacts are less valuable when they exist in isolation.

Use the Golden Thread to connect:

**Strategy → Capability → Process → Decision → Information → Business Rules → Technology/Data → Data Product/Analytics → Action → Value**

The purpose is traceability, not bureaucracy.

---

## 10. Explore Before You Converge

Early discovery should widen understanding before narrowing to a solution.

Investigate alternatives, exceptions, stakeholder perspectives and causes before deciding what should change.

Premature convergence often turns the first plausible solution into an accidental requirement.

---

## 11. Remain Solution Neutral

A good discovery practitioner can discuss possible solutions without becoming attached to them.

Candidate solutions may emerge at any point, but they remain hypotheses until the evidence supports them.

Ask whether process, policy, ownership, skills, information quality, governance or simpler technology changes could deliver the outcome before escalating complexity.

---

## 12. Validate Continuously

Validation is not a final ceremony at the end of discovery.

Validate terminology, assumptions, models, rules, ownership and interpretations throughout the engagement.

DISCOVER is iterative. New evidence may require returning to earlier work.

Progress should be based on **sufficient confidence**, not perfect certainty.

---

## 13. Design Governance In

Ownership, quality, privacy, security, retention, controls and accountability should be considered during discovery rather than added after a solution is designed.

Governance should be proportionate to the business risk and use case.

---

## 14. Every Recommendation Must Deliver Value

Every recommendation should ultimately answer:

> **So what?**

Trace proposed changes to a business outcome and a meaningful way of measuring success.

Value can include:

- revenue growth;
- cost reduction;
- productivity;
- risk reduction;
- regulatory compliance;
- improved customer or employee experience;
- improved speed or quality of decision-making;
- strategic capability.

Where value cannot yet be quantified, state the hypothesis and identify what needs to be measured.

---

# Applying the Principles

Use the principles as a quality check throughout DISCOVER.

A discovery output should be challenged if it:

- begins with technology rather than business outcomes;
- cannot identify the decision or action being supported;
- contains unsupported factual claims;
- hides conflicting definitions;
- creates artefacts without connecting them;
- converges on a solution before the problem is understood;
- lacks a validation route;
- lacks ownership or governance;
- cannot explain the expected value.

The principles are intentionally mutually reinforcing. Together they make DISCOVER business-led, evidence-based, traceable and value-focused.
