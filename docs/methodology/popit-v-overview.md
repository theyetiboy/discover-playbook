# POPIT-V Overview

## Purpose

POPIT-V is the primary analytical lens used throughout the DISCOVER methodology.

It provides six perspectives through which a business domain, capability, process, problem or opportunity can be explored:

```text
P   People

O   Organisation

P   Process

I   Information

T   Technology

V   Value
```

Together:

# POPIT-V

The purpose of POPIT-V is to prevent discovery from becoming too narrowly focused on one aspect of the organisation.

For example, a problem initially described as:

```text
"Our reporting technology is poor."
```

may actually involve:

```text
People

Specialist knowledge exists with only
a small number of analysts.


Organisation

Ownership of reporting is unclear.


Process

Reporting requires several manual
reconciliations.


Information

Definitions differ between reports.


Technology

Information exists across multiple
systems and spreadsheets.


Value

Significant effort is spent creating
reports without clear evidence that
all reports support important decisions.
```

The technology is only one part of the problem.

POPIT-V helps DISCOVER understand the **whole business system**.

---

# Origins of POPIT

The established POPIT model considers five dimensions:

```text
People

Organisation

Process

Information

Technology
```

It is used within professional Business Analysis practice to examine different aspects of a business system and to support analysis of business change.

BCS materials describe POPIT as a model for considering the different aspects involved when analysing business improvements and identifying required business changes (BCS, 2023).

POPIT is also used within BCS Business Analysis Practice when considering Target Operating Models and gap analysis (BCS, 2023).

DISCOVER adopts these five established perspectives and extends the model with a sixth:

```text
Value
```

creating:

# POPIT-V

---

# Why DISCOVER Adds Value

Traditional POPIT helps ensure that organisational change is considered holistically.

DISCOVER adds **Value** because Data, Analytics and AI discovery should explicitly answer:

> **Why does this matter?**

Without this perspective it is possible to create:

```text
Better Process

Better Data

Better Technology

Better Governance
```

without establishing whether the change delivers an outcome that matters.

The Value perspective asks:

```text
What business outcome improves?

Who receives the benefit?

How significant is the benefit?

How will success be measured?

Is the improvement worth the investment?
```

This creates a direct connection between POPIT-V and the DISCOVER Golden Thread.

# The POPIT-V Model

A simple representation is:

```text
                      BUSINESS
                       PROBLEM
                          │
                          ▼

                       PEOPLE
                          │
                          │
           ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
           │              │              │
           ▼              ▼              ▼
     ORGANISATION      PROCESS      INFORMATION
           │              │              │
           └──────────────┼──────────────┘
                          │
                          ▼
                     TECHNOLOGY
                          │
                          ▼
                        VALUE
```

This should not be interpreted as a hierarchy.

The six dimensions interact continuously.

A more accurate representation is:

```text
                 ┌──────────────┐
                 │    PEOPLE    │
                 └──────┬───────┘
                        │
        ┌───────────────┼───────────────┐
        │                               │
        ▼                               ▼
┌──────────────┐                ┌──────────────┐
│ ORGANISATION │                │   PROCESS    │
└──────┬───────┘                └──────┬───────┘
       │                               │
       └───────────────┬───────────────┘
                       │
                       ▼
               ┌──────────────┐
               │ INFORMATION  │
               └──────┬───────┘
                      │
                      ▼
               ┌──────────────┐
               │  TECHNOLOGY  │
               └──────┬───────┘
                      │
                      ▼
               ┌──────────────┐
               │    VALUE     │
               └──────────────┘
```

Changes within one perspective frequently affect several others.

# Systems Thinking

POPIT-V encourages practitioners to consider the organisation as an interconnected system.

Consider a proposed technology change:

```text
Introduce New Finance Platform
```

The change may affect:

```text
PEOPLE

New skills required
Roles may change


ORGANISATION

Ownership may move
Governance may change


PROCESS

Workflows may change
Approvals may change


INFORMATION

Definitions may need standardising
Master data may change


TECHNOLOGY

Applications and integrations change


VALUE

Cost, speed, quality and decision-making
should improve
```

Analysing only:

```text
Technology
```

would provide an incomplete view of the change.

# Information Before Data

DISCOVER distinguishes between:

```text
INFORMATION

What the business needs to know
```

and:

```text
DATA

Recorded facts that can be used
to provide that information
```

For example:

```text
Business Information

Rights Availability

        │
        ▼

May Require Data About

Programme
Contract
Rights
Territory
Platform
Language
Licence Window
Exclusivity
```

Starting with the information need helps avoid allowing available datasets to define the business requirement.

# POPIT-V Is Interconnected

The greatest value comes from understanding relationships across the six dimensions.

Consider:

```text
INFORMATION PROBLEM

Forecast information is unreliable.
```

Further discovery may reveal:

```text
PEOPLE

Different analysts apply
different assumptions.


ORGANISATION

No clear owner exists for
forecast methodology.


PROCESS

Forecast updates occur through
different local processes.


INFORMATION

Forecast definitions and business
rules are inconsistent.


TECHNOLOGY

Forecasts are maintained in
separate spreadsheets.


VALUE

Leaders cannot confidently identify
future revenue risk.
```

The response should therefore not simply be:

```text
Replace the spreadsheets.
```

The problem requires a more holistic response.

# POPIT-V and Root Cause Analysis

POPIT-V can help avoid treating symptoms as root causes.

Suppose the symptom is:

```text
Dashboard takes five days to produce.
```

Ask across POPIT-V:

```text
People

Who produces it?
What knowledge is required?


Organisation

Who owns it?
Why does it exist?


Process

What activities take five days?


Information

Which inputs are difficult to obtain?


Technology

Which systems or tools constrain production?


Value

Who uses it?
What decision does it support?
```

The result may reveal:

```text
The dashboard itself is not the problem.

The underlying issue is inconsistent
business definitions and fragmented data.
```

# POPIT-V and the Golden Thread

POPIT-V and the Golden Thread are complementary.

```text

```

```text

```

```text
                      STRATEGY
                         │
                         ▼
                    CAPABILITY
                         │
            ┌────────────┼────────────┐
            ▼            ▼            ▼
          People       Process    Information
            │            │            │
            └────────────┼────────────┘
                         │
                         ▼
                     Technology
                         │
                         ▼
                    Data Product
                         │

                         │
                         ▼
                       Value
```

POPIT-V asks:

> **Have we understood the whole business system?**

> **Can we connect the solution back to the business need and forward to value?**

# POPIT-V and the Maturity Model

The DISCOVER Maturity Model uses POPIT-V as its assessment dimensions.

For example:

| Dimension | Current | Target |
|---|---:|---:|
| People | 3 | 4 |
| Organisation | 2 | 4 |
| Process | 2 | 4 |
| Information | 1 | 4 |
| Technology | 3 | 4 |
| Value | 2 | 4 |

This prevents maturity being interpreted as a purely technological concern.

A business area may have:

```text
Technology = 4

Information = 1
```

indicating that modern technology exists but poor information governance may still prevent effective outcomes.

# POPIT-V Workshop

A generic POPIT-V discovery workshop may follow this structure.

| Stage | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm business problem |
| 2 | Confirm desired outcome |
| 3 | Explore People |
| 4 | Explore Organisation |
| 5 | Explore Process |
| 6 | Explore Information |
| 7 | Explore Technology |
| 8 | Explore Value |
| 9 | Identify connections |
| 10 | Identify pain points |
| 11 | Identify assumptions |
| 12 | Identify opportunities |
| 13 | Agree further discovery |

POPIT-V should not necessarily be facilitated as six completely separate conversations.

A discussion about one dimension will naturally expose findings in others.

Capture those relationships as they emerge.

# Avoiding POPIT-V Silos

POPIT-V should not create six independent workstreams.

The dimensions interact.

For example:

```text
Information Problem

        │
        ▼

Requires Governance

        │
        ▼

Organisation Problem

        │
        ▼

Requires Ownership

        │
        ▼

People Problem

        │
        ▼

Changes Process

        │
        ▼

May Require Technology

        │
        ▼

Creates Value
```

The relationships between dimensions are often where the most important discovery insights exist.

# Common Pitfalls

## Treating POPIT-V as a Checklist

Do not simply ask:

```text
People done?

Organisation done?

Process done?

Information done?

Technology done?

Value done?
```

The objective is to understand the **relationships** between the dimensions.

---

## Equal Depth Everywhere

Not every engagement requires equal analysis across all six areas.

For example, a semantic problem may require deep Information discovery and relatively lightweight Technology analysis.

Use judgement.

---

## Technology Dominance

Do not allow Technology to consume the entire workshop.

It is one of six dimensions.

---

## Ignoring Value Until the End

Value should be considered throughout discovery.

If the practitioner cannot explain why an area matters, ask whether further investigation is justified.

---

## Assuming Every Problem Needs Six Solutions

POPIT-V is an analytical model.

It does not mean every recommendation requires change across every dimension.

Sometimes the correct response may genuinely be:

```text
Clarify One Business Rule
```

---

## Treating Symptoms as Causes

A Technology problem may originate in Information.

A Process problem may originate in Organisation.

A People problem may originate in Process.

Follow the relationships.

---

## Using POPIT-V Without Evidence

Statements such as:

```text
"Process maturity is poor."
```

should be supported by evidence.

Capture what was observed and why it matters.

---

## Forgetting the User

Even a holistic internal model can lose sight of the person receiving the outcome.

Continue to ask:

```text
Who needs this?

What are they trying to achieve?
```

# What Good Looks Like

Effective POPIT-V discovery should result in:

- A balanced view of the business
- Relevant stakeholders identified
- Organisational context understood
- Processes understood
- Information requirements understood
- Technology dependencies understood
- Business value articulated
- Relationships between dimensions visible
- Root causes distinguished from symptoms
- Important ownership gaps exposed
- Opportunities grounded in evidence
- Recommendations that consider wider change impact
- Traceability into the Golden Thread
- Inputs into prioritisation and roadmapping

The objective is not to populate six boxes.

The objective is to understand the **business as a connected system**.

# POPIT-V Quick Reference

For any discovery problem ask:

## People

```text
Who?
```

Who performs, decides, owns, knows and uses?

---

## Organisation

```text
Why and where?
```

What strategy, capability, structure, ownership and governance apply?

---

## Process

```text
How?
```

How does work happen?

---

## Information

```text
What do we need to know?
```

What concepts, definitions, rules, KPIs and data are required?

---

## Technology

```text
What enables it?
```

Which systems, applications, integrations and platforms support the business?

---

## Value

```text
So what?
```

What improves, for whom, and how will we know?

# The POPIT-V Questions

At its simplest:

```text
PEOPLE

Who is involved?


ORGANISATION

Why does this capability exist
and who owns it?


PROCESS

How does the work happen?


INFORMATION

What does the business need to know?


TECHNOLOGY

What enables or constrains it?


VALUE

Why does any of this matter?
```

If discovery can answer these six questions with evidence, it has established a strong foundation for deeper analysis.
