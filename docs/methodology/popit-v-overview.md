# POPIT-V

POPIT-V is the primary analytical lens used by DISCOVER to understand a business domain from six complementary perspectives:

- **People**
- **Organisation**
- **Process**
- **Information**
- **Technology**
- **Value**

POPIT is an established organisational analysis model. DISCOVER extends it with **Value** so that discovery is explicitly connected to measurable business outcomes.

---

# Why POPIT-V

Business problems rarely belong to one dimension.

A reporting problem may appear to be a Technology problem but actually be caused by unclear ownership, inconsistent definitions, weak processes or conflicting business rules.

POPIT-V reduces the risk of solving the visible symptom while leaving the underlying system unchanged.

---

# The Six Perspectives

## People

Understand the people who perform work, make decisions, use information and experience the consequences of change.

Explore:

- roles and responsibilities;
- skills and knowledge;
- capacity;
- behaviours;
- incentives;
- decision rights;
- pain points;
- adoption needs.

## Organisation

Understand how the organisation is structured and how responsibility is distributed.

Explore:

- business capabilities;
- teams and functions;
- ownership;
- governance;
- operating model;
- organisational boundaries;
- partners and suppliers;
- strategic alignment.

## Process

Understand how work is performed in reality.

Explore:

- triggers;
- activities;
- inputs and outputs;
- hand-offs;
- controls;
- exceptions;
- delays;
- rework;
- manual intervention.

## Information

Understand the information required by the business and the meaning attached to it.

Explore:

- business concepts;
- terminology;
- decisions and information requirements;
- measures and KPIs;
- business rules;
- metadata;
- quality;
- lineage;
- provenance;
- authoritative sources.

## Technology

Understand how technology currently enables or constrains the business.

Explore:

- applications;
- platforms;
- integrations;
- spreadsheets;
- data stores;
- analytics tools;
- automation;
- AI;
- technical constraints and debt.

Technology should explain the current environment. It should not dictate the discovery.

## Value

Understand why the capability exists and what meaningful outcome should improve.

Explore:

- strategic outcomes;
- customer outcomes;
- financial outcomes;
- productivity;
- cost;
- risk;
- compliance;
- KPIs;
- benefits;
- opportunity cost.

Value provides the final challenge: **So what?**

# POPIT-V Is a Lens, Not a Checklist

Across a discovery engagement, practitioners should consider all relevant POPIT-V perspectives.

An individual workshop **does not need to explore all six perspectives**.

Select the perspectives required to achieve the objective of the interaction.

For example:

- an initial stakeholder session may focus on Organisation, People and Value;
- a process deep-dive may focus on Process, People and Information;
- a data-definition workshop may focus primarily on Information and Organisation;
- a technical landscape session may focus on Technology and Information;
- an opportunity review may focus on Value while tracing findings back through the other dimensions.

The objective is to avoid blind spots across the engagement, not to turn POPIT-V into a mechanical agenda.

# Questions Across the Lens

A useful set of cross-cutting questions is:

| Perspective | Core Question |
|---|---|
| People | Who performs the work or makes the decision? |
| Organisation | Which capability owns the outcome? |
| Process | What actually happens? |
| Information | What information and rules are required? |
| Technology | How is the work currently enabled or constrained? |
| Value | What improves if we change it? |

# Relationship to the Golden Thread

POPIT-V and the Golden Thread solve different problems.

**POPIT-V** helps a practitioner examine the domain from multiple perspectives.

**The Golden Thread** connects individual findings from strategy through to value.

For example:

- Organisation may identify `CAP-001 Forecast Financial Performance`;
- Process may identify `PRC-001 Monthly Forecasting`;
- People may identify the decision owner;
- Information may identify `DEC-001`, `INF-001` and `BR-001`;
- Technology may identify `SYS-001` and `DAT-001`;
- Value may identify `VAL-001 Improved Forecast Confidence`.

Together they create a multidimensional but traceable understanding of the business.

# Typical Failure Modes

## Technology Dominates

The workshop becomes a tour of systems rather than an exploration of the business.

**Correction:** Return to capability, process, decision and value.

## Process Is Documented Without Decisions

A detailed process map is created but nobody asks why the activity exists or which decision it supports.

**Correction:** Identify decisions and actions embedded in the process.

## Information Is Treated as Columns

The discussion jumps directly to fields and tables.

**Correction:** Establish business concepts, definitions, rules and information requirements first.

## People Problems Are Blamed on Users

Workarounds are described as resistance or poor discipline.

**Correction:** Investigate incentives, workload, ownership, process and system constraints.

## Value Is Assumed

An initiative is labelled valuable without a measure or beneficiary.

**Correction:** Define the expected outcome, who benefits and how success would be observed.

---

# Expected Result

A successful POPIT-V assessment creates a balanced understanding of the domain and exposes interactions between organisational, human, process, information, technology and value concerns.

It should make it easier to distinguish root causes from symptoms and to identify where further discovery is required.
