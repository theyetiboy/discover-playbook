# The DISCOVER Framework

## Purpose

DISCOVER is a structured methodology for understanding complex business problems and translating that understanding into actionable opportunities, Data Products, AI capabilities and strategic roadmaps.

The framework is designed for discovery across business domains including:

- Finance
- Marketing
- Sales
- Rights
- Content
- Production
- Distribution
- Operations
- Data & Analytics
- Technology

DISCOVER deliberately begins with the **business**, rather than with technology or a predetermined solution.

Its purpose is to create enough shared understanding and evidence to answer:

```text
What problem are we trying to solve?

Why does it matter?

Who is affected?

How does the business operate today?

What decisions need to be made?

What information supports those decisions?

What prevents better outcomes?

What opportunities exist?

Which opportunities should we pursue?

What should happen next?
```

The framework consists of eight stages:

```text
D   Define
I   Investigate
S   Study
C   Capture
O   Organise
V   Validate
E   Evaluate
R   Roadmap
```

# DISCOVER

# Why DISCOVER Exists

Organisations frequently move too quickly from:

```text
Problem
   │
   ▼
Solution
```

For example:

```text
"We need a dashboard."

"We need a new data platform."

"We need AI."

"We need a Knowledge Graph."

"We need to replace this system."

"We need better reporting."
```

These statements may describe a potential solution or symptom rather than the underlying business problem.

DISCOVER creates a deliberate space between identifying a need and committing to a solution.

```text
Problem / Opportunity
        │
        ▼
     DISCOVER
        │
        ▼
Shared Understanding
        │
        ▼
Evidence
        │
        ▼
Options
        │
        ▼
Prioritised Direction
        │
        ▼
Roadmap
```

GDS discovery guidance similarly recommends understanding the problem, users, context, constraints and opportunities before committing to build a service (Government Digital Service, n.d.).

DISCOVER extends this principle specifically into enterprise Business, Data, Analytics and AI discovery.

# The DISCOVER Philosophy

DISCOVER is based on a simple idea:

> **Understand the business before designing the solution.**

The methodology moves progressively from uncertainty towards evidence-based direction.

```text
HIGH
UNCERTAINTY
     │
     │
     ▼
DEFINE
     │
     ▼
INVESTIGATE
     │
     ▼
STUDY
     │
     ▼
CAPTURE
     │
     ▼
ORGANISE
     │
     ▼
VALIDATE
     │
     ▼
EVALUATE
     │
     ▼
ROADMAP
     │
     ▼
HIGHER
CONFIDENCE
```

Discovery does not remove uncertainty completely.

Instead, it seeks to reduce important uncertainty sufficiently to make better decisions.

# DISCOVER is Not a Delivery Lifecycle

DISCOVER is a **discovery methodology**.

It is not intended to replace:

- Agile delivery
- Scrum
- Kanban
- Product Management
- Software Development Lifecycle
- Enterprise Architecture
- Data Engineering
- Project Management
- Change Management

Instead, DISCOVER helps determine:

```text
What should we do?

Why should we do it?

For whom?

What value should it create?

What do we need to understand first?
```

Delivery approaches then determine:

```text
How will we build and implement it?
```

A typical relationship might therefore be:

```text
DISCOVER
    │
    ▼
Opportunity
    │
    ▼
Prioritisation
    │
    ▼
Roadmap
    │
    ▼
Delivery
    │
    ▼
Measure
    │
    ▼
Learn
```

# DISCOVER is Iterative

The eight stages provide structure, but they should not be interpreted as a rigid waterfall.

Discovery frequently moves backwards as new information emerges.

For example:

```text
Define
   │
   ▼
Investigate
   │
   ▼
Study
   │
   ▼
New Stakeholder Discovered
   │
   └──────────────► Investigate
```

Or:

```text
Validate
   │
   ▼
Definition Disputed
   │
   └──────────────► Capture
```

The methodology therefore supports iteration.

```text
Define
  ↓
Investigate
  ↓
Study
  ↓
Capture
  ↓
Organise
  ↓
Validate
  ↓
Evaluate
  ↓
Roadmap
  ↺
```

Learning can cause earlier assumptions to be revisited at any point.

# Divergence and Convergence

DISCOVER combines periods of broad exploration with periods of synthesis and decision-making.

The Design Council's Double Diamond similarly represents design as cycles of divergent exploration followed by convergent focus (Design Council, n.d.).

Within DISCOVER:

```text
DEFINE
   │
   ▼
Establish Focus

INVESTIGATE
   │
   ▼
Expand Understanding

STUDY
   │
   ▼
Explore Deeply

CAPTURE
   │
   ▼
Preserve Knowledge

ORGANISE
   │
   ▼
Create Structure

VALIDATE
   │
   ▼
Establish Confidence

EVALUATE
   │
   ▼
Compare Opportunities

ROADMAP
   │
   ▼
Create Direction
```

The objective is to explore broadly enough to understand the problem while progressively converging towards actionable recommendations.

# The Eight Stages

# 1. Define

## Objective

Establish why discovery is needed, what problem or opportunity is being explored and what success should look like.

The Define stage establishes the initial boundaries of the engagement.

It asks:

```text
Why are we here?

What are we trying to understand?

What business outcome matters?

What is currently known?

What is in scope?

What is outside scope?
```

---

## Key Activities

Typical activities include:

- Understand the initial request
- Identify the business problem
- Understand strategic context
- Define discovery objectives
- Establish scope
- Identify assumptions
- Identify constraints
- Identify initial stakeholders
- Define desired outcomes
- Agree discovery success criteria

---

## Key Questions

- What prompted this discovery?
- What problem is being experienced?
- What evidence exists?
- What business outcome needs to improve?
- Why does this matter now?
- Who is affected?
- What decisions need to be made?
- What is in scope?
- What is explicitly out of scope?
- What would a successful discovery provide?

---

## Typical Artefacts

- Discovery Brief
- Initial Stakeholder Register
- Scope
- Objectives
- Assumptions
- Risks
- Initial Business Questions

---

## Exit Considerations

Before progressing, there should be sufficient agreement about:

```text
Purpose

Scope

Objectives

Stakeholders

Expected Outcomes
```

Perfect certainty is not required.

The objective is sufficient clarity to begin structured investigation.

# 2. Investigate

## Objective

Build an initial understanding of the business landscape.

The Investigate stage broadens discovery.

It seeks to identify the people, capabilities, processes, information, technology and strategic context relevant to the problem.

This aligns with established business analysis practice in which elicitation and collaboration are used to obtain stakeholder information, confirm findings and communicate business analysis information (IIBA, n.d.a).

---

## Key Activities

Typical activities include:

- Stakeholder Mapping
- Stakeholder Interviews
- Document Review
- Business Capability Mapping
- Organisational Analysis
- Existing System Review
- Report and Dashboard Review
- Existing Data Review
- Strategic Context Analysis
- Initial Business Glossary Discovery

---

## Key Questions

- Who understands this area?
- Who owns it?
- Which business capabilities are involved?
- Which processes contribute to the outcome?
- What information exists?
- Which systems are involved?
- What existing reporting exists?
- Which business terms matter?
- What previous work has been completed?
- Which constraints already exist?

---

## Typical Artefacts

- Stakeholder Map
- Stakeholder Register
- Capability Map
- Initial Business Glossary
- Existing System Inventory
- Existing Report Inventory
- Document Inventory
- Discovery Notes

---

## Exit Considerations

The Investigate stage should provide a sufficiently broad understanding to identify:

```text
Who we need to speak to

What we need to study

Where complexity appears to exist

What requires deeper investigation
```

# 3. Study

## Objective

Explore the business in sufficient depth to understand how it actually operates.

The Study stage moves from broad investigation into deeper analysis.

This is where practitioners examine:

- Processes
- Decisions
- Rules
- Information
- Handovers
- Exceptions
- Data
- Technology
- Pain Points
- Value Streams

The emphasis is on discovering **reality**, not merely documented process.

- Observation
- Process Walkthroughs
- Data Analysis

- What actually happens today?
- What triggers the process?
- What decisions are made?
- Who makes them?
- What information is required?
- Which business rules apply?
- Where do exceptions occur?
- Where is work manual?
- Where does work wait?
- Where does rework occur?
- Which systems are involved?
- Where are spreadsheets used?
- Which problems are symptoms rather than causes?

## Typical Artefacts

## Objective

Discovery creates large amounts of information.

Without structured capture, knowledge remains scattered across:

The Capture stage turns that knowledge into reusable artefacts.

# 5. Organise

## Objective

Connect captured knowledge into a coherent model of the business.

Capture creates individual facts.

Organise establishes relationships between them.

For example:

```text
Stakeholder
    │
    ▼
Makes Decision
    │
    ▼
Requires Information
    │
    ▼
Produced By Process
    │
    ▼
Enabled By Capability
    │
    ▼

```

This stage transforms documentation into **connected business understanding**.

# 6. Validate

## Objective

Confirm that the emerging understanding accurately represents the business.

Discovery findings should not become recommendations simply because they were written down.

They must be validated.

Validation involves returning findings to stakeholders and asking:

```text
Is this accurate?

What have we misunderstood?

What is missing?

What is assumption rather than fact?

Do we agree on the problem?
```

IIBA similarly distinguishes between gathering stakeholder information and confirming elicitation results to ensure shared understanding (IIBA, n.d.a).

---

## Key Activities

Typical activities include:

- Stakeholder Playback
- Process Validation
- Definition Review
- KPI Validation
- Data Validation
- Ownership Confirmation
- Assumption Testing
- Evidence Review
- Challenge Sessions
- Cross-Domain Validation

---

## Key Questions

- Is this an accurate representation?
- What have we missed?
- Which findings are disputed?
- What evidence supports this?
- Are the definitions agreed?
- Is ownership correct?
- Are process exceptions represented?
- Are pain points genuine?
- Have all relevant stakeholders contributed?
- Are there conflicting perspectives?

---

## Typical Artefacts

- Workshop Playback
- Validated Process Maps
- Approved Definitions
- Validated KPI Catalogue
- Confirmed Ownership

- Validated Findings

# Validation Does Not Mean Forced Agreement

Different stakeholders may legitimately hold different perspectives.

For example:

```text
Sales Definition
      ≠
Finance Definition
```

Validation does not necessarily mean forcing one definition.

Instead it may establish:

```text
Both definitions are valid

BUT

They apply in different contexts.
```

That distinction should then be captured through:

- Business Glossary
- Domain Model
- Ontology
- Business Rules

The objective is clarity.

## Objective

Turn validated findings into evaluated opportunities.

Discovery will often produce more potential improvements than the organisation can realistically pursue.

The Evaluate stage determines which opportunities deserve investment.

- Is the data available?
- Is technology ready?

# 8. Roadmap

## Objective

Translate prioritised opportunities into a coherent and actionable sequence.

The Roadmap stage answers:

> **Given what we now understand, what should happen next?**

A roadmap should connect proposed activity back to the business problems and outcomes identified during discovery.

---

## Key Activities

Typical activities include:

- Sequence opportunities
- Identify dependencies
- Identify foundational work
- Define horizons
- Identify quick wins
- Identify strategic investments
- Define outcomes
- Define success measures
- Identify owners
- Establish governance
- Identify next discovery activities

---

## Key Questions

- What should happen first?
- What depends upon what?
- Which foundations are required?
- What can deliver value quickly?
- What requires longer-term investment?
- Which capabilities must mature first?
- Who owns each outcome?
- How will success be measured?
- What requires further discovery?
- What decisions are required from leadership?

# DISCOVER and AI

DISCOVER applies the same principle to AI.

Do not begin with:

```text
Where can we use AI?
```

Begin with:

```text
What problem exists?

What outcome matters?

What activity or decision is difficult?

Why is it difficult?

Could the problem be solved without AI?

What role could AI appropriately play?
```

Then evaluate:

```text
Business Value

User Need

Data Readiness

Technical Feasibility

Risk

Human Oversight

Governance
```

AI should be one possible response to a validated business problem rather than the starting assumption.

# Evidence-Based Discovery

DISCOVER should distinguish between:

```text
FACT

Supported by evidence


ASSUMPTION

Believed to be true but not yet validated


OPINION

Stakeholder interpretation


HYPOTHESIS

Explanation that can be tested


FINDING

Conclusion supported by discovery evidence


OPPORTUNITY

Potential response to a validated problem
```

This distinction helps prevent assumptions becoming embedded as facts.

# Timeboxing Discovery

Discovery should have purpose and momentum.

GDS similarly recommends setting a goal for discovery and defines completion in terms of having enough understanding to decide what to do next rather than treating discovery as indefinite research (Government Digital Service, n.d.).

DISCOVER should therefore avoid:

```text
Analysis Paralysis
```

The question is not:

> Have we discovered everything?

The better question is:

> Do we understand enough to make the next important decision confidently?

---

# When Discovery is Complete

Discovery may be considered sufficiently complete when:

- The business problem is understood
- Desired outcomes are clear
- Relevant stakeholders have participated
- Major capabilities are understood
- Important processes have been explored
- Critical information is identified
- Major technology dependencies are understood
- Key terminology is defined
- Pain points have evidence
- Opportunities have been identified
- Opportunities have been evaluated
- Important assumptions are visible
- Risks and dependencies are understood
- Recommendations can be explained
- Next steps can be prioritised

Some uncertainty will remain.

That is expected.

The objective is **decision confidence**, not perfect knowledge.
