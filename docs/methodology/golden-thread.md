# The DISCOVER Golden Thread

## Purpose

The DISCOVER Golden Thread provides a traceable connection between **business strategy and measurable business value**.

It ensures that Data Products, Analytics, AI capabilities and technology investments can be explained in terms of the business need they exist to support.

The Golden Thread is:

```text
Business Strategy
        │
        ▼
Business Capability
        │
        ▼
Business Process
        │
        ▼
Business Decision
        │
        ▼
Business Information
        │
        ▼
Business Rules
        │
        ▼
Technology / Data
        │
        ▼
Data Products / Analytics
        │
        ▼
Insight / Action
        │
        ▼
Business Value
```

The principle is simple:

> **Every solution should be traceable back to a business need and forward to measurable value.**

Within DISCOVER, the Golden Thread prevents discovery from becoming a collection of disconnected artefacts.

It connects:

```text
Why the organisation cares

        │
        ▼

What the organisation must be able to do

        │
        ▼

How the business operates

        │
        ▼

What decisions people make

        │
        ▼

What information those decisions require

        │
        ▼

What rules give that information meaning

        │
        ▼

Where the underlying data comes from

        │
        ▼

What products turn data into something usable

        │
        ▼

What action is taken

        │
        ▼

What value is created
```

---

# Why the Golden Thread Matters

Organisations can accumulate large numbers of:

- Reports
- Dashboards
- KPIs
- Datasets
- Data Pipelines
- Applications
- AI Use Cases
- Data Products
- Technology Platforms

without maintaining a clear understanding of why each exists.

For example:

```text
Dashboard
   │
   ▼
Dataset
   │
   ▼
Pipeline
   │
   ▼
Source System
```

describes technical lineage.

It does not explain:

```text
Why does the dashboard exist?

Who uses it?

What decision does it support?

What business objective does the decision support?

What happens as a result?

What value does it create?
```

The DISCOVER Golden Thread extends traceability beyond technical lineage.

It seeks to establish **business lineage**.

# Traceability

Traceability is the ability to understand relationships and lineage between different elements.

IIBA describes traceability as identifying the lineage of requirements and designs backwards and forwards and using that traceability to maintain alignment throughout an initiative (IIBA, n.d.a).

Within DISCOVER, traceability extends across business, information, data, technology and value.

For example:

```text
Backward Traceability

Data Product
      │
      ▼
Information Need
      │
      ▼
Decision
      │
      ▼
Process
      │
      ▼
Capability
      │
      ▼
Strategy
```

and:

```text
Forward Traceability

Strategy
   │
   ▼
Capability
   │
   ▼
Process
   │
   ▼
Decision
   │
   ▼
Information
   │
   ▼
Data Product
   │
   ▼
Action
   │
   ▼
Value
```

A strong Golden Thread should support both directions.

# The Golden Thread Test

For any proposed Data Product, Analytics capability, AI solution or technology investment, ask:

```text
Can we trace this backwards
to a genuine business need?

AND

Can we trace it forwards
to an expected business outcome?
```

If the answer to either question is:

```text
No
```

then further discovery may be required.

# 1. Business Strategy

## Purpose

The Golden Thread begins with business strategy.

Strategy describes what the organisation is seeking to achieve and where it intends to focus.

Examples might include:

```text
Grow International Revenue

Improve Operating Margin

Increase Customer Retention

Reduce Cost

Improve Content Monetisation

Improve Forecast Accuracy

Accelerate Decision-Making

Improve Operational Efficiency
```

The Golden Thread does not require every initiative to link directly to a Board-level strategic objective.

However, significant investment should normally be explainable in terms of an organisational outcome or priority.

---

# Discovery Questions

Ask:

- What is the organisation trying to achieve?
- Which strategic objective does this work support?
- Why is this important now?
- What business outcome needs to change?
- How is success measured?
- What happens if nothing changes?
- Who owns the outcome?

---

# Example

```text
Strategic Objective

Grow International Content Revenue
```

This provides the starting point.

The next question becomes:

> **What must the organisation be able to do effectively to achieve that objective?**

---

# 2. Business Capability

## Purpose

Business Capabilities describe what the organisation must be able to do.

The Open Group includes Business Capabilities and Business Capability Planning as established Business Architecture perspectives alongside areas such as Value Streams and Information Mapping (The Open Group, n.d.).

Capabilities provide a relatively stable bridge between business strategy and operational activity.

For example:

```text
Strategic Objective

Grow International Content Revenue

        │
        ▼

Capabilities

Content Sales

Rights Management

Customer Management

Revenue Management

Market Intelligence
```

---

# Discovery Questions

Ask:

- Which capability supports the strategic outcome?
- How important is the capability?
- How well does it perform today?
- Who owns it?
- Which processes realise it?
- What information does it require?
- Which capability gaps are preventing the desired outcome?

---

# Example

```text
Strategy

Grow International Content Revenue

        │
        ▼

Capability

Rights Management
```

The next question becomes:

> **How is this capability actually delivered?**

# 4. Business Decision

## Purpose

Business Decisions are one of the most important elements of the Golden Thread.

A process contains activities.

But information becomes valuable primarily when it helps someone:

```text
Understand

Decide

Act
```

DISCOVER therefore places the decision at the centre of Data Product discovery.

Examples:

```text
Can we sell this programme in Germany?

Should we revise the revenue forecast?

Which campaign should receive additional budget?

Which customer opportunity should we prioritise?

Should this contract be approved?

Which production requires intervention?
```

---

# Decision Discovery

For every important decision identify:

```text
Decision Maker

Decision

Frequency

Trigger

Information Required

Business Rules

Possible Actions

Desired Outcome
```

---

# Discovery Questions

Ask:

- Who makes the decision?
- What exactly are they deciding?
- When is the decision made?
- What triggers it?
- How frequently is it made?
- What information is required?
- Which rules influence it?
- What happens after the decision?
- What happens if the decision is wrong?
- What happens if the information arrives too late?

---

# Example

```text
Strategy

Grow International Content Revenue

        │
        ▼

Capability

Rights Management

        │
        ▼

Process

Determine Rights Availability

        │
        ▼

in Germany during 2027?
```

This provides a much stronger starting point for Data & Analytics discovery than:

```text

```

# Information vs Data

DISCOVER distinguishes conceptually between:

```text
Business Information

What the user needs to know
```

and:

```text
Data

The recorded facts from which
that information can be derived
```

For example:

```text
BUSINESS INFORMATION

Rights Availability
```

may depend upon data from:

```text
Rights Contract

Territory

Licence Window

Exclusivity

Platform

Language

Existing Sale
```

This distinction prevents data availability from defining the business requirement.

---

# Discovery Questions

Ask:

- What does the decision maker need to know?
- What business concepts are involved?
- What level of detail is required?
- How current must the information be?
- What historical information is required?
- What contextual information is required?
- Which information is currently unavailable?
- Which information is not trusted?
- Who owns it?

# 6. Business Rules

## Purpose

Information does not have meaning in isolation.

Business Rules determine:

- How concepts are interpreted
- How metrics are calculated
- Which conditions apply
- Which exceptions are allowed
- What actions may occur

For example:

```text
Rights Availability
```

may depend upon rules such as:

```text
Licence period must not overlap
an existing exclusive licence.

Rights must include the requested territory.

Rights must include the requested platform.

Contract restrictions must be satisfied.

Language rights must be available.
```

Without these rules, the underlying data cannot reliably answer the business question.

---

# Rules Can Exist in Many Places

Business Rules may currently exist in:

- Contracts
- Policies
- Regulations
- Systems
- Code
- SQL
- Spreadsheets
- Process Documents
- People's Knowledge

An important DISCOVER objective is to make critical rules visible.

---

# Discovery Questions

Ask:

- What determines the answer?
- Which rules are applied?
- Who owns those rules?
- Where are the rules documented?
- Are they implemented consistently?
- Do rules vary by domain?
- Do they vary by territory?
- Are exceptions allowed?
- Who approves exceptions?
- Are rules encoded within systems?

---

# Example

```text
Decision

Can Programme X be licensed
in Germany during 2027?

        ▼

        │
        ▼

Existing exclusivity must be respected
```

The next question becomes:

> **Which underlying data and technology allow us to evaluate these rules?**

# Do Not Allow Systems to Redefine the Business

The relationship should be:

```text

      │
      ▼

```

```text

      │
      ▼

      │
      ▼

```

The business model should remain independent of any particular technology implementation.

# 9. Insight / Action

## Purpose

Data does not create value simply by existing.

Reports do not create value simply by being viewed.

Analytics becomes valuable when it changes understanding, decisions or behaviour.

The Golden Thread therefore explicitly connects Data Products to:

```text
Insight

Decision

Action
```

---

# Insight

An insight represents something useful learned from the information.

For example:

```text
Rights are available for Germany
from January 2027.
```

or:

```text
Forecast revenue is 12% below target
in three territories.
```

---

# Action

An action is what happens as a result.

For example:

```text
Sales contacts German distributors.
```

or:

```text
Commercial leadership reviews
the underperforming territories.
```

---

# Discovery Questions

Ask:

- What will the user do differently?
- What action does this information enable?
- Who takes the action?
- How quickly must they act?
- Is the action currently possible?
- What prevents action today?
- Can the action be measured?
- What happens if no action is taken?

---

# The "So What?" Test

```text

        │
        ▼

```

Continue until the business value becomes clear.

# Why Decisions Sit at the Centre

One of the distinguishing features of the DISCOVER Golden Thread is the emphasis placed on the **Business Decision**.

Consider two approaches.

## Data-Led

```text
Dataset
    │
    ▼
Dashboard
    │
    ▼
Hope Someone Finds It Useful
```

## Decision-Led

```text
Business Outcome
    │
    ▼
Decision
    │
    ▼
Information Required
    │
    ▼
Data Required
    │
    ▼
Data Product
```

The second approach creates a clearer relationship between analytical investment and business value.

This supports the DISCOVER principle:

> **Decisions drive data.**

---

# Not Every Data Product Supports One Decision

Real business relationships may be many-to-many.

For example:

```text
Revenue Performance Data Product
        │
        ├── Supports Forecast Review
        ├── Supports Territory Review
        ├── Supports Customer Review
        └── Supports Executive Reporting
```

Similarly:

```text
Forecast Review
        │
        ├── Revenue Data Product
        ├── Sales Pipeline Data Product
        └── Market Intelligence Data Product
```

The Golden Thread does not require an artificial one-to-one relationship.

It makes the real relationships visible.

Without the Golden Thread, an organisation might attempt to build the AI capability first and discover the missing foundations later.

# Building the Golden Thread

A practical approach is to work from both directions.

## Top-Down

Start with strategy.

```text
Strategy
   │
   ▼
Capability
   │
   ▼
Process
   │
   ▼
Decision
   │
   ▼
Information
```

This helps identify what Data & Analytics capabilities the business genuinely requires.

---

# Bottom-Up

Existing technology can also be examined backwards.

For example:

```text
Dashboard
   │
   ▼
Which Decision?
   │
   ▼
Which Process?
   │
   ▼
Which Capability?
   │
   ▼
Which Outcome?
```

This can be particularly useful during report or technology rationalisation.

---

# Example – Dashboard Rationalisation

Suppose an organisation has:

```text
250 Dashboards
```

For each dashboard ask:

```text
Who uses it?

What decision does it support?

Which KPI does it contain?

Which process uses it?

Which capability does it support?

Which outcome does it contribute towards?
```

A dashboard with no identifiable consumer, decision or outcome may warrant further investigation.

The result may identify:

```text
Duplicate Dashboards

Unused Dashboards

Conflicting KPIs

Unclear Ownership

Duplicated Data Products

Missing Decision Support
```

The Golden Thread therefore provides a useful framework for rationalisation.

# Validation

The Golden Thread should be validated with the stakeholders represented within it.

For example:

```text

Is this the correct capability?


Process Owner

Does this represent how work happens?


Decision Maker

Is this really the decision you make?


Data Owner

Does this information come from these sources?


Product Owner

Does the product support this need?


Business Owner

Would this action create the expected value?
```

IIBA's validation guidance similarly emphasises ensuring that requirements and designs align to business requirements and support delivery of needed value (IIBA, n.d.b).

# Broken Threads

A **Broken Thread** exists where an important relationship cannot be established.

For example:

```text
Strategic Objective
        │
        ▼
Capability
        │
        ▼
Process
        │
        X
        │
        ▼
Unknown Decision
```

or:

```text
Data Product
        │
        ▼
Insight
        │
        X
        │
        ▼
No Defined Action
```

Broken Threads are not necessarily failures.

They are useful discovery findings because they expose:

- Missing requirements
- Missing ownership
- Poorly understood value
- Unused information
- Weak product definitions
- Unclear strategy
- Incomplete business knowledge

# What Good Looks Like

A strong Golden Thread should allow a practitioner to answer:

- Why does this matter?
- Which strategic objective does it support?
- Which capability is involved?
- Which process is affected?
- Who makes the decision?
- What decision are they making?
- What information do they require?
- Which rules define that information?
- Where does the underlying data originate?
- Which product provides the information?
- What insight is generated?
- What action occurs?
- What value is created?
- How will that value be measured?
- Who owns each important element?

If those questions can be answered, there is strong traceability from business need to business value.

# Golden Thread Checklist

Before recommending a significant Data, Analytics, AI or Technology investment confirm:

- [ ] Business objective identified
- [ ] Business capability identified
- [ ] Relevant process understood
- [ ] Decision maker identified
- [ ] Business decision understood
- [ ] Information requirements defined
- [ ] Business rules captured
- [ ] Data sources identified
- [ ] Ownership understood
- [ ] Data Product or solution defined
- [ ] Expected insight understood
- [ ] Business action identified
- [ ] Business value articulated
- [ ] Success measure identified
- [ ] Dependencies understood
- [ ] Stakeholders have validated the thread

A large number of unanswered items suggests that more discovery may be required.

# The Golden Thread in One Question

At its simplest, the DISCOVER Golden Thread asks:

> **Can we explain how this investment helps someone make a better decision or take a better action that contributes to a meaningful business outcome?**

If we cannot, we should challenge why the investment exists.
