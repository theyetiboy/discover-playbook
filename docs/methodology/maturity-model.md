# DISCOVER Maturity Model

## Purpose

The DISCOVER Maturity Model provides a structured way to assess the current and desired maturity of a business capability, domain, process, Data Product or organisational area.

It helps answer:

```text
Where are we today?

Where do we need to be?

Where are the important gaps?

Which gaps matter most?

What should improve first?

What investment is required?
```

The purpose of the model is not to award an organisation a maturity score.

Its purpose is to support **discovery, prioritisation and improvement**.

Within DISCOVER, maturity is assessed through the six POPIT-V dimensions:

```text
People

Organisation

Process

Information

Technology

Value
```

This prevents maturity assessments from becoming narrowly focused on technology.

---

# Why Assess Maturity?

Discovery frequently reveals statements such as:

```text
"Our data is immature."

"Our reporting process is very manual."

"We need better governance."

"We aren't ready for AI."

"Our technology is old."

"Our processes aren't standardised."
```

These statements may contain useful insight but are difficult to act upon without further analysis.

A maturity assessment provides a more structured view.

For example:

```text
Capability

Revenue Management


People        3

Organisation  2

Process       2

Information   1

Technology    3

Value         3
```

This immediately suggests that:

```text
Technology may not be the primary constraint.

Information maturity may require attention first.
```

This is exactly the type of insight the DISCOVER Maturity Model is intended to expose.

---

# Maturity is Multi-Dimensional

An organisation should not be described simply as:

```text
"Maturity Level 3"
```

without explaining what that means.

Different dimensions may operate at very different levels of maturity.

For example:

```text
People
████████░░ 4

Organisation
██████░░░░ 3

Process
████░░░░░░ 2

Information
██░░░░░░░░ 1

Technology
████████░░ 4

Value
██████░░░░ 3
```

This provides a far richer picture than a single overall number.

The UK Government Data Maturity Assessment similarly uses multiple themes and topics rather than treating data maturity as a single technical measure (Central Digital and Data Office, 2023).

---

# The Five DISCOVER Maturity Levels

DISCOVER uses five maturity levels.

```text
Level 1    Fragmented

Level 2    Developing

Level 3    Defined

Level 4    Managed

Level 5    Adaptive
```

These levels describe a progression from fragmented and reactive capability towards capability that is understood, governed, measured and able to adapt as business needs change.

The DISCOVER levels are an original assessment structure.

They are informed by the wider concept of staged maturity and capability assessment used within frameworks such as CMMI and ISO/IEC 33020.

---

# Level 1 – Fragmented

## Characteristics

At Level 1, capability exists but is largely dependent upon individuals, local practices and disconnected tools.

Typical characteristics include:

```text
Reactive

Inconsistent

Manual

Poorly Documented

Siloed

Person Dependent

Limited Governance

Limited Measurement
```

Processes may work because experienced people know how to make them work.

Knowledge frequently exists in:

```text
People's heads

Spreadsheets

Emails

Local documents

Personal workarounds
```

rather than within governed organisational structures.

---

## Typical Evidence

Examples might include:

- No clearly identified capability owner
- Different teams use different processes
- Important definitions are disputed
- Significant spreadsheet dependency
- Manual reconciliation
- Knowledge concentrated in a few individuals
- Limited documentation
- Data quality problems are discovered reactively
- Multiple versions of reports exist
- Technology is fragmented
- Success is difficult to measure

---

## Typical Risk

```text
Key-Person Dependency

Operational Failure

Inconsistent Decisions

Poor Data Quality

Slow Processes

Low Trust

Limited Scalability
```

---

# Level 2 – Developing

## Characteristics

At Level 2, the organisation recognises the need for improvement and some more consistent practices are emerging.

Typical characteristics include:

```text
Partially Defined

Locally Standardised

Some Ownership

Some Governance

Some Automation

Growing Awareness

Improvement Initiatives Underway
```

Different teams may have developed good practices, but those practices are not yet consistently adopted.

---

## Typical Evidence

Examples might include:

- Some process documentation exists
- Local owners have been identified
- Important KPIs have definitions
- Initial Data Products exist
- Some workflows are automated
- Data quality is monitored in selected areas
- Business terminology is beginning to be documented
- Teams are attempting to standardise working practices
- Technology consolidation has begun
- Improvement programmes exist

---

## Typical Risk

```text
Local Optimisation

Duplicated Solutions

Inconsistent Adoption

Unclear Enterprise Standards

Fragmented Governance
```

---

# Level 3 – Defined

## Characteristics

At Level 3, important organisational practices have been deliberately defined, documented and adopted.

Typical characteristics include:

```text
Standardised

Documented

Governed

Owned

Repeatable

Integrated

Understood
```

The capability is no longer primarily dependent upon individual knowledge.

---

## Typical Evidence

Examples might include:

- Clear capability ownership
- Defined end-to-end processes
- Agreed Business Glossary
- Defined KPI Catalogue
- Defined business rules
- Data ownership established
- Standard Data Products available
- Technology architecture documented
- Governance forums established
- Roles and responsibilities understood
- Standard ways of working adopted

---

## Typical Risk

The capability may be consistent but still lack sufficient performance management.

For example:

```text
"We have a defined process,
but we do not know how well
it performs."
```

The next stage therefore focuses increasingly on measurement and optimisation.

---

# Level 4 – Managed

## Characteristics

At Level 4, the capability is not merely defined.

It is actively measured and managed.

Typical characteristics include:

```text
Measured

Monitored

Data Driven

Proactive

Predictable

Quality Managed

Performance Focused
```

Management decisions are increasingly based upon reliable evidence.

---

## Typical Evidence

Examples might include:

- Process performance is measured
- Data quality has defined measures
- Data Products have service expectations
- KPI performance is monitored
- Ownership is actively exercised
- Benefits are measured
- Exceptions are monitored
- Automated controls exist
- Performance trends are analysed
- Root causes are investigated systematically
- Improvement activity is prioritised using evidence

---

## Typical Questions

At this stage organisations increasingly ask:

```text
How well are we performing?

Why is performance changing?

Where should we intervene?

What can we predict?

Where can we optimise?
```

---

# Level 5 – Adaptive

## Characteristics

At Level 5, the capability is able to continuously adapt as business needs, technology and external conditions change.

Typical characteristics include:

```text
Adaptive

Continuous Improvement

Predictive

Highly Integrated

Reusable

Self-Service

Optimised

Innovation Enabled
```

The organisation can improve the capability without repeatedly rebuilding its foundations.

---

## Typical Evidence

Examples might include:

- Continuous improvement is embedded
- Data Products are reusable across domains
- Business knowledge is machine-readable where valuable
- Predictive insight supports decisions
- Processes can adapt to changing conditions
- Automation is used appropriately
- AI augments suitable business activities
- Performance feedback drives improvement
- Technology components can evolve independently
- Business rules and semantics are governed centrally or through clear domain ownership
- New use cases can reuse existing data and knowledge foundations

---

## Adaptive Does Not Mean Maximum Technology

Level 5 should not be interpreted as:

```text
Everything Automated

Everything Uses AI

Newest Technology Everywhere
```

A highly mature organisation may deliberately retain manual activity where:

```text
Human Judgement Matters

Risk Requires Oversight

Automation Provides Little Value

The Activity Occurs Infrequently
```

Maturity means making **appropriate and informed choices**.

---

# Maturity Is Not a Race to Level 5

Not every capability needs to achieve Level 5.

The appropriate target depends upon:

```text
Strategic Importance

Business Criticality

Risk

Regulation

Cost

Volume

Customer Impact

Competitive Differentiation
```

For example:

```text
Critical Revenue Management Capability
                │
                ▼
Target Maturity = 4 or 5
```

while:

```text
Low-Volume Internal Administrative Process
                │
                ▼
Target Maturity = 2 or 3
```

may be entirely appropriate.

The objective is:

> **Fit-for-purpose maturity.**

Not maximum maturity.

---

# Assessment Scope

Before conducting a maturity assessment, clearly define what is being assessed.

Possible scopes include:

```text
Business Domain

Business Capability

Business Process

Data Capability

Data Product

Reporting Capability

Technology Capability

AI Capability
```

Avoid assessing:

```text
"The whole company"
```

at an excessively abstract level unless that is genuinely useful.

Smaller scopes generally produce more actionable findings.

# Assessment Example

```text
Scope

Revenue Forecasting Capability


Business Area

Finance


Business Objective

Improve forecast accuracy and
reduce manual reporting effort.
```

The assessment can then examine Revenue Forecasting across POPIT-V.

# The POPIT-V Maturity Assessment

DISCOVER assesses maturity through:

```text
                    CAPABILITY

                        │
           ┌────────────┼────────────┐
           │            │            │
        People     Organisation    Process
           │            │            │
           └────────────┼────────────┘
                        │
                   Information
                        │
                   Technology
                        │
                      Value
```

Each dimension is scored independently.

# People Maturity

## Purpose

Assess whether the organisation has the people, skills, knowledge and responsibilities required to perform the capability effectively.

---

## Level 1 – Fragmented

```text
Knowledge concentrated in individuals.

Responsibilities unclear.

Significant key-person dependency.

Training largely informal.
```

---

## Level 2 – Developing

```text
Important roles emerging.

Some skills development.

Responsibilities partially understood.

Communities of practice may exist.
```

---

## Level 3 – Defined

```text
Roles documented.

Responsibilities understood.

Required skills identified.

Training and knowledge transfer established.
```

---

## Level 4 – Managed

```text
Skills actively assessed.

Capacity and performance measured.

Succession and knowledge risks managed.

Workforce planning aligned to capability needs.
```

---

## Level 5 – Adaptive

```text
Skills evolve with business need.

Knowledge is widely reusable.

Cross-functional collaboration is embedded.

People increasingly focus on higher-value activity.
```

---

# People Discovery Questions

Ask:

- Who performs the work?
- Who owns the outcome?
- Are responsibilities clear?
- Is specialist knowledge concentrated?
- Are the required skills available?
- What happens when experienced people leave?
- Is training available?
- Can people access the knowledge they need?
- Are roles evolving with the capability?

---

# Organisation Maturity

## Purpose

Assess whether strategy, ownership, governance and organisational structures effectively support the capability.

---

## Level 1 – Fragmented

```text
Ownership unclear.

Teams operate independently.

Governance largely reactive.

Priorities frequently conflict.
```

---

## Level 2 – Developing

```text
Local owners exist.

Some governance structures exist.

Cross-team collaboration is emerging.

Strategic alignment is partially understood.
```

---

## Level 3 – Defined

```text
Capability ownership established.

Governance documented.

Decision authority clear.

Strategic alignment understood.
```

---

## Level 4 – Managed

```text
Governance effectiveness measured.

Investment aligned to performance.

Cross-domain dependencies actively managed.

Accountability operates in practice.
```

---

## Level 5 – Adaptive

```text
Operating model evolves with strategy.

Decision authority is clear and responsive.

Governance enables rather than obstructs change.

Capabilities can respond quickly to new needs.
```

---

# Organisation Discovery Questions

Ask:

- Who owns the capability?
- Is accountability clear?
- Which strategic objectives does it support?
- Which governance forums exist?
- Are decisions made at the appropriate level?
- Do organisational boundaries create problems?
- Are priorities aligned?
- Are dependencies actively managed?

# Process Maturity

## Purpose

Assess how consistently, efficiently and effectively work is performed.

---

## Level 1 – Fragmented

```text
Processes vary by individual.

Manual workarounds common.

Exceptions dominate.

Documentation limited.
```

---

## Level 2 – Developing

```text
Basic processes documented.

Local standards emerging.

Some automation.

Common problems recognised.
```

---

## Level 3 – Defined

```text
End-to-end processes understood.

Roles and decisions clear.

Business rules documented.

Standard processes adopted.
```

---

## Level 4 – Managed

```text
Process performance measured.

Bottlenecks monitored.

Automation used appropriately.

Exceptions and root causes actively managed.
```

---

## Level 5 – Adaptive

```text
Processes continuously improve.

Feedback informs change.

Automation can respond to context.

Processes adapt as customer and business needs evolve.
```

---

# Process Discovery Questions

Ask:

- Is the process documented?
- Is it consistently followed?
- How much variation exists?
- Where is manual work required?
- Where does rework occur?
- Are process measures available?
- Are bottlenecks understood?
- Is continuous improvement established?


## Level 2 – Developing

```text

Some Data Owners identified.

Initial quality monitoring.

Local Data Products emerging.
```

---

## Level 3 – Defined

```text
Business Glossary established.

Critical data ownership defined.

Data Products have defined semantics.
```

```text
Data quality measured.

```

---

```text
Semantic relationships are explicitly modelled.
```

# Information Discovery Questions

Ask:

- Are key business terms defined?
- Are KPI definitions agreed?
- Who owns the information?
- Is data trusted?
- Is quality measured?
- Is lineage understood?
- Can people find the information they need?
- Are business rules captured?
- Can information be reused across domains?
- Is the information suitable for AI where required?

Ask:

# Value Maturity

## Purpose

Assess how effectively the organisation understands, measures and manages the value created by the capability.

---

## Level 1 – Fragmented

```text
Benefits unclear.

Success largely subjective.

Activity measured instead of outcomes.

Investment cases weak.
```

---

## Level 2 – Developing

```text
Desired outcomes identified.

Some KPIs exist.

Benefits discussed but not consistently measured.

Basic prioritisation used.
```

---

## Level 3 – Defined

```text
Outcomes defined.

Success measures agreed.

Business cases connect investment to value.

Prioritisation uses consistent criteria.
```

---

## Level 4 – Managed

```text
Benefits actively measured.

Investment decisions use performance evidence.

Products are assessed against outcomes.

Benefits ownership established.
```

---

## Level 5 – Adaptive

```text
Investment continuously responds to evidence.

Low-value activity can be stopped quickly.

Benefits feedback shapes the roadmap.

Resources move towards higher-value opportunities.
```

# Value Discovery Questions

Ask:

- What value does the capability create?
- Who receives that value?
- How is success measured?
- Are outcomes clearly defined?
- Are benefits tracked?
- Who owns the benefit?
- Does performance influence investment?
- Can low-value activity be stopped?

# The DISCOVER Maturity Matrix

A summary view:

| Level | People | Organisation | Process | Information | Technology | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Fragmented** | Individual dependency | Unclear ownership | Inconsistent | Fragmented | Disconnected | Unclear |
| **2 Developing** | Skills emerging | Local ownership | Partially defined | Initial governance | Improving | Outcomes emerging |
| **3 Defined** | Roles established | Governed | Standardised | Defined & owned | Standard architecture | Measures defined |
| **4 Managed** | Skills managed | Performance governed | Measured | Quality managed | Monitored | Benefits measured |
| **5 Adaptive** | Continuously evolving | Responsive | Continuously improving | Reusable & semantic | Adaptable | Value optimised |

The matrix is intended as a discovery aid.

Detailed evidence should always sit behind the score.

# Scoring

Each POPIT-V dimension can be scored from:

```text
1 = Fragmented

2 = Developing

3 = Defined

4 = Managed

5 = Adaptive
```

For example:

| Dimension | Current | Target |
|---|---:|---:|
| People | 3 | 4 |
| Organisation | 2 | 4 |
| Process | 2 | 4 |
| Information | 1 | 4 |
| Technology | 3 | 4 |
| Value | 2 | 4 |

# Do Not Average Blindly

It is technically possible to calculate:

```text
Average Maturity Score
```

but this can hide important differences.

For example:

```text
People        5

Organisation  4

Process       4

Information   1

Technology    5

Value         5
```

produces a reasonably high average.

But:

```text
Information = 1
```

may completely constrain the capability.

DISCOVER therefore prioritises the **maturity profile** over a single aggregate score.

---

# Current vs Target Maturity

Maturity assessment becomes more useful when current state is compared with the level actually required.

For example:

| Dimension | Current | Target | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| People | 3 | 4 | 1 |
| Organisation | 2 | 3 | 1 |
| Process | 2 | 4 | 2 |
| Information | 1 | 4 | 3 |
| Technology | 3 | 4 | 1 |
| Value | 2 | 4 | 2 |

This immediately identifies:

```text
Largest Gap

Information
```

But gap size alone does not determine priority.

Strategic importance must also be considered.

# Maturity Gap Analysis

For each gap capture:

| Dimension | Current State | Target State | Gap | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

For example:

| Dimension | Current State | Target State | Gap | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Information | Revenue definitions vary | Agreed governed definitions | High | Create Revenue Glossary |
| Process | Reconciliation manual | Standard automated process | High | Redesign reconciliation |
| Organisation | Ownership unclear | Revenue Data Owner established | Medium | Define ownership |
| Technology | Data fragmented | Integrated Finance Data Product | High | Build Finance Data Product |

This starts turning assessment into an improvement plan.

# Strategic Importance vs Maturity

Maturity becomes especially useful when combined with strategic importance.

For example:

| Capability | Strategic Importance | Maturity |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue Management | High | Low |
| Financial Reporting | High | Medium |
| Expense Management | Low | High |
| Treasury | Medium | High |

A useful prioritisation hypothesis is:

```text
High Strategic Importance
          +
Low Current Maturity
          =
Potential Investment Priority
```

This is a prompt for investigation rather than an automatic investment rule.

# Maturity and Prioritisation

A low maturity score does not automatically mean:

```text
Fix immediately.
```

Prioritisation should consider:

```text
Strategic Importance

Business Value

Risk

Customer Impact

Regulation

Dependency

Maturity Gap

Cost

Effort
```

For example:

```text
Capability A

Maturity = 1
Strategic Importance = Low


Capability B

Maturity = 2
Strategic Importance = Critical
```

Capability B may be the better investment priority.

# Maturity and Roadmapping

The gap between current and target maturity can help define roadmap outcomes.

For example:

```text
CURRENT

Information Maturity = 1

        │
        ▼

TARGET

Information Maturity = 4
```

The journey might require:

```text
NOW

Agree terminology
Identify Data Owners
Define critical KPIs

        │
        ▼

NEXT

Create governed Data Products
Introduce Data Quality Measures
Establish lineage

        │
        ▼

LATER

Create semantic relationships
Enable self-service
Support advanced analytics and AI
```

The roadmap should describe meaningful capability outcomes rather than simply:

```text
Move from Level 1 to Level 4.
```

# Maturity Dependencies

Different dimensions often depend upon one another.

For example:

```text
AI Capability
      │
      ▼
Requires Trusted Information
      │
      ▼
Requires Data Governance
      │
      ▼
Requires Clear Ownership
      │
      ▼
Requires Organisational Accountability
```

This means:

```text
Technology Maturity = 5
```

cannot necessarily compensate for:

```text
Information Maturity = 1
```

The maturity profile should therefore be analysed as a system.

# AI Readiness

The DISCOVER Maturity Model can provide useful context when assessing AI opportunities.

For example:

```text
People        2

Organisation  1

Process       2

Information   1

Technology    4

Value         1
```

may indicate:

```text
Technically possible

BUT

Organisationally unready
```

In this situation, the most valuable next step may not be implementing AI.

It may be improving:

```text
Business Definitions

Information Quality

Ownership

Process Understanding

Use-Case Value
```

---

# Data Readiness

The same principle applies to Data Products.

A sophisticated Data Platform does not automatically mean the organisation has mature Data Products.

Consider:

```text
Technology    4

Information   1
```

This could indicate:

```text
Modern Platform

+

Poor Definitions

+

Unclear Ownership

+

Low Trust
```

The response should therefore address the missing Information and Organisation foundations rather than simply investing further in platform technology.

This sequence shows why immediately introducing AI may not be the highest-value first step.

# Conducting a Maturity Workshop

A DISCOVER maturity workshop might follow this structure.

| Stage | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm assessment scope |
| 2 | Explain maturity model |
| 3 | Assess People |
| 4 | Assess Organisation |
| 5 | Assess Process |
| 6 | Assess Information |
| 7 | Assess Technology |
| 8 | Assess Value |
| 9 | Capture evidence |
| 10 | Agree confidence |
| 11 | Define target maturity |
| 12 | Identify gaps |
| 13 | Identify dependencies |
| 14 | Identify improvement opportunities |

Avoid spending excessive time debating whether something is:

```text
2.0

or

2.5
```

The objective is understanding, not mathematical precision.

---

# Individual Assessment Before Workshop

For larger groups, participants may score the capability independently before discussion.

For example:

| Dimension | Finance | Data | Technology | Product |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| People | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| Organisation | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 |
| Process | 2 | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| Information | 1 | 2 | 3 | 2 |
| Technology | 2 | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| Value | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 |

The differences are often more interesting than the eventual agreed score.

For example:

```text
Finance believes Information maturity = 1

Technology believes Information maturity = 3
```

This should trigger:

> **Why do we see this differently?**

The conversation may reveal important assumptions or communication gaps.

---

# Assessment Scale

Where useful, use the following response options:

```text
1 – Strong evidence of Fragmented maturity

2 – Strong evidence of Developing maturity

3 – Strong evidence of Defined maturity

4 – Strong evidence of Managed maturity

5 – Strong evidence of Adaptive maturity

? – Insufficient evidence
```

Do not force a score where evidence is insufficient.

```text
Unknown
```

is a valid discovery result.

# Reassessment

Maturity should be reassessed periodically where improvement is being actively managed.

For example:

```text
Initial Assessment

        │
        ▼

Improvement Roadmap

        │
        ▼

Delivery

        │
        ▼

Measure Outcomes

        │
        ▼

Reassess

        │
        ▼

Adjust Roadmap
```

The purpose is to determine whether meaningful capability improvement has occurred.

---

# Measure Outcomes, Not Scores

An organisation should not celebrate:

```text
"We moved from Level 2 to Level 3."
```

without understanding what changed.

Prefer:

```text
Revenue definitions are now governed.

Manual reconciliation reduced by 60%.

Finance reporting reduced from five days to two.

Forecast accuracy improved.

Data ownership established.
```

The maturity score summarises the change.

The business outcomes demonstrate the value.

# Maturity Anti-Patterns

## Score Chasing

```text
"We need every capability at Level 5."
```

Challenge:

> Why?

---

## Technology Bias

```text
"We have modern cloud technology,
therefore we are mature."
```

Technology is only one dimension.

---

## False Precision

```text
"Maturity = 3.72"
```

The model is an assessment framework, not a precision measurement instrument.

---

## Assessment Without Evidence

```text
"We think we're a 4."
```

Ask:

> What evidence demonstrates that?

---

## Assessment Without Action

```text
Assessment Completed

        │
        ▼

Report Produced

        │
        ▼

Nothing Changes
```

Maturity assessment should inform improvement.

---

## Comparing Teams Without Context

Avoid using maturity scores as simplistic league tables.

Different capabilities:

```text
Have Different Purposes

Carry Different Risks

Require Different Target Maturity
```

---

## Treating Maturity as Performance

A capability may be:

```text
Mature but underperforming
```

or:

```text
Immature but currently achieving good results
through exceptional individuals.
```

Maturity and performance are related but distinct.

# What Good Looks Like

An effective maturity assessment should:

- Have clear scope
- Assess all relevant POPIT-V dimensions
- Use evidence
- Identify uncertainty
- Avoid false precision
- Define appropriate target maturity
- Identify meaningful gaps
- Consider strategic importance
- Identify dependencies
- Generate improvement opportunities
- Support prioritisation
- Inform the roadmap
- Be reassessed when useful
- Focus on outcomes rather than scores

# Recommended Maturity Questions – Quick Reference

Before concluding a maturity assessment, ensure you can answer:

- What exactly are we assessing?
- Why does this capability matter?
- What is its strategic importance?
- What evidence do we have?
- What is the People maturity?
- What is the Organisation maturity?
- What is the Process maturity?
- What is the Information maturity?
- What is the Technology maturity?
- What is the Value maturity?
- Where is our evidence weak?
- What maturity is actually required?
- Where are the largest gaps?
- Which gaps constrain other dimensions?
- Which gaps create business risk?
- Which improvements create the greatest value?
- What should happen first?
- Who owns the improvement?
- How will we know the capability has improved?
