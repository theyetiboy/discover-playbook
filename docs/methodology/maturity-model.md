# DISCOVER Maturity Model

The DISCOVER maturity model provides a way to assess how consistently a business domain operates across the six POPIT-V dimensions.

It is intended to support discovery and prioritisation, not to produce a vanity score.

---

# Five Levels

## 1 — Fragmented

Practices are largely local, undocumented or person-dependent.

Typical characteristics:

- inconsistent ways of working;
- unclear ownership;
- important knowledge held by individuals;
- manual workarounds;
- conflicting definitions;
- limited measurement;
- reactive technology changes;
- value rarely tracked.

## 2 — Developing

The organisation recognises the need for consistency and has begun to establish common approaches.

Typical characteristics:

- some documented processes;
- emerging ownership;
- local standards;
- partial data definitions;
- recurring reporting;
- pockets of automation;
- early governance;
- benefits discussed but not consistently measured.

## 3 — Defined

Common practices, definitions and ownership are documented and used across the relevant domain.

Typical characteristics:

- defined capabilities and responsibilities;
- repeatable processes;
- agreed concepts and measures;
- known authoritative sources;
- documented rules;
- governed technology patterns;
- benefits and KPIs linked to initiatives.

## 4 — Managed

The organisation actively measures performance, quality and outcomes and uses evidence to manage the domain.

Typical characteristics:

- operational performance is measured;
- data quality is monitored;
- decisions are supported by trusted information;
- exceptions are visible;
- ownership and controls are actively exercised;
- technology performance is monitored;
- benefits realisation is reviewed.

## 5 — Adaptive

The organisation continually improves using feedback, evidence and changing business needs.

Typical characteristics:

- capabilities evolve intentionally;
- processes are optimised based on evidence;
- data products are managed as products;
- automation and AI are introduced where justified;
- governance adapts to risk and context;
- learning loops are embedded;
- value is continuously reassessed.

---

# Assess Each POPIT-V Dimension Separately

Do **not** reduce maturity to one averaged score unless there is a specific and justified reason.

A domain might be:

| Dimension | Example Level |
|---|---|
| People | Developing |
| Organisation | Defined |
| Process | Fragmented |
| Information | Developing |
| Technology | Managed |
| Value | Fragmented |

That profile is more informative than saying the organisation is simply "Level 2.7".

---

# Dimension Guidance

## People

Assess:

- role clarity;
- skills;
- knowledge resilience;
- decision rights;
- adoption;
- ability to use data and insight.

## Organisation

Assess:

- capability ownership;
- governance;
- operating model clarity;
- cross-functional coordination;
- accountability.

## Process

Assess:

- repeatability;
- documentation;
- controls;
- exception handling;
- efficiency;
- measurement;
- continuous improvement.

## Information

Assess:

- definitions;
- ownership;
- quality;
- lineage;
- metadata;
- accessibility;
- business rules;
- trust.

## Technology

Assess:

- architecture;
- integration;
- resilience;
- maintainability;
- observability;
- automation;
- technical debt;
- fitness for purpose.

## Value

Assess:

- outcome clarity;
- KPI alignment;
- benefits definition;
- prioritisation discipline;
- benefits measurement;
- feedback from realised value into future investment.

---

# Evidence Standard

Every maturity assessment should include:

- the proposed level;
- evidence supporting the level;
- gaps or counter-evidence;
- confidence;
- what would need to be true to reach the next level.

Use evidence from supplied documents, observed practice and validated stakeholder input.

Avoid scoring based on reputation or intuition alone.

---

# How to Use the Model

1. Define the scope being assessed.
2. Collect evidence across the relevant POPIT-V dimensions.
3. Propose a level for each dimension.
4. Record supporting and conflicting evidence.
5. Validate the assessment with stakeholders.
6. Identify gaps with the greatest impact on business outcomes.
7. Use the profile to inform opportunities and roadmap decisions.

The maturity model is diagnostic. Higher maturity is not automatically the goal. The appropriate level depends on business need, risk, scale and value.
