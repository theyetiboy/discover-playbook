# Stakeholder Mapping

> *"Discovery quality is constrained by whose knowledge you include—and whose you miss."*

## Purpose

Stakeholder Mapping identifies the people, roles, teams, partners and governance groups who influence, perform, own, consume or are affected by the business domain under discovery.

The technique is not simply about communication planning. It is a way of finding the knowledge and decision authority needed to build a defensible understanding of the business.

## Why This Matters

Weak discovery often interviews only:

- the project sponsor;
- senior managers;
- technology owners;
- the people already known to the delivery team.

That can miss:

- operational experts;
- users who perform manual workarounds;
- data owners;
- control functions;
- downstream consumers;
- external partners;
- decision approvers;
- people who understand exceptions.

A stakeholder map helps identify **who knows**, **who decides**, **who owns**, and **who is affected**.

## How It Fits DISCOVER

Stakeholder Mapping is especially useful in:

- **Define** — identify sponsor, outcomes and initial stakeholders;
- **Investigate** — broaden the stakeholder landscape;
- **Study** — target interviews/workshops;
- **Validate** — identify the right validators;
- **Roadmap** — understand adoption and ownership.

## Stakeholder Categories

Potential categories include:

- sponsors;
- capability owners;
- process owners;
- decision makers;
- operational users;
- subject-matter experts;
- data owners/stewards;
- system owners;
- analysts;
- control/risk/legal/privacy teams;
- customers;
- suppliers;
- external partners;
- regulators;
- governance forums;
- change/adoption communities.

## Role vs Named Person

Use roles for durable discovery artefacts:

- Financial Controller;
- Rights Manager;
- Sales Director;
- Data Steward.

Use named individuals for immediate engagement planning where appropriate.

This prevents the playbook becoming obsolete when people move roles.

## Influence, Impact and Knowledge

A stakeholder can be important for different reasons.

Assess separately:

### Influence
Can they approve, block or redirect change?

### Impact
How strongly will the problem or change affect them?

### Knowledge
Do they understand the process, rules, data or exceptions?

### Ownership
Are they accountable for an outcome, definition or decision?

A low-seniority operational user may have far more domain knowledge than a high-influence executive.

## Suggested Workshop / Analysis Process

### 1. Start from the Outcome

Ask:

- Who owns the outcome?
- Who receives the value?
- Who is affected when it fails?

### 2. Walk the Capability and Process

For each area ask:

- Who performs the activity?
- Who decides?
- Who approves?
- Who creates information?
- Who consumes the result?

### 3. Add Governance and External Parties

Identify:

- steering forums;
- control teams;
- regulators;
- vendors;
- agencies;
- data/research suppliers;
- customers/partners.

### 4. Assess Engagement Need

Classify stakeholders by:

- influence;
- impact;
- knowledge;
- engagement approach;
- validation role.

### 5. Identify Gaps

Ask:

- Whose perspective is missing?
- Which role always appears late?
- Who owns the disputed definition?
- Who understands exceptions?
- Who could invalidate our assumptions?

## Discovery Questions

- Who is accountable for this outcome?
- Who performs the work?
- Who makes the key decisions?
- Who can approve an exception?
- Who owns the data or definition?
- Who consumes the report/data product?
- Who understands the historical context?
- Who will challenge this view?
- Which external parties matter?
- Which governance forum can resolve disagreement?
- Who would be affected by automation or AI?
- Who must validate the final model?

## Engagement Strategy

A stakeholder register might include:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Stakeholder / Role | Durable identification |
| Area / Capability | Business context |
| Influence | Ability to affect direction |
| Impact | Degree of change exposure |
| Knowledge | Domain expertise |
| Decision Rights | Authority |
| Interest / Concern | Motivation |
| Engagement Method | Interview, workshop, validation etc. |
| Evidence Needed | What we need to learn |
| Validation Role | What they can confirm |

## RACI Is Not Stakeholder Mapping

RACI can clarify responsibility for a defined activity, but it does not tell you:

- who has hidden knowledge;
- who influences decisions informally;
- who is impacted;
- who holds a conflicting view;
- who should be interviewed.

Use RACI as a supporting artefact, not a replacement.

## Finance Example

For revenue-recognition discovery, stakeholders might include:

- Finance Director;
- Financial Controller;
- Commercial Finance;
- FP&A;
- Sales Operations;
- Rights / Contract experts;
- Accounts Receivable;
- Data/Reporting analysts;
- Internal Audit;
- external audit contact where relevant;
- system owners.

A discovery based only on Finance leadership may miss the people performing reconciliation and manual adjustments.

## Marketing Example

Potential stakeholders:

- Marketing Director;
- campaign managers;
- media planners;
- creative;
- CRM/customer team;
- marketing analytics;
- Finance;
- agencies;
- platform specialists;
- Sales;
- brand teams;
- data/privacy specialists.

## Evidence and Validation

For each important stakeholder statement:

- capture the role;
- record the statement;
- classify it appropriately;
- seek corroborating evidence where material;
- identify other stakeholders who can confirm or challenge it.

Do not attribute sensitive comments unnecessarily in widely shared outputs.

## Common Pitfalls

- only mapping senior people;
- using the org chart as the stakeholder map;
- ignoring external parties;
- confusing influence with knowledge;
- failing to revisit the map as discovery expands;
- assuming the sponsor can validate operational detail;
- excluding sceptics;
- treating engagement as a one-off workshop invitation list.

## Outputs

- Stakeholder Register;
- Stakeholder Map;
- Influence / Impact / Knowledge Matrix;
- Interview Plan;
- Validation Plan;
- RACI where useful;
- Change/Adoption Stakeholder View.

## Practitioner Checklist

- [ ] Outcome owner is known.
- [ ] Operational users are represented.
- [ ] Decision owners are identified.
- [ ] Information/data owners are identified.
- [ ] Control and external stakeholders are considered.
- [ ] Knowledge and influence are assessed separately.
- [ ] Validation responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Missing perspectives are actively sought.
- [ ] Stakeholder map is revisited during discovery.

## Chapter Summary

Stakeholder Mapping ensures discovery is informed by the right perspectives and validated by the right owners. It is a knowledge-discovery technique as much as an engagement technique.
