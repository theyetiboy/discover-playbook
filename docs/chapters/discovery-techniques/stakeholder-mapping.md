# Stakeholder Mapping

## Purpose

Stakeholder Mapping is the process of identifying the people, groups and organisations that influence, participate in, depend upon or are affected by a business area, initiative, product or change.

Within DISCOVER, Stakeholder Mapping is used to understand:

- Who should participate in discovery
- Who holds important business knowledge
- Who makes decisions
- Who owns capabilities, processes and information
- Who consumes Data Products and analytics
- Who is affected by proposed change
- Who may influence adoption or delivery
- Where relationships and dependencies exist
- Where important perspectives may be missing

The International Institute of Business Analysis (IIBA) describes stakeholder lists and maps as techniques for identifying stakeholders and analysing their characteristics and relationships (IIBA, n.d.a).

Stakeholder Mapping is particularly useful during the **Define**, **Investigate**, **Study**, **Validate** and **Roadmap** stages of DISCOVER.

---

# Why This Technique Matters

Discovery rarely fails because nobody asked any questions.

It can fail because the **right people were not involved**.

Important business knowledge is often distributed across:

- Executives
- Business leaders
- Process owners
- Operational users
- Subject Matter Experts
- Data owners
- Analysts
- Technology teams
- Customers
- Suppliers
- External partners

Each stakeholder sees the organisation from a different perspective.

A Finance Director may understand:

```text
Business Outcomes
Financial Risk
Strategic Priorities
Governance
```

while an operational Finance Analyst may understand:

```text
Manual Workarounds
Spreadsheet Dependencies
Data Quality Problems
Process Exceptions
```

Both perspectives are important.

Stakeholder Mapping helps ensure discovery does not become dominated by whichever stakeholders are easiest to access or most senior.

---

# When to Use Stakeholder Mapping

Stakeholder Mapping should normally begin during engagement preparation and be maintained throughout discovery.

It is particularly useful when:

- Entering an unfamiliar business area
- Multiple departments are involved
- Ownership is unclear
- Decisions cross organisational boundaries
- Data is shared across teams
- A new Data Product is being designed
- Transformation will affect existing roles
- AI capabilities are being considered
- Business processes cross multiple teams
- Governance is complex
- Different stakeholder groups have conflicting priorities
- External organisations are involved

Stakeholder analysis should be treated as iterative.

As understanding improves, new stakeholders and relationships will often emerge.

---

# What is a Stakeholder?

A stakeholder can be understood as a person, group or organisation that can affect, or is affected by, organisational activity or outcomes (Freeman, 1984).

Within DISCOVER, this definition is intentionally broad.

Stakeholders may therefore include people who:

```text
Make Decisions

Perform Work

Own Information

Own Technology

Consume Information

Fund Change

Govern Change

Provide Expertise

Receive Outcomes

Experience Impact
```

Do not limit stakeholder discovery to people who attend project meetings.

For example:

```text

├── Financial Controllers
├── FP&A

├── Commercial Finance
└── Finance Analysts

Sales
│
├── Sales Leadership
├── Account Executives
└── Sales Operations

Technology
│
├── Product
├── Engineering
├── Architecture
└── Platform Operations

Data
│
├── Data Product
├── Data Engineering
├── Analytics
├── Data Governance
└── Data Science
```

IIBA similarly recommends identifying stakeholder groups and then identifying the relevant individuals within them (IIBA, n.d.b).

# Stakeholder Roles

Stakeholders may fulfil several different roles during discovery.

## Sponsor

Provides executive sponsorship, direction or funding.

---

## Decision Maker

Has authority to make or approve important decisions.

---

## Business Owner

Owns the business capability, process or outcome being explored.

---

## Domain Expert

Provides specialist knowledge about the business domain.

---

## Process Owner

Accountable for a particular business process.

---

## Operational User

Performs the work and understands how the process operates in practice.

---

## Information Owner

Accountable for important business information or data.

---

## Data Steward

Supports the quality, definition and governance of data.

---

## System Owner

Accountable for a technology platform or application.

---

## Consumer

Uses reports, analytics, Data Products or other outputs.

---

## Delivery Stakeholder

Contributes to the implementation of recommendations.

---

## Impacted Stakeholder

May experience changes to responsibilities, processes, information or technology as a result of the initiative.

A stakeholder may perform several of these roles simultaneously.

```text
                    Revenue Management
                           │
             ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
             │             │             │
           Sales         Finance        Rights
             │             │             │
             └─────────────┼─────────────┘
                           │
                          Data
                           │
                           ▼
                     Leadership
```

The objective is to understand the network of people involved in creating and using the capability.

# Stakeholder Mapping Dimensions

Stakeholders can be assessed using several dimensions.

DISCOVER commonly considers:

- Influence
- Interest
- Impact
- Decision Authority
- Knowledge
- Engagement
- Change Impact

Not every engagement requires every dimension.

Use only the dimensions that improve understanding.

---

# Influence

Influence considers the stakeholder's ability to affect decisions, priorities or outcomes.

Possible ratings:

```text
High

Medium

Low
```

Ask:

- Can this person approve or stop the initiative?
- Can they influence investment?
- Do others look to them for direction?
- Do they control an important dependency?

---

# Interest

Interest describes how relevant or important the initiative is to the stakeholder.

Ask:

- How closely are they connected to the problem?
- Will they use the outcome?
- Are they actively interested in improving this area?
- Does success materially affect their objectives?

---

# Impact

Impact considers how significantly the stakeholder may be affected by the proposed change.

For example:

```text
Executive Sponsor

Influence = High
Impact = Low
```

while:

```text
Operational Analyst

Influence = Low
Impact = High
```

The second stakeholder should not be ignored simply because they have less organisational authority.

Government stakeholder-mapping guidance similarly considers influence or power, interest and impact when determining stakeholder positioning and engagement (Government Analysis Function, 2024a).

---

# Knowledge

Knowledge considers how much relevant domain expertise a stakeholder holds.

For example:

| Stakeholder | Domain Knowledge |
|-------------|------------------|
| CFO | High strategic knowledge |
| Finance Manager | High process knowledge |
| Finance Analyst | High operational knowledge |
| Data Engineer | High technical/data knowledge |

This is useful during discovery because organisational authority and domain knowledge are not the same thing.

---

# Decision Authority

Identify stakeholders who can make or approve important decisions.

Examples:

```text
Approve Investment

Approve KPI Definition

Approve Business Rules

Approve Product Scope

Approve Data Access

Approve Process Change
```

Decision authority should subsequently connect to the RACI Matrix and governance model.

---

# Power / Interest Matrix

A common stakeholder-mapping technique positions stakeholders according to their level of power or influence and their level of interest.

```text
                     HIGH POWER

                         │
        KEEP             │          MANAGE
      SATISFIED          │          CLOSELY
                         │
                         │
LOW INTEREST ────────────┼──────────── HIGH INTEREST
                         │
                         │
       MONITOR           │           KEEP
                         │         INFORMED
                         │
                     LOW POWER
```

The matrix is intended to guide the **type and intensity of engagement**, rather than to determine whose views matter.

Power/interest mapping is widely used as a stakeholder-analysis technique (Government Analysis Function, 2024a).

---

# Manage Closely

Typically:

```text
High Influence
+
High Interest
```

Possible stakeholders:

- Executive Sponsor
- Business Owner
- Product Owner
- Senior Process Owner

Engagement may include:

- Regular involvement
- Decision sessions
- Playback meetings
- Roadmap reviews
- Prioritisation

---

# Keep Satisfied

Typically:

```text
High Influence
+
Lower Interest
```

These stakeholders may not need detailed involvement in every discovery activity but should remain confident that the engagement is aligned with business priorities.

---

# Keep Informed

Typically:

```text
Lower Influence
+
High Interest
```

These stakeholders often include important users and Subject Matter Experts.

They can provide significant discovery value even when they have limited formal authority.

---

# Monitor

Typically:

```text
Lower Influence
+
Lower Interest
```

These stakeholders may require less frequent engagement.

However, their position should be reassessed as the engagement evolves.

---

# Do Not Confuse Influence With Importance

One of the risks of a simple Power / Interest Matrix is that teams may focus only on senior stakeholders.

DISCOVER should deliberately consider:

```text
Influence
+
Impact
+
Knowledge
+
Decision Authority
```

For example:

```text
Finance Analyst

Formal Influence = Low

Operational Knowledge = High

Change Impact = High
```

This person may be essential to understanding how the business actually operates.

# Stakeholder Relationship Mapping

Stakeholder Mapping can also show relationships between people and groups.

For example:

```text
                     CFO
                      │
                      ▼
             Finance Director
                      │
              ┌───────┴───────┐
              ▼               ▼
          FP&A Team      Finance Control
              │               │
              └───────┬───────┘
                      ▼
                Finance Data
                      │
                      ▼
                 Data Team
```

Relationship mapping can expose:

- Decision pathways
- Information dependencies
- Organisational silos
- Informal influence
- Collaboration patterns
- Potential communication gaps

# Formal and Informal Influence

The organisation chart does not always reveal how decisions really happen.

During discovery ask:

- Who is formally accountable?
- Who is actually consulted?
- Who has trusted expertise?
- Who influences the decision maker?
- Who understands the history?
- Who can unblock the work?
- Who is frequently asked for advice?

Informal networks can be particularly important in complex organisations.

---

# Missing Voices

Stakeholder Mapping should actively look for groups that have not yet been represented.

Ask:

```text
Who have we not spoken to?

Who performs the work?

Who receives the output?

Who will experience the change?

Who understands the exceptions?

Who disagrees with the current approach?

Who owns upstream information?

Who consumes downstream information?
```

An incomplete stakeholder map can create an incomplete model of the business.

---

# Stakeholder Interviews

Once stakeholders have been identified, determine who requires individual interviews.

Interviews are particularly useful where:

- Specialist expertise exists
- Sensitive subjects need discussion
- Senior stakeholder time is limited
- Conflicting viewpoints require exploration
- Significant complexity exists
- Detailed process knowledge is needed

Interview findings should feed back into the Stakeholder Register and other DISCOVER artefacts.

# Stakeholder Engagement Strategy

Once stakeholders are understood, determine how they should be engaged.

| Stakeholder | Engagement | Method | Frequency |
|-------------|------------|--------|-----------|
| Executive Sponsor | Decision / Direction | Playback | Fortnightly |
| Business Owner | Active Collaboration | Workshop / Interview | Weekly |
| SME | Discovery | Workshop | As Required |
| Operational User | Discovery / Validation | Interview / Observation | As Required |
| Data Owner | Consultation | Workshop | As Required |

The objective is to engage stakeholders appropriately rather than maximise the number of meetings.

---

# Engagement Preferences

Where useful, capture how stakeholders prefer to engage.

Examples:

- Workshop
- One-to-One Interview
- Written Review
- Playback Session
- Dashboard
- Email Update
- Executive Summary
- Working Session

Understanding communication preferences can improve participation and reduce unnecessary engagement overhead.

---

# Stakeholder Sentiment

Discovery may also benefit from understanding a stakeholder's current position.

For example:

```text
Supportive

Neutral

Concerned

Resistant

Unknown
```

This should not be used to label people negatively.

Instead, understand the reason behind the position.

Ask:

```text
What concern does this person have?

What outcome are they protecting?

What previous experience influences their view?

What evidence would help?
```

Resistance may reveal legitimate risks or missing requirements.

---

# Stakeholder Needs

For key stakeholders capture:

```text
Goals

Responsibilities

Decisions

Information Needs

Pain Points

Success Measures
```

For example:

```text
Stakeholder
Finance Director

Goal
Accurate and timely view of financial performance

Decisions
Where performance requires intervention

Information
Actual Revenue
Forecast Revenue
Variance
Margin

Pain Point
Reporting requires manual consolidation
```

This begins connecting stakeholders to Data Product requirements.



# Stakeholder Mapping and RACI

Stakeholder Mapping and RACI answer different questions.

Stakeholder Mapping asks:

```text
Who matters?

Why?

How should we engage them?
```

RACI asks:

```text
Who is Responsible?

Who is Accountable?

Who should be Consulted?

Who should be Informed?
```

A useful sequence is therefore:

```text
Stakeholder Mapping
       │
       ▼
Stakeholder Understanding
       │
       ▼
Ownership & Decision Analysis
       │
       ▼
RACI
```

# Stakeholder Mapping and Capability Mapping

Capabilities provide a useful mechanism for identifying stakeholders.

For example:

```text
Revenue Management
        │
        ├── Capability Owner
        ├── Process Owner
        ├── Finance Users
        ├── Sales Users
        ├── Data Owners
        └── Technology Owners
```

This helps avoid stakeholder identification becoming purely organisation-chart driven.

---

# Stakeholder Mapping and Process Modelling

Processes help identify people involved in actual work.

For each process ask:

```text
Who initiates it?

Who performs each activity?

Who approves decisions?

Who receives the output?

Who resolves exceptions?
```

These stakeholders should be reflected in the Stakeholder Register.

---

# Stakeholder Mapping and Information Discovery

For every important information concept ask:

```text
Who creates it?

Who owns it?

Who maintains it?

Who uses it?

Who approves its definition?
```

This can reveal different forms of ownership.

For example:

```text
Business Owner

Data Owner

Data Steward

System Owner

Consumer
```

These responsibilities should not automatically be assumed to belong to the same person.

---

# Stakeholder Mapping and Data Products

Data Products exist to serve users and decisions.

For each candidate Data Product identify:

- Product Owner
- Business Owner
- Data Owner
- Data Producer
- Consumer
- Decision Maker
- Technical Owner

Then ask:

```text
Who needs this product?

What decision does it support?

How frequently is it needed?

How will it be consumed?

What would make the user trust it?
```

Stakeholder discovery therefore provides direct input into Data Product Design.

---

# Stakeholder Mapping and AI

AI discovery requires particular care because the users of an AI capability may not be the only people affected by it.

Consider:

```text
Users

Decision Makers

People Subject to Decisions

Data Owners

Domain Experts

Risk

Legal

Privacy

Security

Technology

AI / Data Science
```

Stakeholder engagement for Data and AI initiatives should be continuous and should consider groups affected by the initiative, not simply those designing or sponsoring it (Ministry of Justice, 2025).

Useful questions include:

- Who will use the AI capability?
- Whose work will change?
- Who is affected by its outputs?
- Who provides the knowledge or data?
- Who validates the results?
- Who is accountable for decisions?
- Who manages errors?
- Who can override the AI?
- Who needs to understand how it works?

These findings should feed into the AI Opportunity Canvas.

# Finance Example

## Discovery Objective

Identify the stakeholders involved in financial planning, revenue management and reporting.

An initial map might identify:

```text
                         CFO
                          │
                 Finance Director
                          │
        ┌─────────────────┼─────────────────┐
        │                 │                 │
       FP&A         Financial Control   Commercial Finance
        │                 │                 │
        └─────────────────┼─────────────────┘
                          │
                    Finance Data
                          │
          ┌───────────────┼───────────────┐
          │               │               │
        Sales            Data            Rights
```

### Stakeholder Questions

- Who owns financial reporting?
- Who owns revenue definitions?
- Who approves recognition rules?
- Who creates forecasts?
- Who consumes forecasts?
- Who owns actual revenue information?
- Which teams reconcile data?
- Who approves financial adjustments?
- Who owns upstream commercial data?
- Who understands the manual workarounds?
- Who is accountable for statutory reporting?
- Who can approve improvements?

### Potential Findings

Discovery may reveal:

```text
Finance owns Revenue definitions.

Sales owns important upstream contract information.

Several Finance teams create independent forecasts.

Operational Analysts understand undocumented reconciliation rules.

Data ownership between Finance and Sales is unclear.

Executive reporting depends upon manually curated information.
```

### Potential Outputs

- Finance Stakeholder Map
- Finance Stakeholder Register
- RACI Matrix
- Data Ownership Model
- KPI Ownership
- Governance Requirements
- Data Product Ownership

# Stakeholder Mapping Workshop

A DISCOVER Stakeholder Mapping session might follow this structure.

| Stage | Activity |
|-------|----------|
| 1 | Confirm business scope |
| 2 | Identify stakeholder groups |
| 3 | Identify individuals |
| 4 | Identify stakeholder roles |
| 5 | Assess influence |
| 6 | Assess interest |
| 7 | Assess impact |
| 8 | Identify knowledge holders |
| 9 | Identify decision makers |
| 10 | Map relationships |
| 11 | Identify missing voices |
| 12 | Agree engagement approach |

The map should then be validated and updated throughout discovery.

# Maintaining the Stakeholder Map

Stakeholder Mapping should not end after the first workshop.

Review the map when:

- New business areas are discovered
- Scope changes
- New dependencies appear
- Product ownership becomes clearer
- Organisational responsibilities change
- New Data Products are identified
- AI opportunities are identified
- Roadmap decisions are being made

IIBA also describes stakeholder analysis as something that should be maintained throughout an initiative rather than treated as a one-time exercise (IIBA, n.d.c).

# Common Pitfalls

## Only Mapping Senior Stakeholders

Senior stakeholders provide strategic context.

Operational stakeholders often reveal how the business actually works.

Both are required.

---

## Using the Organisation Chart as the Stakeholder Map

Organisation charts show formal reporting structures.

They do not necessarily show:

- Informal influence
- Data ownership
- Process dependencies
- Domain expertise
- Consumers
- External relationships

Use the organisation chart as evidence, not as the final model.

---

## Ignoring Low-Influence / High-Impact Stakeholders

People who experience significant change may have little formal authority.

Their perspective still matters.

---

## Failing to Identify Domain Experts

The most knowledgeable stakeholder may not be the most senior stakeholder.

---

## Treating Resistance as a Problem to Overcome

Understand why a stakeholder is concerned.

They may be exposing:

- Risk
- Missing requirements
- Previous failures
- Operational consequences
- Governance concerns

---

## Inviting Everyone to Everything

Stakeholder engagement should be purposeful.

Select participants based upon the objective of each discovery activity.

---

## Capturing Names Without Context

A list of names is not Stakeholder Mapping.

Capture why each stakeholder matters.

---

## Failing to Update the Map

Discovery changes understanding.

The stakeholder model should evolve with it.

---

# What Good Looks Like

Effective Stakeholder Mapping should result in:

- Relevant stakeholder groups identified
- Key individuals identified
- Business owners identified
- Domain Experts identified
- Decision makers identified
- Operational users represented
- Information owners identified
- Data and technology stakeholders identified
- Influence understood
- Impact understood
- Relationships understood
- Missing voices identified
- Appropriate engagement planned
- Ownership gaps exposed

Most importantly, the map should help ensure that discovery is based upon a **representative understanding of the business**.
