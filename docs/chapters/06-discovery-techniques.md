# Chapter 6 – Discovery Techniques

> *"The quality of discovery depends not only on the questions you ask, but also on the techniques you use to uncover the answers."*

---

# 6.1 Introduction

Discovery is more than interviewing stakeholders.

Successful discovery combines multiple techniques to develop a complete understanding of the business, validate assumptions and identify opportunities for improvement.

No single technique provides all of the answers.

The DISCOVER methodology encourages practitioners to select the most appropriate techniques depending upon the business problem, stakeholders involved and desired outcomes.

---

# 6.2 Objectives

The objectives of discovery techniques are to:

- Understand the business context
- Validate assumptions
- Explore current ways of working
- Capture business knowledge
- Identify pain points
- Discover opportunities
- Build consensus
- Reduce uncertainty

---

# 6.3 Selecting the Right Technique

Different techniques provide different insights.

| Technique | Best Used For |
|-----------|---------------|
| Interviews | Individual perspectives |
| Workshops | Shared understanding |
| Observation | Understanding real work |
| Document Analysis | Existing knowledge |
| Capability Mapping | Business architecture |
| Process Mapping | Understanding workflows |
| Event Storming | Complex business domains |
| Data Profiling | Understanding data quality |
| System Analysis | Technology landscape |
| Journey Mapping | User experience |
| Root Cause Analysis | Problem investigation |
| Brainstorming | Opportunity generation |

Most engagements will use several techniques.

---

# 6.4 Interviews

Interviews provide detailed understanding from individual stakeholders.

Suitable for:

- Executives
- Subject Matter Experts
- Operational users
- Technology teams

Interviews allow:

- Open discussion
- Detailed questioning
- Exploration of sensitive topics
- Clarification of assumptions

Typical outputs:

- Interview notes
- Stakeholder insights
- Risks
- Opportunities

---

# 6.5 Workshops

Workshops bring together multiple perspectives.

Suitable for:

- Business discovery
- Process mapping
- Capability definition
- Prioritisation
- Playback sessions

Advantages include:

- Collaboration
- Consensus building
- Shared understanding
- Faster discovery

---

# 6.6 Observation

Observation helps understand how work is actually performed.

Observe:

- Daily activities
- Manual processes
- System usage
- Workarounds
- Team interactions

Observation often reveals differences between documented processes and reality.

---

# 6.7 Document Analysis

Review existing documentation before engaging stakeholders.

Examples include:

- Business strategies
- Process documentation
- User guides
- Architecture diagrams
- Data catalogues
- Existing reports
- Operating procedures

Document analysis reduces duplication and identifies areas requiring clarification.

---

# 6.8 Capability Mapping

Capability Mapping identifies what the business does rather than how it does it.

Capabilities remain relatively stable even when processes or technology change.

Typical outputs include:

- Capability Maps
- Capability Hierarchies
- Capability Assessments

---

# 6.9 Process Mapping

Process Mapping documents how work flows through the organisation.

Typical techniques include:

- Swimlane diagrams
- BPMN
- SIPOC
- Value Stream Mapping

Outputs include:

- Process Maps
- Pain Points
- Dependencies
- Improvement opportunities

---

# 6.10 Event Storming

Event Storming is a collaborative technique used to explore complex business domains.

It is particularly useful when:

- Processes are poorly understood
- Multiple systems are involved
- Business rules are complex
- Numerous stakeholders participate

Outputs include:

- Business Events
- Commands
- Actors
- Aggregates
- Business Rules

---

# 6.11 Data Discovery

Data discovery focuses on understanding business information.

Activities include:

- Identifying data sources
- Reviewing reports
- Understanding KPIs
- Assessing data quality
- Identifying ownership
- Reviewing metadata

Outputs include:

- Data Catalogue
- KPI Catalogue
- Business Glossary

---

# 6.12 Technology Discovery

Technology discovery develops an understanding of:

- Applications
- Integrations
- Platforms
- Interfaces
- Infrastructure
- Security
- Technical debt

Outputs include:

- System Landscape
- Integration Catalogue
- Technology Assessment

---

# 6.13 Root Cause Analysis

Symptoms should never be confused with problems.

Useful techniques include:

- Five Whys
- Fishbone Diagram
- Cause and Effect Analysis

The objective is to identify underlying business problems rather than treating symptoms.

---

# 6.14 Journey Mapping

Journey Mapping explores experiences from the perspective of users.

Typical journeys include:

- Customer Journey
- Employee Journey
- Partner Journey
- Data Journey

Journey Mapping identifies friction and opportunities for improvement.

---

# 6.15 Brainstorming

Brainstorming should be used after the current state has been understood.

Possible topics include:

- AI opportunities
- Automation
- Process improvements
- Data products
- Reporting improvements
- Innovation

Separate idea generation from evaluation.

---

# 6.16 Choosing Techniques

The following matrix can help select appropriate techniques.

| Objective | Recommended Techniques |
|-----------|------------------------|
| Understand strategy | Interviews, Document Analysis |
| Understand business capabilities | Capability Mapping, Workshops |
| Understand processes | Process Mapping, Observation |
| Understand data | Data Discovery, Report Review |
| Understand systems | Technology Discovery |
| Understand users | Interviews, Observation |
| Identify opportunities | Workshops, Brainstorming |
| Validate findings | Playback Workshops |

---

# 6.17 Combining Techniques

Discovery should never rely on a single technique.

For example:

1. Review documentation.
2. Interview stakeholders.
3. Observe operational activities.
4. Facilitate workshops.
5. Validate findings.
6. Prioritise opportunities.

Each technique reinforces the others.

---

# 6.18 Deliverables

Discovery techniques may contribute to an appropriate subset of artefacts, depending on the discovery objective and evidence gathered:

- Stakeholder Register
- Capability Map
- Process Maps
- Decision Catalogue
- Business Rules Catalogue
- Business Glossary / Concept Catalogue
- Enterprise Ontology
- Data Catalogue
- Technology Landscape
- Evidence Register
- Assumption & Open Question Log
- Pain Point Register
- Opportunity Register
- Golden Thread Traceability

Detailed delivery backlogs are created by the chosen delivery method after sufficient discovery evidence exists; they are not a mandatory DISCOVER output.

# Chapter Summary

Discovery techniques provide the practical tools required to understand a business domain.

Selecting and combining appropriate techniques enables discovery teams to validate assumptions, uncover hidden knowledge and develop a comprehensive understanding of how the business operates.
