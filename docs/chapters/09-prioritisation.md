# Chapter 9 – Prioritisation

> *"Discovery creates opportunities. Prioritisation determines where to invest."*

---

# 9.1 Introduction

Discovery engagements often identify dozens of opportunities.

Attempting to deliver all of them simultaneously is neither practical nor desirable.

Prioritisation provides a structured approach for evaluating opportunities against business value, strategic importance, feasibility and organisational readiness.

The objective is to ensure resources are focused on initiatives that deliver the greatest business benefit.

---

# 9.2 Objectives

Prioritisation aims to:

- Identify high-value opportunities
- Align initiatives to business strategy
- Balance effort against value
- Consider dependencies
- Assess delivery risk
- Support investment decisions
- Create an achievable outcome-led sequence

---

# 9.3 What Should Be Prioritised?

Typical opportunities include:

- Data Products
- AI initiatives
- Process improvements
- Technology enhancements
- Data quality improvements
- Reporting enhancements
- Governance improvements
- Automation opportunities

---

# 9.4 Prioritisation Principles

Every opportunity should be assessed consistently.

Consider:

- Business value
- Strategic alignment
- User impact
- Delivery effort
- Cost
- Risk
- Data readiness
- Technical feasibility
- Dependencies
- Time to value

---

# 9.5 Business Value

Business value should always be considered first.

Examples include:

- Revenue growth
- Cost reduction
- Productivity improvement
- Risk reduction
- Compliance
- Customer satisfaction
- Employee experience

---

# 9.6 Effort

Estimate the effort required.

Consider:

- Complexity
- Delivery duration
- Resource availability
- Technical challenges
- Integration effort
- Data preparation

---

# 9.7 Strategic Alignment

Ask:

- Does this support business strategy?
- Does this support organisational objectives?
- Does leadership support this initiative?
- Will this enable future capabilities?

---

# 9.8 Data Readiness

Assess:

- Data availability
- Data quality
- Business ownership
- Metadata
- Governance
- Security

Poor data readiness may reduce priority.

---

# 9.9 Risk Assessment

Consider:

- Delivery risk
- Technical risk
- Adoption risk
- Regulatory risk
- Operational risk
- Change management risk

---

# 9.10 Dependencies

Identify:

- Business dependencies
- Technical dependencies
- Data dependencies
- Resource dependencies
- Programme dependencies

Some opportunities cannot begin until others have completed.

---

# 9.11 Scoring Model

An example scoring model is shown below.

| Criterion | Score |
|------------|------:|
| Business Value | 1–5 |
| Strategic Alignment | 1–5 |
| User Impact | 1–5 |
| Data Readiness | 1–5 |
| Technical Feasibility | 1–5 |
| Delivery Effort | 1–5 |
| Risk | 1–5 |

The weighting applied to each criterion should reflect organisational priorities.

Scoring is a **decision aid**, not objective truth.

Avoid fabricated precision. Where evidence is limited, use qualitative categories, record confidence and make assumptions explicit.

---

# 9.12 Prioritisation Matrix

Opportunities can be grouped into four categories.

| Value | Effort | Priority |
|---------|---------|----------|
| High | Low | Quick Win |
| High | High | Strategic Initiative |
| Low | Low | Fill-In Work |
| Low | High | Avoid or Defer |

---

# 9.13 Validating Priorities

Prioritisation should be reviewed with stakeholders.

Confirm:

- Assumptions
- Business value
- Dependencies
- Risks
- Resource availability

Priorities should be agreed collaboratively.

---

# 9.14 Deliverables

Prioritisation should produce:

- Prioritised Opportunity Register
- Scoring Matrix
- Dependency Map
- Benefits Register
- Investment Recommendations
