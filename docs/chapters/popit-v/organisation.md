# Organisation

> *"Capabilities and accountability matter more than the boxes on the organisation chart."*

## Purpose

The **Organisation** perspective focuses on how the enterprise is structured to create value: business capabilities, ownership, governance, operating model, organisational boundaries, strategic alignment and relationships with external parties.

An organisation chart shows reporting lines. It does not necessarily show who owns an end-to-end outcome, where a capability sits, how decisions are governed, or how several teams collaborate to deliver a customer or commercial result.

DISCOVER therefore models the organisation primarily through **capabilities and accountabilities**, using organisational structure as supporting context.

## Why This Matters

Many data and transformation problems are organisational before they are technical.

Examples include:

- no owner for an enterprise definition;
- two teams performing the same capability;
- one process crossing several teams with no end-to-end accountability;
- conflicting priorities between functions;
- governance forums that cannot make the decision they are asked to make;
- data ownership inferred from system ownership;
- local optimisation creating enterprise-wide friction;
- external partners holding critical information or capability.

Understanding Organisation prevents technology from being used to paper over unclear accountability.

## Discovery Objectives

Establish:

- the strategic context;
- the business capabilities involved;
- capability ownership;
- organisational units contributing to the outcome;
- end-to-end accountability;
- governance and decision forums;
- operating-model relationships;
- external partner dependencies;
- regulatory or corporate boundaries;
- where duplication or fragmentation exists;
- how priorities are set;
- where capability maturity constrains outcomes.

## What Good Looks Like

You should be able to answer:

- Which business capability delivers the outcome?
- Which capabilities enable it?
- Who owns each capability?
- Which teams perform the work?
- Where do responsibilities cross boundaries?
- Who has authority to change a rule or process?
- Which forums set priorities or resolve conflicts?
- Which external organisations participate?
- Which strategic objectives are supported?
- Where does local optimisation conflict with enterprise value?

## Capabilities, Processes and Organisation Units

Keep three concepts separate:

**Capability** — what the organisation must be able to do.

**Process** — how work flows to achieve an outcome.

**Organisation unit** — who currently performs or owns the work.

A capability such as *Manage Rights* may be performed by several teams and supported by several processes and systems. It should not be renamed every time the organisation restructures.

This distinction is central to keeping DISCOVER business-led.

## What to Discover

### Strategy and Outcomes

Understand:

- corporate and business-unit objectives;
- value propositions;
- commercial priorities;
- regulatory commitments;
- strategic constraints;
- investment themes;
- measures of success.

### Business Capabilities

Identify:

- core capabilities;
- supporting capabilities;
- differentiating capabilities;
- capability dependencies;
- duplicated capabilities;
- capability gaps;
- maturity differences;
- ownership.

### Organisational Boundaries

Explore:

- functions;
- business units;
- labels / brands / territories;
- central versus local responsibilities;
- shared services;
- matrix relationships;
- outsourced activities;
- joint ventures and partners.

### Governance

Understand:

- decision forums;
- escalation forums;
- architecture / data governance;
- risk and compliance governance;
- investment and portfolio governance;
- decision authority;
- meeting cadence;
- inputs and outputs;
- unresolved decisions.

### Operating Model

Explore:

- centralised versus federated activity;
- shared capabilities;
- service relationships;
- global versus local autonomy;
- standards and policies;
- ownership and stewardship models;
- product / platform models.

### External Ecosystem

Identify:

- customers;
- suppliers;
- agencies;
- distributors;
- regulators;
- research providers;
- platform partners;
- data providers;
- strategic partners.

## Generic Discovery Questions

### Strategy

- What outcome is this business area responsible for?
- Which strategic objective does it support?
- What has leadership prioritised this year?
- What must the organisation become better at?
- What would happen if this capability failed?

### Capabilities

- What must the organisation be able to do to create this outcome?
- Which capability is most important?
- Which capabilities are shared?
- Where is the same capability performed in several places?
- Which capabilities are difficult to scale?
- Which capability is constrained by poor information?

### Ownership

- Who owns the capability?
- What does ownership mean in practice?
- Who controls the budget?
- Who sets policy?
- Who owns performance?
- Where is end-to-end accountability missing?

### Governance

- Which forum can make this decision?
- What information does that forum require?
- Which decisions are repeatedly deferred?
- Where do escalations go?
- Are governance decisions documented?
- Which decision rights conflict?

### Operating Model

- What is intentionally centralised?
- What is intentionally local?
- Where are standards mandatory?
- Where can teams choose their own approach?
- Which activities are provided as shared services?
- Where does the model create friction?

### External Parties

- Which third parties perform critical activities?
- Who owns the commercial relationship?
- Which information is exchanged?
- Which obligations or SLAs apply?
- What would happen if the partner stopped providing the service?


## Evidence to Capture

Do not treat workshop conversation as automatically validated fact. Capture important findings using the DISCOVER evidence model:

- **Evidence** — explicitly supported by a trusted source, observed artefact or validated record.
- **Stakeholder Statement** — stated by a stakeholder but not yet independently validated.
- **Assumption** — plausible but unverified.
- **Interpretation** — an analytical conclusion drawn from evidence.
- **Recommendation** — a proposed intervention.
- **Open Question** — information still required.

Where evidence conflicts, record the contradiction and identify who or what can validate it. Use `UNKNOWN` when an important Golden Thread link is not established.

## Typical Pain Points

- fragmented ownership;
- duplicated capabilities;
- unclear accountability;
- slow governance;
- conflicting priorities;
- weak escalation;
- global/local tension;
- organisational silos;
- duplicated reporting;

## Opportunities

- clarify capability ownership;
- rationalise duplicated activity;
- establish end-to-end accountability;
- redesign governance;
- define enterprise standards with appropriate local flexibility;
- establish domain/data ownership;
- consolidate shared capabilities;
- improve portfolio prioritisation;
- create a capability-led data roadmap;
- identify where platform capabilities can be reused.

## Maturity Signals

### Fragmented
Capabilities are implicit, ownership follows org charts, governance is reactive, and teams optimise locally.

### Developing
Some capability views and governance exist, but ownership and boundaries remain inconsistent.

### Defined
Capabilities, accountability, governance and operating-model choices are documented and understood.

### Managed
Capability performance, maturity and investment are actively measured and used in prioritisation.

### Adaptive
The organisation can reconfigure teams and technology without losing clarity about durable capabilities and outcomes.

## Finance Example

### Discovery Focus

Understand how Finance capabilities such as Financial Planning, Financial Control, Revenue Accounting, Treasury, Tax and Management Reporting work together.

### Questions

- Which Finance capability owns forecast quality?
- Who owns revenue recognition policy?
- Where does Global Finance depend on local Finance?
- Which governance forum resolves accounting treatment questions?
- Which activities are centralised versus performed within business units?
- Are planning, actuals and commercial finance capabilities duplicated?
- Which external auditors, regulators or advisors influence the process?

### Outputs

- Finance Capability Map;
- Finance governance model;
- capability ownership;
- organisational dependency map;
- maturity profile;
- capability-to-value Golden Threads.

## Marketing Example

### Discovery Focus

Understand how brand, campaign planning, media, creative, CRM, performance marketing, analytics and agencies combine to deliver growth.

### Questions

- Which capability owns customer/audience strategy?
- Which decisions are central versus channel-specific?
- Who owns campaign effectiveness?
- Where do agencies provide critical capability?
- Which teams duplicate audience or performance analysis?
- Which governance forums approve budget and campaign direction?


## Golden Thread Connection

This perspective should not exist as an isolated checklist. Connect findings to the Golden Thread wherever the evidence supports it:

**Business Strategy → Business Capability → Business Process → Business Decision → Business Information → Business Rules → Technology / Data → Data Product / Analytics → Insight / Action → Business Value**

Use stable identifiers when the engagement requires traceability:

- `STR-` Strategic Objective
- `CAP-` Business Capability
- `PRC-` Business Process
- `DEC-` Business Decision
- `INF-` Information
- `BR-` Business Rule
- `SYS-` System / Technology
- `DAT-` Data Asset
- `DP-` Data Product
- `ACT-` Business Action
- `VAL-` Business Value

Supporting discovery records may use `EVD-`, `ASM-`, `OQ-`, `PAIN-`, `OPP-` and `RSK-`.

The objective is traceability rather than bureaucracy. Do not invent links merely to complete the chain.


## Facilitation Cautions

- Do not turn the capability map into the org chart.
- Do not name capabilities after systems.
- Avoid debating future reorganisation during early current-state discovery.
- Distinguish who performs work from who owns the capability.
- Validate capability boundaries with several stakeholders.
- Use maturity overlays only after a stable base map exists.

## Practitioner Checklist

- [ ] Strategic outcome is understood.
- [ ] Relevant capabilities are identified.
- [ ] Capability ownership is explicit or marked unknown.
- [ ] Organisational boundaries are understood.
- [ ] Governance forums and decision rights are captured.
- [ ] External dependencies are visible.
- [ ] Capability/process/team concepts have not been conflated.
- [ ] Maturity is evidence-based.
- [ ] Organisational findings connect to decisions, information and value.

## Chapter Summary

The Organisation perspective makes the enterprise understandable beyond reporting lines. By modelling durable capabilities, accountability, governance, operating-model choices and external dependencies, DISCOVER can connect strategy to the people, processes, information and technology needed to create value.
