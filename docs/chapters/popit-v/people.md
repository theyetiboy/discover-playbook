# People

> *"Businesses don't make decisions—people do. Understanding the people behind the processes is fundamental to successful discovery."*

---

# Purpose

The People perspective focuses on understanding the individuals, teams and roles that enable the organisation to operate.

It seeks to understand responsibilities, decision making, ownership, collaboration and knowledge. Effective discovery begins with understanding the people who perform the work before exploring the processes and technology that support them.

---

# Why This Matters

People create information.

People execute processes.

People make decisions.

People own systems.

People drive change.

Without understanding the human aspects of an organisation, it is difficult to identify genuine business problems or design solutions that will be successfully adopted.

Understanding people helps to:

- Clarify ownership
- Understand decision making
- Identify subject matter experts
- Discover knowledge gaps
- Improve collaboration
- Support organisational change
- Build trust throughout the discovery process

---

# Discovery Objectives

The objectives of the People perspective are to:

- Identify key stakeholders
- Understand organisational roles
- Discover decision makers
- Understand responsibilities
- Identify ownership
- Understand collaboration
- Identify knowledge holders
- Discover pain points experienced by users

---

# What Good Looks Like

At the end of discovery you should be able to answer:

- Who performs the work?
- Who owns the work?
- Who makes key decisions?
- Who consumes the outputs?
- Who creates the information?
- Who influences priorities?
- Who approves change?
- Who should be involved in future initiatives?

If these questions cannot be answered confidently, further discovery is required.

# Generic Discovery Questions

## Understanding Roles

- What is your role?
- What are your primary responsibilities?
- What does a typical day look like?
- Which activities consume most of your time?

---

## Decision Making

- Which decisions do you make?
- Which decisions require approval?
- What information do you rely upon?
- What makes decision making difficult?

---

## Ownership

- What are you accountable for?
- Which reports do you own?
- Which data do you own?
- Which processes are your responsibility?

---

## Collaboration

- Which teams do you work with regularly?
- Where do handovers occur?
- Where do delays happen?
- Which relationships work well?

---

## Knowledge

- Which knowledge exists only in people's heads?
- What documentation is missing?
- What training is required?
- What would happen if key individuals left?

---

## Improvement

- What frustrates you most?
- Which tasks are repetitive?
- Which activities add little value?
- If you could change one thing tomorrow, what would it be?

# Finance Example

## Discovery Focus

The objective is to understand how Finance teams collaborate to deliver accurate financial reporting, forecasting and regulatory compliance.

### Example Questions

#### Roles

- Which finance teams are involved in month-end close?
- Who owns budgeting and forecasting?
- Who is responsible for financial reporting?

#### Decision Making

- Who approves journals?
- Who approves budgets?
- Who signs off forecasts?
- Who owns regulatory submissions?

#### Collaboration

- Which teams work closely with Finance?
- Where do delays occur during month-end?
- Which approvals take the longest?

#### Knowledge

- Which activities rely on specialist knowledge?
- What documentation exists?
- Where are manual workarounds used?

### Typical Outputs

- Finance Stakeholder Map
- Decision Matrix
- Finance RACI
- Finance Ownership Register
- Finance Collaboration Model

---

# Marketing Example

## Discovery Focus

The objective is to understand how Marketing teams plan, execute and evaluate campaigns while collaborating across the wider organisation.

### Example Questions

#### Roles

- Who plans campaigns?
- Who creates campaign content?
- Who manages marketing platforms?
- Who analyses campaign performance?

#### Decision Making

- Who approves campaigns?
- Who approves budgets?
- Who prioritises initiatives?

#### Collaboration

- How does Marketing work with Sales?
- How does Marketing work with Finance?
- Which external agencies are involved?

#### Knowledge

- Which campaign knowledge is undocumented?
- Which expertise is difficult to replace?
- Which reporting skills are specialised?

### Typical Outputs

- Marketing Stakeholder Map
- Campaign Responsibility Matrix
- Marketing Decision Register
- Collaboration Map
- Marketing Ownership Register

# Key Outputs

A completed People discovery should provide:

- A clear understanding of organisational roles
- Defined ownership and accountability
- Documented decision-making responsibilities
- Identified collaboration patterns
- Recognition of key knowledge holders
- A stakeholder engagement plan for future initiatives

The People perspective ensures discovery begins with the individuals who operate, manage and improve the business.
