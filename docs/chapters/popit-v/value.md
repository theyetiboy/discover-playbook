# Value

> *"Discovery has no purpose unless it leads to meaningful, measurable business value."*

## Purpose

The **Value** perspective answers the most important question in DISCOVER:

> **So what?**

It focuses on why a capability, process, decision, information product or change exists; who benefits; what outcome is intended; what risk is reduced; and how success will be recognised.

Value prevents discovery from becoming an exercise in producing artefacts. A process map, ontology, dashboard or AI agent is an output. The real question is whether it enables a better business outcome.

## Why This Matters

Organisations can improve process documentation, data quality, architecture and reporting without materially improving business performance.

Without an explicit Value perspective it becomes difficult to:

- prioritise opportunities;
- challenge weak business cases;
- align investment with strategy;
- compare benefits and trade-offs;
- decide what not to build;
- measure whether change succeeded;
- justify ongoing product investment;
- connect data and AI to real outcomes.

Value is therefore both the end of the Golden Thread and a lens applied throughout discovery.

## Discovery Objectives

Understand:

- strategic outcomes;
- operational outcomes;
- customer/user outcomes;
- commercial outcomes;
- risk and compliance outcomes;
- KPIs and success measures;
- current cost and effort;
- opportunity cost;
- expected benefits;
- who receives the value;
- how benefits will be measured;
- when value should become visible;
- what happens if nothing changes.

## What Good Looks Like

You should be able to answer:

- What outcome matters?
- Who benefits?
- Which capability creates that value?
- Which decision or action must improve?
- How will success be measured?
- What is the current baseline?
- What is the cost of the current problem?
- What happens if no change is made?
- What benefit is expected and how confident are we?
- What risks could reduce or reverse value?
- How will benefits be measured after delivery?
- Which opportunity should be prioritised and why?

## What to Discover

### Strategic Value

- strategic objectives;
- market position;
- growth;
- differentiation;
- organisational capability;
- resilience;
- strategic optionality.

### Financial Value

- revenue growth;
- margin;
- cost reduction;
- cost avoidance;
- working capital;
- forecasting accuracy;
- investment return.

Avoid inventing financial precision when no evidence exists.

### Operational Value

- cycle-time reduction;
- throughput;
- productivity;
- fewer errors;
- reduced rework;
- improved SLA performance;
- reduced manual effort.

### Customer / User Value

- better experience;
- faster service;
- improved relevance;
- reduced friction;
- greater trust;
- improved decision confidence.

### Risk Value

- regulatory compliance;
- control effectiveness;
- auditability;
- security;
- privacy;
- resilience;
- reduced reputational risk.

### Knowledge / Decision Value

For data and analytics, an important value chain is:

**better information → better decision → better action → better outcome**

Capture the decision and action explicitly. “More insight” is not a sufficient value statement.

### Cost of Inaction

Ask:

- What happens if nothing changes?
- Does effort grow with scale?
- Is there a regulatory deadline?
- Is revenue lost?
- Is customer trust damaged?
- Is strategic opportunity missed?
- Does technical debt increase future cost?

## Generic Discovery Questions

### Business Outcomes

- What outcome is this business function trying to create?
- Which outcome matters most?
- Which strategic priority does it support?
- What would leadership notice if it improved?

### Measures

- How is success measured today?
- Is there a reliable baseline?
- Which KPI is closest to the business outcome?
- Which leading indicators exist?
- Which target or threshold matters?

### Benefits

- Who receives the benefit?
- What becomes faster, cheaper, safer or more effective?
- Which benefit is measurable?
- Which benefit is qualitative but still important?
- How quickly could value be realised?

### Cost and Effort

- How much manual effort exists?
- What is the cost of rework?
- Which systems or services are expensive?
- Which activity consumes scarce specialist time?
- What is the opportunity cost?

### Risk

- What business risk exists today?
- How likely and severe is it?
- What control currently mitigates it?
- Could the proposed change create new risk?
- Which risks matter to leadership, regulators or customers?

### Prioritisation

- If only one problem could be addressed, which should it be?
- Which opportunity unlocks others?
- Which has the strongest evidence?
- Which benefit depends on behaviour or adoption?
- Which opportunity should be stopped?

### Future Value

- Which new decision could better information enable?
- Which capability would create new commercial opportunity?
- Where could AI produce measurable value?
- Where would a simpler process change create the same benefit?


## Evidence to Capture

Do not treat workshop conversation as automatically validated fact. Capture important findings using the DISCOVER evidence model:

- **Evidence** — explicitly supported by a trusted source, observed artefact or validated record.
- **Stakeholder Statement** — stated by a stakeholder but not yet independently validated.
- **Assumption** — plausible but unverified.
- **Interpretation** — an analytical conclusion drawn from evidence.
- **Recommendation** — a proposed intervention.
- **Open Question** — information still required.

Where evidence conflicts, record the contradiction and identify who or what can validate it. Use `UNKNOWN` when an important Golden Thread link is not established.


## Value Statements

A useful value statement connects the problem, user, decision/action and measure.

Weak:

> Build a Finance dashboard.

Stronger:

> Reduce the time Finance analysts spend reconciling forecast inputs so that the monthly forecast can be challenged earlier, measured by reconciliation effort and time to an agreed forecast.

Weak:

> Use AI to answer rights questions.

Stronger:

> Reduce the time sales teams spend locating validated rights availability information while preserving human approval for contractual decisions, measured by response time, answer accuracy and avoided rework.

## Benefits Register

For each candidate benefit capture:

- benefit ID;
- outcome;
- beneficiary;
- current baseline;
- expected change;
- measure;
- evidence;
- owner;
- dependency;
- confidence;
- validation method;
- realisation horizon.

Qualitative benefits are legitimate. Label them honestly rather than fabricating monetary values.

## Typical Pain Points

- undefined value;
- output-based success measures;
- benefits not owned;
- no baseline;
- conflicting priorities;
- technology-led investment;
- ROI claimed without evidence;
- benefits counted twice;
- compliance treated only as cost;
- adoption excluded from value assumptions;
- projects declared complete at go-live rather than outcome realisation.

## Opportunities

- improve strategic alignment;
- prioritise by evidence and outcome;
- stop low-value work;
- quantify manual effort;
- improve business cases;
- establish benefit ownership;
- create leading and lagging measures;
- connect data products to decisions;
- measure adoption;
- create product feedback loops;
- govern AI by realised value and risk.

## Maturity Signals

### Fragmented
Work is prioritised by urgency or seniority; benefits are assumed and rarely measured.

### Developing
Some initiatives define KPIs and benefits, but baselines and ownership are inconsistent.

### Defined
Outcomes, value measures and benefit owners are standard parts of discovery and investment.

### Managed
Benefits are tracked after delivery; value evidence informs prioritisation and product decisions.

### Adaptive
The organisation continuously reallocates investment based on realised value, learning and changing strategy.

## Finance Example

### Discovery Focus

Understand how Finance creates value through control, compliance, planning and commercial insight.

### Questions

- Which financial outcomes are most important?
- How is forecast accuracy measured?
- What is the business value of earlier forecast confidence?
- What is the cost of manual reconciliation?
- Which controls reduce material risk?
- Which reporting delays affect commercial decisions?
- How would better revenue information change an action?
- What is the cost of incorrect recognition or attribution?

### Candidate Measures

Examples to validate—not defaults:

- forecast error;
- time to close;
- reconciliation effort;
- number/value of manual adjustments;
- time to answer a commercial finance question;
- audit exceptions;
- reporting cycle time.

## Marketing Example

### Discovery Focus

Understand how Marketing creates value through audience growth, customer engagement, brand outcomes and commercial performance.

### Questions

- Which campaign outcome matters?
- How is incremental impact distinguished from activity?
- Which decision would better information improve?
- How does budget allocation change based on performance?
- What is the cost of slow optimisation?
- Which measures are decision-useful rather than vanity metrics?


## Golden Thread Connection

This perspective should not exist as an isolated checklist. Connect findings to the Golden Thread wherever the evidence supports it:

**Business Strategy → Business Capability → Business Process → Business Decision → Business Information → Business Rules → Technology / Data → Data Product / Analytics → Insight / Action → Business Value**

Use stable identifiers when the engagement requires traceability:

- `STR-` Strategic Objective
- `CAP-` Business Capability
- `PRC-` Business Process
- `DEC-` Business Decision
- `INF-` Information
- `BR-` Business Rule
- `SYS-` System / Technology
- `DAT-` Data Asset
- `DP-` Data Product
- `ACT-` Business Action
- `VAL-` Business Value

Supporting discovery records may use `EVD-`, `ASM-`, `OQ-`, `PAIN-`, `OPP-` and `RSK-`.

The objective is traceability rather than bureaucracy. Do not invent links merely to complete the chain.


## Prioritisation Guidance

DISCOVER should not rely on fabricated scoring precision.

Where evidence is weak, use transparent qualitative categories such as:

- value: Low / Medium / High;
- confidence: Low / Medium / High;
- effort: indicative only;
- dependency: None / Moderate / Significant;
- risk: Low / Medium / High.

Make the evidence behind the rating visible.

## Practitioner Checklist

- [ ] Business outcome is explicit.
- [ ] Beneficiary is known.
- [ ] Decision/action link is visible.
- [ ] Baseline exists or is marked unknown.
- [ ] Success measure is meaningful.
- [ ] Benefit owner is identified.
- [ ] Risks and costs are considered.
- [ ] Cost of inaction is understood.
- [ ] Assumptions are labelled.
- [ ] Recommendations trace to value.
- [ ] Benefits realisation extends beyond go-live.

## Chapter Summary

The Value perspective ensures DISCOVER never loses sight of why discovery exists. Every recommendation should trace to a meaningful business outcome, supported by evidence and a credible way to measure whether the change actually worked.
