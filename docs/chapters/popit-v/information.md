# Information

> *"Information is the lifeblood of an organisation. Understanding how it is created, managed and consumed is fundamental to designing effective data products."*

---

# Purpose

The Information perspective focuses on understanding the information required to operate the business.

This includes business concepts, data, reports, KPIs, business rules, metadata and the relationships between them.

The goal is not simply to identify data sources, but to understand how information supports business decisions and enables organisational outcomes.

---

# Why This Matters

Poor information management results in:

- Multiple versions of the truth
- Duplicate reporting
- Inconsistent KPI definitions
- Poor decision making
- Low confidence in data
- Manual reconciliation
- Inefficient processes
- Difficulty adopting AI

By understanding information properly, organisations create the foundations for trusted reporting, reusable data products and enterprise-wide knowledge management.

---

# Discovery Objectives

The objectives of the Information perspective are to:

- Understand the information required by the business
- Identify critical business entities
- Discover KPIs and business measures
- Identify reports and dashboards
- Understand business rules
- Capture metadata and definitions
- Identify ownership
- Assess data quality
- Support the creation of the Enterprise Ontology

---

# What Good Looks Like

At the end of discovery you should be able to answer:

- What information is critical to this business area?
- Where does that information originate?
- Who owns it?
- Who consumes it?
- How is it transformed?
- Which reports use it?
- Which KPIs depend upon it?
- Can everyone agree on its definition?
- Can it be trusted?

If the answer to any of these questions is "we don't know", further discovery is required.

# Generic Discovery Questions

## Business Information

- What information is essential for your team?
- What information do you create?
- What information do you receive from other teams?
- What information do you share with others?

---

## Business Entities

- What are the most important business objects?
- Which entities does your team manage?
- How are they uniquely identified?
- What relationships exist between them?

---

## Reports

- Which reports do you rely on daily?
- Which reports are produced manually?
- Which reports are duplicated?
- Which reports are no longer useful?

---

## KPIs

- How do you measure success?
- Which KPIs matter most?
- How are they calculated?
- Who owns each KPI?
- Which KPIs are disputed?

---

## Data Quality

- Which information cannot be trusted?
- Where do errors occur?
- What requires manual correction?
- Which datasets generate the most questions?

---

## Business Rules

- What business rules govern this information?
- Which calculations are business critical?
- Which rules are undocumented?
- Which rules frequently change?

---

## Metadata

- Are business definitions documented?
- Does everyone use the same terminology?
- Where are definitions stored?
- Is lineage understood?

---

## Ownership

- Who owns this information?
- Who maintains it?
- Who approves changes?
- Who consumes it?

# Finance Example

## Discovery Focus

The objective is to understand how financial information supports statutory reporting, operational management and strategic decision making.

### Example Questions

#### Financial Reporting

- Which financial reports are produced each month?
- Which reports are required by regulators or auditors?
- Which reports require manual intervention?

#### Revenue

- How is revenue recognised?
- Are there different revenue recognition rules?
- Which adjustments are performed manually?

#### Cost Management

- How are costs allocated?
- How are budgets managed?
- How are forecasts produced?

#### KPIs

- Which financial KPIs are reported to executives?
- Which KPIs are externally reported?
- Which KPIs are disputed?

#### Data

- Which finance systems are considered the source of truth?
- Where are manual spreadsheets used?
- Which reconciliations consume the most effort?

### Typical Outputs

- Finance Business Glossary
- Revenue Ontology
- Financial KPI Catalogue
- Chart of Accounts Mapping
- Financial Reporting Catalogue
- Finance Data Product Opportunities

# Marketing Example

## Discovery Focus

The objective is to understand how marketing information supports campaign planning, customer engagement and commercial performance.

- Which metrics matter most?
- Which channels perform best?

- Which audiences generate the greatest value?

#### Reporting

- Which campaign reports are produced?
- Which dashboards are used by leadership?
- Which reports require manual preparation?

#### Data

- How is attribution performed?

- Campaign Ontology
- Marketing KPI Catalogue

- Attribution Rules
- Marketing Data Product Opportunities

# Chapter Summary

The Information perspective ensures that organisations understand not only what information exists, but why it matters, who owns it and how it supports business decisions.

By establishing trusted definitions, documenting business rules and identifying opportunities for improvement, discovery creates the foundation for governed data products, enterprise ontologies and AI-enabled capabilities.
