# Chapter 4 – The POPIT-V Framework

> *"A business can only be fully understood when viewed from multiple perspectives."*

---

# 4.1 Introduction

The DISCOVER Framework defines the lifecycle of a discovery engagement.

The POPIT-V Framework defines **what should be explored** during that engagement.

Together they provide a structured approach for understanding any business domain before designing data products, analytics or Artificial Intelligence (AI) solutions.

Rather than focusing solely on systems or processes, POPIT-V encourages discovery teams to examine the organisation through six complementary perspectives.

Each perspective contributes a different understanding of the business.

When combined, they provide a complete picture of how value is created.

---

# 4.2 Why POPIT-V?

Many discovery engagements focus too heavily on technology.

Others focus exclusively on business processes.

Both approaches create blind spots.

Successful discovery requires understanding:

- Who performs the work
- Why the work exists
- How the work is performed
- What information is required
- Which technology enables the work
- How business value is created

The POPIT-V Framework ensures each of these perspectives is explored consistently.

---

# 4.3 The Six Perspectives

| Perspective | Focus |
|------------|-------|
| People | Roles, responsibilities, ownership and decision making |
| Organisation | Strategy, governance, capabilities and objectives |
| Process | Activities, workflows and business operations |
| Information | Business concepts, knowledge, reports and data |
| Technology | Systems, applications, integrations and platforms |
| Value | Business outcomes, benefits and prioritisation |

Each perspective complements the others.

No single perspective should be considered in isolation.

---

# 4.4 How POPIT-V Supports DISCOVER

The POPIT-V Framework is applied primarily during the Investigate, Study, Capture and Organise stages of the DISCOVER Framework.

```
DISCOVER

Define
    │
Investigate  ← POPIT-V
    │
Study        ← POPIT-V
    │
Capture      ← POPIT-V
    │
Organise     ← POPIT-V
    │
Validate
    │
Evaluate
    │
Roadmap
```

The framework acts as a discovery lens rather than a sequential process.

---

# 4.5 People

Understanding people means understanding:

- Decision makers
- Stakeholders
- Data owners
- Process owners
- Report consumers
- Executive sponsors
- Subject Matter Experts
- Customers

Questions include:

- Who owns this process?
- Who makes decisions?
- Who creates the data?
- Who consumes the outputs?
- Who challenges the numbers?

Deliverables include:

- Stakeholder Register
- RACI Matrix
- Decision Matrix

---

# 4.6 Organisation

Organisation focuses on why the business exists.

Areas explored include:

- Business strategy
- Capabilities
- Governance
- Objectives
- Operating model
- Customers
- Products
- Services

Typical outputs include:

- Capability Map
- Strategy Summary
- KPI Catalogue

---

# 4.7 Process

Process examines how work flows through the organisation.

Discovery focuses on:

- Activities
- Decisions
- Events
- Inputs
- Outputs
- Exceptions
- Bottlenecks

Outputs include:

- Process Maps
- Value Streams
- Pain Register

---

# 4.8 Information

Information is the foundation of every data product.

Discovery explores:

- Business concepts
- Business glossary
- Data sources
- KPIs
- Reports
- Metrics
- Metadata
- Business rules

Outputs include:

- Business Glossary
- Data Catalogue
- Report Catalogue
- Enterprise Ontology

---

# 4.9 Technology

Technology enables business capabilities.

Discovery includes:

- Applications
- Integrations
- Platforms
- Infrastructure
- Security
- Technical debt
- Reporting tools
- AI capabilities

Outputs include:

- System Landscape
- Integration Catalogue
- Technology Assessment

---

# 4.10 Value

Value provides the reason every initiative exists.

Discovery considers:

- Desired business outcomes
- Measures of success
- Revenue growth
- Cost reduction
- Efficiency and productivity
- Customer or user experience
- Risk reduction and compliance
- Strategic contribution
- Opportunity cost

Outputs may include:

- KPI Catalogue
- Benefits / Value Register
- Opportunity Register
- Benefits Realisation Measures

---

# 4.11 Relationships Between Perspectives

The six perspectives should never be viewed independently.

People perform Processes.

Processes create and consume Information.

Technology enables Processes.

Organisation defines why Processes exist.

Value measures the success of every capability.

Together they provide a complete understanding of the business.

---

# 4.12 Applying POPIT-V

Each discovery workshop should intentionally explore all six perspectives.

Not every workshop will spend equal time on every perspective.

For example:

- Executive workshops may focus on Organisation and Value.
- Operational workshops may focus on Process and Information.
- Technical workshops may focus on Technology and Information.
- Finance workshops may place greater emphasis on Information and Value.

The framework should remain flexible while ensuring no important perspective is overlooked.

---

# 4.13 Deliverables

POPIT-V informs the selection of discovery artefacts rather than prescribing a fixed document set.

Depending on the objective, useful outputs may include:

- Stakeholder Register
- Capability Map
- Process Maps
- Decision Catalogue
- Business Rules Catalogue
- Business Glossary
- Information / Concept Catalogue
- Data Catalogue
- Technology Landscape
- Evidence Register
- Pain Point Register
- Opportunity Register
- Benefits / Value Register
- Golden Thread Traceability Matrix
- Maturity Profile
- Outcome-led Roadmap

Create only the artefacts that help answer the discovery objective.

# Chapter Summary

The POPIT-V Framework provides the primary analytical lens used throughout the DISCOVER methodology.

Rather than representing a sequence of activities, POPIT-V defines the perspectives through which every business domain should be explored.
