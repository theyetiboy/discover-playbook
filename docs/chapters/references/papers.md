# Papers, Research and Technical Guidance

## Ontology Engineering

### Noy, N.F. & McGuinness, D.L. (2001)

**Ontology Development 101: A Guide to Creating Your First Ontology.**

A practical ontology-development guide covering:

- classes;
- properties;
- constraints;
- instances;
- iterative ontology design.

Useful to DISCOVER's Ontology Discovery technique.

### Grüninger, M. & Fox, M.S. (1995)

Work on methodology for ontology design and evaluation, particularly the use of **competency questions** to define what an ontology must be able to represent or answer.

DISCOVER adopts competency questions as a pragmatic scope and validation technique.

## AI Risk and Governance

### NIST (2023)

**Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0), NIST AI 100-1.**

Official source:
https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

The framework organises risk-management activity around:

- Govern;
- Map;
- Measure;
- Manage.

As of 2026, NIST is undertaking revision work on the AI RMF. DISCOVER therefore records the version explicitly rather than describing 1.0 as permanently current.

### NIST (2024)

**Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile, NIST AI 600-1.**

Official source:
https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

Useful for GenAI-specific risk considerations.

## Evidence Use in DISCOVER

External literature can support:

- technique choice;
- terminology;
- recognised standards;
- governance requirements.

It must not be used to overwrite organisation-specific evidence.

For example:

> BABOK may describe a technique, but it does not tell us who owns a decision in the organisation.

> OWL defines ontology constructs, but it does not validate the organisation's definition of a Licence.

> NIST provides AI risk-management guidance, but it does not determine the organisation's risk appetite.

Preserve this distinction throughout the playbook.
