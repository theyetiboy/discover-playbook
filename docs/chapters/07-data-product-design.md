# Chapter 7 – Data Product Design

> *"Data only becomes valuable when it solves a business problem."*

---

# 7.1 Introduction

Discovery creates understanding.

Data Product Design transforms that understanding into reusable business assets that deliver measurable value.

Rather than producing reports or dashboards in isolation, data products are designed to solve specific business problems, support business decisions and improve organisational performance.

A successful data product combines trusted data, business logic, user experience and governance into a single product that can evolve over time.

---

# 7.2 What is a Data Product?

A Data Product is a reusable product that provides trusted information to support a defined business outcome.

Unlike a report or dashboard, a data product has:

- A clearly defined purpose
- A business owner
- Target users
- Governed data
- Defined success measures
- Ongoing maintenance
- A product lifecycle

Examples include:

- Executive KPI Dashboard
- Revenue Forecasting Platform
- Customer 360
- Rights Availability Explorer
- AI Metadata Generator
- Programme Performance Dashboard

---

# 7.3 Characteristics of a Good Data Product

Successful data products are:

- Business focused
- User centred
- Trusted
- Governed
- Reusable
- Discoverable
- Secure
- Scalable
- Measurable
- Continuously improved

---

# 7.4 Inputs

Formal Data Product Design should begin once **sufficient validated discovery evidence** exists to understand the business problem, intended users, decisions, information needs and expected value.

Candidate data-product hypotheses may emerge earlier during discovery. They should be recorded as hypotheses and tested rather than treated as approved requirements.

Discovery does not need to be "finished" in a waterfall sense before design thinking can begin. The evidence should simply be strong enough to avoid premature commitment to a solution.

Typical inputs include:

- Business capabilities
- Process maps
- Business glossary
- Enterprise ontology
- Data catalogue
- Stakeholder interviews
- Opportunity register
- AI opportunity register
- Business objectives

---

# 7.5 Start With the Business Problem

Avoid beginning with data.

Instead begin by defining the business problem.

Examples include:

- Forecast accuracy is poor.
- Revenue reporting is inconsistent.
- Customer information is fragmented.
- Rights availability is difficult to understand.
- Executives lack trusted KPIs.

A clearly defined problem should exist before considering possible solutions.

---

# 7.6 Identify the Users

Every data product should have clearly defined users.

Examples include:

- Executives
- Analysts
- Finance teams
- Sales teams
- Marketing teams
- Operations
- Technology teams
- External partners

Capture:

- User goals
- Decisions supported
- Frequency of use
- Required information

---

# 7.7 Define the Business Outcome

Clearly identify the outcome the data product should achieve.

Examples include:

- Improve forecast accuracy
- Increase operational efficiency
- Reduce manual reporting
- Improve data quality
- Increase revenue visibility
- Support faster decision making
- Improve customer understanding

Business outcomes should always be measurable.

---

# 7.8 Define Success Measures

Examples include:

- Adoption rate
- Reduction in manual effort
- Time saved
- Forecast accuracy
- Data quality score
- Customer satisfaction
- Revenue improvement
- Report usage
- Decision cycle time

Define baseline measurements where possible.

---

# 7.9 Identify Data Requirements

Capture:

- Required datasets
- Source systems
- Business entities
- KPIs
- Dimensions
- Measures
- Refresh frequency
- Data ownership
- Data quality expectations

The Enterprise Ontology should provide the foundation for understanding business entities and relationships.

---

# 7.10 Define Product Features

Examples include:

- Dashboards
- Interactive reporting
- Alerts
- Recommendations
- Search
- Natural language queries
- Forecasting
- AI assistants
- Workflow integration
- Mobile access

Prioritise features according to business value.

---

# 7.11 Governance

Every data product should define:

- Business Owner
- Product Owner
- Data Owner
- Technical Owner
- Support Model
- Security Model
- Data Classification
- Access Controls

Ownership should be clearly documented.

---

# 7.12 Product Lifecycle

Data products evolve over time.

Typical lifecycle stages include:

- Discovery
- Design
- Build
- Test
- Release
- Operate
- Improve
- Retire

Each stage should include feedback from users.

---

# 7.13 Common Data Product Types

Examples include:

## Operational

Supports day-to-day activities.

Examples:

- Task queues
- Operational dashboards
- Alerts

---

## Analytical

Supports analysis and insight.

Examples:

- KPI dashboards
- Trend analysis
- Executive reporting

---

## Predictive

Supports future decisions.

Examples:

- Forecasting
- Risk scoring
- Recommendations
- Machine Learning models

---

## AI Enabled

Supports knowledge work.

Examples:

- AI assistants
- Document summarisation
- Metadata generation
- Natural language querying
- Conversational analytics

---

# 7.14 Measuring Value

Data products should demonstrate measurable value.

Examples include:

- Hours saved
- Revenue increased
- Cost reduced
- Decisions improved
- Adoption achieved
- Customer satisfaction improved
- Reporting effort reduced

Benefits should be monitored after implementation.

---

# 7.15 Deliverables

Data Product Design should produce enough definition to support validation and a responsible hand-off into delivery.

Typical outputs include:

- Product Vision
- Data Product Canvas
- Target Users / Consumers
- Decisions and Actions Supported
- Success Measures
- Candidate Feature Set
- Information and Data Requirements
- Quality and Freshness Expectations
- Ownership and Governance Model
- Evidence and Open Questions
- Dependencies
- Delivery Handoff

A detailed Product Backlog, release plan and delivery schedule belong to the organisation's delivery process rather than to DISCOVER itself.

# Chapter Summary

Data Product Design transforms business understanding into reusable products that deliver measurable business value.

By beginning with business problems rather than technology, organisations can create trusted data products that support decision making, improve operational efficiency and provide a foundation for analytics and Artificial Intelligence.
