# Applying DISCOVER

> **Purpose:** Turn an ambiguous business request into a clear, proportionate discovery approach.

---

# 1. The Practitioner Starts With Intent

Business requests rarely arrive in DISCOVER language.

They sound like:

- "We need to understand Finance."
- "Our reporting is a mess."
- "We need better data."
- "Can we use AI?"
- "We need a single source of truth."
- "This process takes too long."
- "We need an ontology."
- "We want a data product."

The practitioner should not accept the wording as the problem definition.

Instead ask:

> **What are we actually trying to understand, decide or improve?**

That answer determines how DISCOVER should be applied.

---

# 2. Translate the Request Into a Discovery Objective

Common discovery objectives include:

- Understand a Business Domain
- Understand a Business Capability
- Improve an Existing Process
- Understand a Business Decision
- Resolve Information / Definition Problems
- Rationalise Reporting
- Design a Data Product
- Identify AI Opportunities
- Build a Business Ontology
- Assess Data Quality
- Establish Governance
- Modernise Technology
- Support Executive Strategy

The objective does not need to be perfect at the beginning.

It must be clear enough to guide the first discovery activity.

---

# 3. Identify the Key Uncertainty

The most useful planning question is:

> **What do we not yet understand well enough to make the next decision?**

Examples:

| Situation | Important Uncertainty |
|---|---|
| New Finance domain | What capabilities, decisions and information actually define the domain? |
| Slow month-end | Where does time go and why? |
| Conflicting reports | Are definitions, rules, sources or timing different? |
| AI request | Which user task or decision needs improvement? |
| Data product request | Who is the consumer and what decision/action is being supported? |
| Ontology request | Which business questions require shared semantics? |

Planning should target the uncertainty.

---

# 4. Select DISCOVER Stages

Do not assume every engagement begins at Define and completes all eight stages sequentially.

Choose the stages required by the current objective.

## Define

Use when you need to establish:

- business outcome;
- scope;
- sponsor;
- context;
- constraints.

## Investigate

Use when the business landscape is not sufficiently understood.

## Study

Use when priority areas require deeper investigation.

## Capture

Use throughout once useful evidence emerges.

## Organise

Use when findings need structure, relationships and traceability.

## Validate

Use continuously and at meaningful decision points.

## Evaluate

Use when evidence is sufficient to compare problems, opportunities or interventions.

## Roadmap

Use when validated findings can be translated into outcome-led direction.

---

# 5. Select POPIT-V

Select POPIT-V perspectives based on the uncertainty.

Examples:

### "I need to understand Finance."

Likely focus:

- Organisation
- Process
- Information
- Value

Supporting:

- People

Light initial coverage:

- Technology

### "The month-end process takes too long."

Likely focus:

- Process
- People
- Information

Supporting:

- Technology
- Value

### "Our dashboards disagree."

Likely focus:

- Information
- Process
- Organisation

Supporting:

- Technology
- Value

The selection must always include a rationale.

---

# 6. Select Techniques

Choose the technique that most efficiently creates the evidence you need.

Examples:

| Need | Useful Technique |
|---|---|
| Understand business abilities | Capability Mapping |
| Find stakeholders / owners | Stakeholder Mapping |
| Understand workflow | Process Modelling |
| Understand end-to-end delay | Value Stream Mapping |
| Explore complex domain collaboratively | EventStorming |
| Resolve terminology | Business Glossary |
| Understand semantic boundaries | Domain-Driven Design |
| Model concepts / relationships | Ontology Discovery |
| Explore strategic component evolution | Wardley Mapping |

Do not select techniques because they are fashionable or familiar.

---

# 7. Select the Engagement Format

Not every discovery activity needs a workshop.

Possible formats:

- document review;
- interview;
- observation / shadowing;
- process walkthrough;
- workshop;
- data profiling;
- report review;
- terminology validation;
- evidence review;
- playback / validation session.

Choose the cheapest reliable method of reducing the uncertainty.

---

# 8. Choose the Questions

Only after the objective, uncertainty, lenses and techniques are understood should questions be generated.

For every question ask:

> **Why am I asking this?**

Example:

Question:

> "Which systems do you use?"

Possible purpose:

> Identify where critical information originates or where process constraints are introduced.

Without that purpose, the question may create a system inventory rather than business understanding.

---

# 9. Choose the Artefacts

Artefacts preserve useful knowledge.

They are not measures of discovery progress.

Examples:

- Stakeholder Register;
- Capability Map;
- Decision Catalogue;
- Business Rules Catalogue;
- Business Glossary;
- Evidence Register;
- Pain Point Register;
- Golden Thread Traceability.

Only create an artefact when it improves:

- understanding;
- traceability;
- validation;
- governance;
- reuse.

---

# 10. Define Sufficient Confidence

Before beginning, decide what must be understood to move forward.

Examples:

### Domain Discovery

Sufficient confidence may mean:

- major capabilities understood;
- important decision makers known;
- high-value processes identified;
- critical information and definitions known;
- major pain points evidenced;
- important unknowns visible.

### Process Discovery

Sufficient confidence may mean:

- trigger/outcome agreed;
- major flow validated;
- decisions and rules known;
- exceptions identified;
- delay/rework evidence available.

Do not require perfect completeness.

---

# 11. Define the Next Decision

Discovery should support a next decision.

Examples:

- Which capability should we investigate next?
- Which process should be improved?
- Is a data product justified?
- Is AI worth experimenting with?
- Which ontology concepts need formalisation?
- Which reporting products should be retired?

If the engagement cannot name the next decision, revisit the purpose.

---

# 12. Applying DISCOVER — Worked Example

Request:

> "I need to run workshops with Finance users to understand their domain."

## Objective

Understand a Business Domain.

## Main Uncertainties

- What capabilities define Finance?
- Which decisions matter?
- Which processes create value or control risk?
- Which information and rules support those decisions?
- Where are the important semantic conflicts?
- What pain is material enough to investigate further?

## DISCOVER Stages

Primary:

- Define
- Investigate
- Study

Continuous:

- Capture
- Validate

Later:

- Organise
- Evaluate
- Roadmap

## POPIT-V

Very High:

- Organisation
- Process
- Information
- Value

High:

- People

Medium initially:

- Technology

## Techniques

- Stakeholder Mapping
- Capability Mapping
- Process Walkthrough
- Business Glossary
- Decision Catalogue

Potentially:

- EventStorming
- Ontology Discovery

## Workshop Sequence

1. Finance Business Context and Capabilities
2. Finance Processes and Decisions
3. Finance Information, Rules and Terminology
4. Validation and Prioritisation

## Core Artefacts

- Stakeholder Register
- Capability Map
- Decision Catalogue
- Business Glossary
- Evidence Register
- Pain Point Register
- Golden Thread

## Exit Criteria

Sufficient confidence exists to identify which Finance problems or opportunities deserve deeper discovery.

---

# 13. Applying DISCOVER — Quality Checklist

Before recommending an approach:

- [ ] Business objective understood.
- [ ] Key uncertainty stated.
- [ ] DISCOVER stages selected.
- [ ] POPIT-V perspectives selected and justified.
- [ ] Techniques selected for evidence value.
- [ ] Workshop/interview format proportionate.
- [ ] Questions have a stated purpose.
- [ ] Artefacts are necessary.
- [ ] Validation approach defined.
- [ ] Exit criteria defined.
- [ ] Next decision known.
