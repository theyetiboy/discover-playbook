# Planning Discovery

> **Purpose:** Provide a repeatable planning workflow before interviews, workshops or analysis begin.

---

# 1. Discovery Planning Is a Decision

A discovery plan should answer:

> **What is the minimum set of activities required to reduce the important uncertainty enough to support the next business decision?**

This is different from producing a project plan.

A discovery plan is a learning plan.

---

# 2. Minimum Planning Inputs

Before recommending an approach, establish as much of the following as practical:

## Objective

What is the practitioner trying to achieve?

## Business Area

Which domain, capability, process, decision, data product or problem is involved?

## Current Understanding

What is already known?

## Primary Uncertainty

What is not understood?

## Time / Access

What practical constraints exist?

## Stakeholders

Who can provide evidence or validate findings?

## Existing Evidence

Which documents, reports, models, data or prior discovery already exist?

---

# 3. Clarify Only What Matters

Do not ask twenty planning questions before helping.

Ask a clarifying question only if the answer would materially alter:

- the selected DISCOVER stages;
- POPIT-V emphasis;
- technique choice;
- workshop sequence;
- stakeholder group;
- outputs.

For example, these questions may materially alter the plan:

- Is this a brand-new domain or an existing area being improved?
- Are you trying to understand the business, build a product, or solve a known problem?
- Do you have one hour or several sessions?
- Are operational users available?

---

# 4. Planning Decision 1 — Objective

Classify the objective.

Examples:

| Objective | Primary Planning Pattern |
|---|---|
| Learn unfamiliar business area | Understand a Business Domain |
| Diagnose delay / friction | Improve an Existing Process |
| Resolve disagreement in metrics | Resolve Information / Definitions |
| Create reusable data capability | Design a Data Product |
| Explore LLM / ML use | Identify AI Opportunity |
| Create shared semantic model | Build an Ontology |
| Reduce duplicated dashboards | Rationalise Reporting |

If multiple objectives exist, identify the **primary objective** and sequence the others.

---

# 5. Planning Decision 2 — Discovery Depth

Choose a proportionate depth.

## Orientation

Purpose:

Gain enough context to know where deeper discovery is needed.

Typical duration:

- one or two interviews;
- one 60–90 minute workshop.

## Focused Discovery

Purpose:

Understand a specific capability, process, decision or information problem.

Typical duration:

- several interviews/workshops;
- evidence review;
- validation.

## Domain Discovery

Purpose:

Understand a broad business domain and establish reusable artefacts.

Typical duration:

- multiple sessions;
- broader stakeholder coverage;
- capability/process/semantic modelling.

## Deep Diagnostic

Purpose:

Establish root cause for a high-value or high-risk problem.

Typical duration:

- targeted process/data/rule investigation;
- measurement;
- evidence triangulation.

Do not use a Domain Discovery when an Orientation or Focused Discovery is enough.

---

# 6. Planning Decision 3 — Current Confidence

Assess confidence in:

- outcome;
- scope;
- ownership;
- process;
- decisions;
- information;
- rules;
- technology;
- value.

Use:

- Low
- Medium
- High

Do not average into one meaningless score.

Low confidence in a high-impact area should influence the next discovery activity.

---

# 7. Planning Decision 4 — Stakeholder Coverage

Identify who can answer or validate the key uncertainty.

Potential stakeholder types:

- sponsor;
- capability owner;
- operational user;
- decision maker;
- subject-matter expert;
- data owner;
- system owner;
- control / risk;
- customer / partner;
- downstream consumer.

The best stakeholder is not necessarily the most senior stakeholder.

---

# 8. Planning Decision 5 — Evidence Sources

Before scheduling another workshop, check whether the answer already exists in:

- policy;
- strategy;
- process documents;
- contracts;
- business glossary;
- data catalogue;
- reports;
- KPI definitions;
- architecture diagrams;
- audit findings;
- support tickets;
- operational data.

A document review may reduce uncertainty more cheaply than a workshop.

# 10. Workshop Sequencing

Do not make every topic a separate workshop.

Sequence workshops where one creates knowledge needed by the next.

Example:

```text
Business Context / Capability
        ↓
Process / Decisions
        ↓
Information / Rules
        ↓
Technology / Data
        ↓
Validation / Opportunity
```

This sequence is a pattern, not a mandatory lifecycle.

---

# 11. Parallel Discovery

Some activities can happen in parallel.

Example:

```text
Stakeholder Interviews ─────┐
                            ├──> Shared Domain Model
Document Review ────────────┤
                            │
Data / Report Review ───────┘
```

Use parallel work when:

- evidence sources are independent;
- it shortens elapsed discovery time;
- outputs can be reconciled later.

---

# 12. Planning for Validation

Every plan should identify:

- what needs validation;
- who can validate it;
- what source may confirm it;
- when validation should occur.

Do not leave all validation until the end.

---

# 13. Planning for Unknowns

Unknowns are expected.

Use:

- `ASM-*` for assumptions;
- `OQ-*` for open questions;
- `UNKNOWN` for missing Golden Thread fields.

The goal is not to eliminate uncertainty immediately.

The goal is to make uncertainty manageable.

---

# 14. Planning Anti-Patterns

## The Template Plan

"We need to complete the stakeholder map, capability map, process map and glossary."

Problem:

Outputs are driving discovery.

## The Workshop Plan

"We have four workshops available, so what should they be?"

Problem:

Available meetings are defining the problem.

## The Technology Plan

"We're replacing the ERP, so let's document the current ERP."

Problem:

The current technology is defining the business model.

## The Everything Plan

"Let's cover all POPIT-V lenses in one session."

Problem:

Breadth replaces learning.

---

# 15. Planning Quality Checklist

- [ ] Objective explicit.
- [ ] Next decision known.
- [ ] Key uncertainties prioritised.
- [ ] Depth proportionate.
- [ ] Stakeholder coverage sufficient.
- [ ] Existing evidence considered.
- [ ] DISCOVER stages selected.
- [ ] POPIT-V selected.
- [ ] Techniques justified.
- [ ] Activities sequenced.
- [ ] Artefacts minimal and useful.
- [ ] Validation included.
- [ ] Exit criteria defined.
