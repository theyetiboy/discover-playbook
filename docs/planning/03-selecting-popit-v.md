# Selecting POPIT-V Perspectives

> **Purpose:** Help practitioners choose which POPIT-V perspectives deserve attention for the discovery objective in front of them.

---

# 1. POPIT-V Is a Lens, Not a Checklist

POPIT-V protects discovery from blind spots.

It should not force every workshop to investigate:

- People;
- Organisation;
- Process;
- Information;
- Technology;
- Value

with equal depth.

Across the engagement, consider all relevant perspectives.

Within an individual activity, prioritise only the lenses that will materially improve understanding.

---

# 2. Selection Question

For each perspective ask:

> **Will deeper understanding of this perspective materially reduce an important uncertainty?**

If yes, include it.

If not, keep it light or exclude it from that activity.

---

# 3. Priority Scale

Use a simple scale:

- **Very High** — central to the objective.
- **High** — important supporting understanding.
- **Medium** — useful but not a primary focus.
- **Low** — only explore if evidence indicates relevance.
- **Not Required Yet** — deliberately defer.

Avoid numerical precision unless a genuine scoring model exists.

---

# 4. People

Select People when the uncertainty relates to:

- roles;
- responsibilities;
- decision rights;
- knowledge;
- capacity;
- adoption;
- collaboration;
- workarounds;
- skills.

Typical question behind the lens:

> **Who performs, decides, owns or experiences this?**

People often deserves high attention when:

- process relies on tacit knowledge;
- manual work is material;
- ownership is unclear;
- adoption matters;
- AI or automation is being considered.

---

# 5. Organisation

Select Organisation when the uncertainty relates to:

- business capabilities;
- strategic alignment;
- governance;
- accountability;
- operating model;
- organisational boundaries;
- global/local ownership;
- external partners.

Typical question:

> **Why does this business area exist and how is responsibility organised?**

Organisation is often very high priority for unfamiliar-domain discovery.

---

# 6. Process

Select Process when the uncertainty relates to:

- flow;
- delay;
- hand-offs;
- exceptions;
- rework;
- approvals;
- controls;
- operational execution.

Typical question:

> **How does work actually move from trigger to outcome?**

Process is often very high priority when the stated problem involves:

- time;
- manual effort;
- failure;
- reconciliation;
- bottlenecks.

---

# 7. Information

Select Information when the uncertainty relates to:

- business concepts;
- definitions;
- KPIs;
- reports;
- business rules;
- quality;
- lineage;
- provenance;
- semantics;
- data needed for decisions.

Typical question:

> **What does the business need to know, and what does that information mean?**

Information is usually central to data, analytics, reporting, ontology and AI discovery.

---

# 8. Technology

Select Technology when the uncertainty relates to:

- applications;
- integrations;
- platforms;
- spreadsheets;
- reliability;
- security;
- technical constraints;
- automation;
- AI components.

Typical question:

> **How does technology enable or constrain the business?**

Technology should not automatically become high priority simply because the initiative is sponsored by Technology.

---

# 9. Value

Select Value when the uncertainty relates to:

- outcome;
- benefit;
- commercial impact;
- cost;
- risk;
- customer/user value;
- productivity;
- strategic importance;
- cost of inaction.

Typical question:

> **Why does this matter, and how will we know it improved?**

Value should normally remain visible throughout the engagement, even when it is not the main workshop topic.

---

# 10. Example Selection — Understand Finance Domain

Objective:

Understand Finance sufficiently to identify priority data/analytics opportunities.

Recommended emphasis:

| Perspective | Priority | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| People | High | Decision ownership and specialist knowledge matter. |
| Organisation | Very High | Need capabilities, governance and accountability. |
| Process | Very High | Need to understand forecasting, close, recognition etc. |
| Information | Very High | Financial definitions, rules and KPIs are central. |
| Technology | Medium | Need landscape context but should not start with systems. |
| Value | Very High | Finance value includes control, planning, insight and risk. |

| Organisation | Medium | Ownership/governance may affect hand-offs. |
| Process | Very High | Main uncertainty is flow and delay. |
| Information | High | Missing/late data may drive rework. |

# 12. Example Selection — AI Opportunity

| Perspective | Priority | Reason |
|---|---|---|

| Organisation | High | Ownership/governance matter. |

# 14. Selecting Perspectives for a Workshop

For every selected perspective define:

## Why

Why is it relevant?

## What We Need to Learn

Which uncertainties should be reduced?

## Questions

Which questions will produce the evidence?

## Evidence

What evidence should emerge?

## Artefacts

Where should the learning be captured?

Example:

### Information

**Why**

Finance decisions depend on consistent definitions and rules.

**Need to learn**

- important concepts;
- conflicting definitions;
- rules;
- KPI logic.

**Questions**

- What does recognised revenue mean?
- Does that meaning differ by team?
- Which rule determines the period?
- Who owns the definition?

**Evidence**

- definitions;
- rules;
- examples;
- ownership.

**Artefacts**

- Business Glossary;
- Business Rules Catalogue;
- Evidence Register.

---

# 15. Cross-Lens Reasoning

The strongest discoveries often exist between lenses.

Examples:

### People + Process

A manual bottleneck may exist because only one specialist can approve an exception.

### Organisation + Information

Conflicting metrics may exist because no domain owns the definition.

### Process + Information

Rework may result from information arriving after the decision point.

### Information + Technology

A system may contain technically accurate data that has ambiguous business meaning.

### Technology + Value

A reliable platform may still produce little value if it supports no important decision.

The AI assistant should actively look for these connections.

---

# 16. Avoiding POPIT-V Bias

Do not assume:

- People = training issue;
- Organisation = org chart;
- Process = process map;
- Information = database;
- Technology = system inventory;
- Value = ROI.

Each lens is broader than its most obvious artefact.

---

# 17. POPIT-V Selection Checklist

- [ ] Discovery objective explicit.
- [ ] Key uncertainty known.
- [ ] Each high-priority lens has a rationale.
- [ ] Low-priority lenses deliberately deferred.
- [ ] Value remains visible.
- [ ] Technology is not automatically dominant.
- [ ] Cross-lens relationships considered.
- [ ] Questions linked to the selected lens purpose.
- [ ] Artefacts chosen only where needed.
