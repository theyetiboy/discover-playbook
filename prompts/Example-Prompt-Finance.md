# Finance Discovery Focus

Use these prompts when exploring Finance capabilities such as:

- FP&A;
- planning;
- forecasting;
- P&L;
- revenue recognition;
- revenue attribution;
- actuals versus forecast;
- management reporting;
- statutory/regulatory reporting;
- month-end;
- reconciliations;
- commercial finance.

These are prompts for discovery, not assertions about how the Finance function operates.

---

# 1. Finance Discovery Kick-off

> I am beginning discovery with Finance.
>
> Known concerns are **[insert known concerns]**.
>
> Stakeholders include **[roles]**.
>
> Using DISCOVER, help me understand which Finance capabilities, processes, decisions, information and value measures I should investigate first.
>
> Do not assume the reported concerns are root causes.

---

# 2. Revenue Recognition Workshop

> Design a 90-minute discovery workshop focused on revenue recognition.
>
> I want to understand:
> - the business events that trigger recognition;
> - who makes or approves decisions;
> - rules and exceptions;
> - information required;
> - hand-offs;
> - reconciliation;
> - reporting consequences;
> - risk and value.
>
> Keep the session solution-neutral.
>
> Use Process, Information, People, Organisation and Value as the primary POPIT-V lenses unless the evidence suggests otherwise.

---

# 3. Forecasting Discovery

> Help me investigate Finance forecasting.
>
> Structure the discovery around:
> - decisions being made;
> - planning horizon;
> - forecast inputs;
> - assumptions;
> - ownership;
> - adjustments/overrides;
> - actuals;
> - variance analysis;
> - confidence;
> - timing;
> - information quality;
> - business actions resulting from forecasts.
>
> Identify what I need to ask before discussing forecasting technology or AI.

---

# 4. Month-End Process

> Build a workshop to understand month-end close.
>
> I want to expose:
> - critical activities;
> - dependencies;
> - manual hand-offs;
> - spreadsheets;
> - reconciliations;
> - adjustments;
> - approval points;
> - exceptions;
> - control requirements;
> - bottlenecks;
> - measures of success.
>
> Include prompts that distinguish symptoms from root causes.

---

# 5. Finance Decision Catalogue

> From the supplied Finance sources and notes, build a Finance Decision Catalogue.
>
> For each decision capture:
> - decision;
> - decision maker;
> - timing/trigger;
> - required information;
> - financial/business rules;
> - action produced;
> - consequence of poor or late information;
> - evidence;
> - unknowns.

---

# 6. Finance Information / Concept Model

> Identify the Finance business concepts in the sources.
>
> Pay particular attention to terms such as:
> - revenue;
> - recognised revenue;
> - forecast;
> - actual;
> - contract;
> - licence;
> - territory;
> - currency;
> - cost;
> - margin;
> - royalty;
> - accrual;
> - adjustment;
> - period.
>
> Only include concepts actually supported by the evidence.
>
> Highlight ambiguous definitions and terms that appear to mean different things to different teams.

---

# 7. Finance Reporting Challenge

> Finance has asked for **[report/dashboard]**.
>
> Before treating this as a requirement, determine:
> - who uses it;
> - what decision it supports;
> - what action follows;
> - frequency;
> - required granularity;
> - definitions;
> - rules;
> - reconciliation requirements;
> - current problem;
> - value of improving it.
>
> Produce the questions I should take back to Finance.

---

# 8. Finance Data Product Candidates

> Based on validated Finance discovery evidence, identify candidate data products.
>
> Do not simply rename existing reports as data products.
>
> For each candidate show:
> - consumer;
> - decision/action supported;
> - core business concepts;
> - information/data required;
> - quality/freshness expectations;
> - ownership;
> - governance;
> - current pain point;
> - measurable business value;
> - unresolved questions.

---

# 9. Finance AI Opportunity

> Explore whether AI could create value in the Finance area currently under discovery.
>
> Consider candidate uses such as forecasting support, anomaly detection, explanation, reconciliation assistance or conversational access only if the evidence suggests a relevant problem.
>
> For each candidate distinguish:
> - problem;
> - user;
> - data/information;
> - decision;
> - human oversight;
> - risk;
> - value;
> - simpler non-AI alternative.
